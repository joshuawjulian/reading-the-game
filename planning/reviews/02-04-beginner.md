# 02-04 Spread Formations: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher, never played, has read 01-01 to 02-03 (and so already has: box,
nickel/dime, Mike/Will/Sam, man vs zone in passing, play-action, screen, the flat, press/jam, pulling,
X/Y/Z/H, splits and the split ladder, passing vs TE strength, covered receivers).
Read from PDF `pdfs/02-04-spread-and-modern-formations.pdf` (22 pp., built 15:18, newer than the qmd).
Prose is about **9,200 words against a 4,500 target**. It reads well; the issues are in a few diagrams
that don't match what their captions say, one big conceptual gap (tight vs condensed), and too much repetition.

## Top issues (fix first)

1. **Fig 4 (fig-trips-choice) shows something different from its caption.** Caption: "the right
   safety (SS) comes down over the third receiver, so the trips side gets an extra defender". In the
   picture W moves back *into the box* (w 6.6 to 2.8) as SS comes down, so the trips side still has three
   underneath defenders. The on-field labels go from "3 receivers vs 3 + safety" (left) to
   "3 receivers vs 3 defenders" (right), so the count I can see goes *down*. I can't find the "extra
   defender". Either leave W outside (C, $, W, SS = 4 vs 3) or say in the caption that the extra
   man went to the box and the deep help moved to the middle.
2. **Tight vs condensed: the chapter never explains how the old picture differs from the new one.** Fig 1's
   "Tight" panel (X at -9, H on a wing, Z at 10.5, 7 in the box) is shown as what the spread replaced and
   improved on. The crack-toss / Drill 3 "condensed" formation (X -8.6, H -5.6, Z 8.6) is nearly the same
   picture, and it's presented as a revolution. Section "What condensed gives up" even admits that the
   box gets more crowded. So what is different: the shotgun? Wide receivers instead of a second back? The same
   picture for run and pass? Motion? This chapter needs one paragraph and probably a side-by-side.
   Related: the chapter never says that **count (2x2/3x1) and width (spread/condensed) are two separate
   dials**. Drill 3 calls it "a condensed 2x2", and "condensed alignments … outnumbering spread ones in
   2023" leaves me unsure whether condensed trips counts as a spread formation.
3. **Fig 9 (bunch traffic strip) doesn't show traffic.** (a) Frame 4 caption: "Y catches it". The
   ball is in flight about 3 yd *behind* the LOS and about 10 yd from Y (I checked at 200 dpi). (b) The SS
   "has to go around the traffic", but his trail runs deeper and wider, away from everyone, and never
   meets a crossing receiver. (c) Frame 2: Y and Z markers overlap. (d) Frame 4: H sits on the top edge
   of the window. (e) The trails cross *behind* the LOS, not "right in front of the defenders". A
   reader can't see a defender being slowed. Have the SS's path actually bend around Z/H, put the
   ball at Y on the last frame, and widen the window.
4. **Drill 3 answer contradicts the drill picture and the crack-toss lesson.** Answer: "X and Z are already
   *inside* the nickel and the linebackers, so they can block down on them". In the figure X (-8.6) is
   *outside* the nickel (-6.4) and the W (-2.6). "Inside the cornerback" was the point taught at Fig 12.
   The drill also puts the corners *inside* the receivers, which isn't how the crack toss started (there the
   corner was outside X), so the run the reader is told to expect no longer matches the run they were shown.
   The last paragraph adds to the confusion: "the corners are now outside the receivers, where a crack
   block can't reach them, which leaves the crack-and-replace version". The corner was *never* the crack
   target. In crack-and-replace the guard takes him.
5. **Drill 2 answer misreads its own figure.** "with the nearest safety twelve or thirteen yards deep".
   In the figure SS is about 7.5 yd deep (level with the corners). Also D (dime) sits *on* the shaded box
   edge, so a careful reader may count 6 in the box. Move D a yard wider or drop him back.
6. **Fig 11 (two-way go) left panel says two different things.** The caption says the receiver's "only real option is to fight
   back inside", and the solid arrow goes inside, straight at the defender who has inside leverage. The text says
   the defender "takes away the inside and lets the sideline take away the rest". So what *can* the
   receiver do? Also the "sideline = help" label sits on the grey out-arrow's head, and the "in" label on the
   right panel is struck through by a hash tick.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

- **"run fits"** in the Condensed "Why it exists" callout ("in the run fits near the ball"). It's not in any prior chapter and not glossed.
- **"point of attack"** in the Spread "Why it exists" callout. It appeared once in 02-03. Gloss it in 3 words.
- **"apex and overhang defender"** gets its name, but the chapter never says what the names mean or why they're called that.
  Half a sentence would do: "standing at the apex, midway between the tackle and the slot".
- **"switch" call** (Chiefs film room: "it's a zone or a 'switch' call"). The body called it *swap*. Pick one word.
- **"hitch"** isn't named in prose, but Fig 2 draws it. **"out", "fade", "dig"** (Drill 3 answer) are route names
  the reader hasn't been taught. "Corner" is glossed only inside the Fig 10 caption.
- **"jet sweeps", "toss sweeps", "crack-and-replace"** appear in a list after Fig 12. "Toss" was glossed and the other two weren't.
- **"try"** (Fig 15 caption: "on a try after a touchdown"). Prior chapters say extra point/two-point conversion.
- **"receiver strength (or passing strength)"** is italicised as if new, but 02-02 already taught passing strength
  (and Rip/Liz). Link back instead of re-introducing it.
- **Label collisions across figures:** "C" is both the center (blue) and a cornerback (red) in Figs 1-12 and 16-18.
  Fig 11 shows both side by side. In Fig 15, **H = holder** (H has meant the slot receiver all chapter) and **S =
  safety** (in 02-01, S = Sam linebacker). In Fig 13 (wildcat), **S = Sam**, and that isn't defined in the caption.
  In Fig 5, **D** first appears and isn't defined until Drill 2. Fig 15's **L** (linebacker) is undefined too.
- **"a running back with a one-on-one in the hole"** (after Fig 2). With 5 blockers on 5 box defenders, who is the back
  one-on-one *with*? The safeties are 12 yards deep. Say "the back's first unblocked man is a safety 12 yards away".

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

- **How to count a side** is an objective ("Count receivers against defenders on each side"), but the procedure is
  never stated. Which defenders count: only those outside the box? Within some depth? Is a deep safety
  "half a defender"? Fig 4 writes "3 + safety", while Drill 1's answer counts "defenders outside the box over them" and treats
  the safety separately. Give a 3-line rule and use it the same way in Fig 4, the Watch-for-it list and Drill 1.
- **Numbering bunch and stack is never shown.** The variant plates use `numbers=False`, which is exactly where
  numbering gets hard. Who is #1 in a stack, where both men share the same width? In a bunch, is the point man #2?
  A reader will hit this on Sunday.
- **Rubs and the rules.** "The receivers never touched the defenders … Running into a defender … more than a yard
  past the line of scrimmage, is OPI." The next questions are obvious: so contact *within* a yard is legal? Is
  that why bunch rubs happen right at the line, as Fig 9's trails show? How do officials tell a "natural pick" from
  a pick? One sentence on the 1-yard window would turn the footnote into the reason bunch is designed the way it is.
- **Crack block legality.** A block from the side on someone who isn't looking sounds like a penalty. Say that it's legal
  above the waist and illegal below it (a "crackback"), or the reader assumes it's a cheap shot.
- **02-02 and 02-04 disagree about the two-way go.** 02-02's split ladder says "On the numbers … leaves room to
  break either way". 02-04 says the standard wide receiver (the plates put X/Z at 15.5 yd, about on the numbers) has
  only one way, and that condensing is what gives outside receivers a two-way go. Reconcile them: say which split loses
  the two-way go (outside the numbers? into the boundary?).
- **Fig 10 doesn't show why the wide split is worse.** Both panels draw the same corner route, and in the wide panel it
  reaches the sideline with room to spare. The extra 8 yards in the condensed panel go unused. Show where the
  ball has to land, or the corner's path, so the cost is visible.
- **Wildcat arithmetic.** "The quarterback, now split out wide, still needs a defender on him". Why, if everyone
  knows he's not the runner? Because he can still catch a pass, or take a throwback? Say so. "Every blocker is
  accounted for" (Fig 13 caption) is cryptic.
- **Unbalanced line payoff isn't drawn.** Fig 14 caption: "the offense has an extra blocker on that side". The
  figure has no count. Add "4 blockers vs 3 defenders" labels on the strong side of the Tackle-over panel.
- **Fig 7 caption**: "the down and distance on which … the defense is most likely to blitz" is asserted without
  support. Either cite it or soften it.
- **Empty plates vs hook**: the hook has the RB settle "a few yards outside the tight end", but the Empty plate puts R
  *inside* Y (R #3, Y #2). It's minor, but a careful reader compares them.

## 3. Diagrams: decoding problems and captions that don't say what to notice

- Fig 4: see Top issue 1.
- Fig 5 (empty pressure): the M and T label boxes overlap at the line, the W box sits under a rush arrow, and the ringed E's
  label overlaps his ring. The "five linemen block five rushers" pairing is too small to see. Rush arrows that stop
  at blockers read as no movement at all. D is undefined (see above).
- Fig 9: see Top issue 3.
- Fig 10: see Leaps.
- Fig 11: see Top issue 6.
- Fig 12 (crack toss): this one mostly works. Frame 2: the $ box is hidden under X's ring. Frames 3-4: the E box sits
  on top of the LT dot. The caption calls the crack target the nickel, but the body defines a crack block as
  on "a linebacker or safety". Add "or nickel" to the definition.
- Fig 13 (wildcat): the "keep" label overlaps a hash tick, and the keep arrow (2 yards, straight into M) gives no
  sense of a play. No blocking is drawn, so the claimed arithmetic edge is invisible. Defense labels aren't
  defined in the caption.
- Fig 14 (unbalanced): the ringed LT's ring overlaps Y's gold ring and the E box. The caption's payoff isn't drawn.
- Fig 15 (swinging gate): label conflicts (H, S, L). Otherwise it's clear.
- Drill 2 figure: D is on the box edge. Drill 3 figure: the C and Q labels at the center overlap, the LCB "C" and "$"
  boxes touch, and at full-field width the dots are tiny. The cornerbacks are barely inside X/Z (about 1 yd), so "lined
  up inside the receivers" is hard to see. Exaggerate it.
- Drill 1 figure (and 2, 3): the thick gold line across the field is the line to gain, which no drill caption says.
  It's obvious once you know, but it's a new reader's first question.

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? (drills attempted before the answers)

- **Name the formations and number the receivers**: yes for 2x2, 3x1, trey, empty, nub and quads. **No for bunch and stack**,
  which were never numbered (see Leaps).
- **Explain why spreading forces the defense to declare and opens space**: yes. Fig 1 and Fig 2 are excellent.
- **Count receivers vs defenders on each side**: shaky. Without a stated procedure, Fig 4 left me confused (the count went
  down when the caption said it went up).
- **Explain condensed splits**: I can list the four reasons, but I can't tell a condensed formation from Fig 1's
  "tight" one, which the chapter says is worse.
- **Recognize wildcat, unbalanced, swinging gate**: yes.
- **Read the empty data**: yes. Both charts are clear.

**Drill 1 (count the sides)**: My answer: 3x1 trips right; Z #1, H #2, Y #3, X #1 on the left. On the right, C and $ are
outside the box and W is inside, so it's 3 v 2, with a quick throw to Y. **Matched the answer.** Nice drill. (I had to judge
by eye that W is "in the box" because no box is drawn here. Shade it as in Drill 2.)

**Drill 2 (empty, running QB)**: My answer: probably a pass (3rd-and-4, empty, 94%), but 5 in the box vs 5 OL, so a QB draw.
**Matched**, but only because the body and the Bills box had already told me the answer twice. I hesitated over whether D
was in the box, since he sits on its edge. The answer's "nearest safety twelve or thirteen yards deep" didn't match the
picture (SS about 7.5).

**Drill 3 (everybody's tight)**: My answer: open grass outside X and Z; crack-toss run or outside-breaking route (corner);
corners should widen. I also expected **play-action**, since it's first down, under center, and condensed (reason #4,
"everything looks the same"). The answer doesn't mention play-action, which seems like the chapter's own headline idea for
this look. **Partly matched.** The run explanation ("X and Z are already inside the nickel") contradicted what I saw and
undid my understanding of the crack (Top issue 4).

## 6. Knowledge gaps (questions I still have that this chapter should answer)

- What's the difference between Fig 1's "tight" formation and a condensed one? (Top issue 2.)
- Which is more common, 2x2 or 3x1? The text says trips is "one of the most common pictures" but gives no number. The
  BDB 2022 `receiverAlignment` split is public in many write-ups, and even a cited range would help.
- How wide is a "normal" formation? The Rams film room says "60% … less than 20 yards wide". Give the comparison width
  (Fig 1's spread is about 31 yd end to end) so the 20 means something.
- Why did empty fall to a decade low (6.9%) in 2025? Is it related to the 11-personnel low and 12-personnel high from 02-01?
  The chapter says "no big trend", but its own chart's last point invites the question.
- Is the wildcat still used? NGS had a WILDCAT label through 2022, so a usage line would show the 2008 spike and fade
  the text describes.
- Which way does the defense set its strength against trips nub? 02-02 said "front to the TE, coverage to passing
  strength". Say that this is exactly the nub's purpose, and connect the two chapters.
- How do I tell condensed from a normal "reduced" split on a broadcast angle? The Rams "What to notice" gives the numbers
  as a landmark. Repeat that in the Watch-for-it checklist as a concrete measure (e.g., "outside receiver inside the
  numbers = condensed").
