# Beginner-reader review: 03-04 Option Football

Reviewer stance: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read only 01-01 through 03-03 in curriculum order.
I read the source start to finish and the 21-page PDF (rasterized at 60, 130 and 220 dpi to check the diagrams).

**Verdict:** a strong chapter. The opening hook, the rule "if he can tackle the back, keep; if he can't, give", the
scrape-exchange section and the data section's selection, situation and survivorship caveats all land very well.
Most of the problems are in the diagrams, and four of them actively mislead: Fig 6/13, Fig 8, Fig 10 left and Fig 3/9.
There is also one arithmetic error ("eleventh blocker"), and a few "why" gaps a careful reader will trip on.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **Arithmetic error (p.12, "Why it exists").** The text says "a quarterback carrying the ball turns the running back into an
   **eleventh blocker**". The chapter's own count on p.1 is "Nine offensive players block or run routes, the ball carrier carries". A QB run
   makes the back the **tenth** blocker. An analytically minded reader will catch this and start doubting the other numbers.
2. **Bash beats the scrape exchange: asserted, not shown (p.5–6, and again in the Drill 1 answer).** The text says "the crashing end
   has crashed onto the quarterback's fake and the scraping linebacker is chasing a back who started outside him." When I pictured it
   myself, the scraping Will runs *outside*, which is exactly where the bash back is going, so he seems to meet the back. The real
   argument is probably about the head start, or that the defense assigns by player rather than by lane, and it needs a sentence of
   mechanism or a small two-panel picture. Right now the chapter's most important counter-punch rests on trust.
3. **Slow-play: what does the quarterback do? (p.12)** "A slow-playing end wants the quarterback to give… or to hesitate." Under the
   chapter's rule, a squared-up end who is "inside enough to threaten the back" *can* tackle the back, so the rule says keep. Yet the
   text says the slow-player wants a give. I could not work out the right answer from the rule. Add one line: the quarterback's
   answer to a slow-play (give and trust the back? a keep with an arc block?), and why the rule as stated doesn't settle it.
4. **A forward pitch: so what? (p.7)** "a toss that goes forward is legally a forward pass, and the offense gets only one of
   those." Why is that bad on the play itself? A dropped *forward* toss would be an incomplete pass, which sounds *safer* than a
   fumble. The reader needs the actual risk spelled out: an illegal forward pass beyond the line, or the play being blown dead, or
   whatever it really is. Otherwise the point reads backwards.
5. **Veer vs triple option (p.8).** The veer is "an option family" and "the base of the triple option", and then "A back trailing
   the quarterback adds a third option. … That three-way play is the triple option." So is "the veer" a two-way play, or another
   name for the triple? Say plainly: veer = the blocking and read rule (release the tackle, read the first man outside him), and the
   triple = veer plus a pitch man.
6. **Inverted veer vs bash.** Both put the back outside and the quarterback inside. The chapter never sets them side by side. The
   difference is which end is read, and power blocking versus zone blocking. A one-line contrast would stop the reader merging them,
   and the Ravens box's "counter bash" makes the need sharper.
7. **"It needs under-center snaps" (p.9).** Why? The zone read and the speed option are run from the shotgun in this same chapter.
   One sentence on the dive's timing and the ride from under center would fill the gap, as would whether a shotgun triple exists
   (it does, in college and in RPO form).
8. **The Watch-for-it pre-snap tell is too broad (p.16).** "Shotgun or pistol with a back beside or behind the quarterback is the
   read look." That describes most NFL snaps. Tell the reader roughly how often that look is actually a read, so they don't predict
   option on every gun handoff, and what separates a real read from a fake one (for example, the quarterback's eyes on the end
   rather than the back, or the ride length).
9. **Data, p.14–15:** the 2006 bar (about 0.6, higher than 2007–2011) and the 2025 drop to about 0.77 go unexplained. The peak year
   is stated, but not why the series falls back. The right panel's wild early swings (2007 at about −0.04, 2008 at about +0.27)
   need a sample-size note, such as "a few hundred plays a season before 2012".

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **Fig 6 (triple option) and Fig 13 (Drill 2): the arc line runs straight through the Sam.** The play-side A-back's path from A
  to the safety passes *through the S square and its ring*, so it reads as "A-back blocks the Sam". That is the exact opposite of
  the figure's point, which is that the Sam is unblocked, the pitch key. Route the arc visibly *outside* the S, or start it wider.
  Also, the pitch man is still drawn at his pre-motion spot while the caption says "has gone in motion… at the snap".
- **Fig 8 (midline): the play-side A-back's lead path passes through the play-side 5's square** on its way to the Sam, so it looks
  as if the A-back blocks the 5 and the RT blocks the Sam. The caption says the reverse. The backside A-back's short arrow ("fakes
  the pitch man's path" in code) isn't mentioned in the caption.
- **Fig 10 left (QB counter): the play side is a knot.** Both pullers' lines end hidden under RT and the 5. There is no visible
  kick-out tee on the end, and the QB's thick arrow passes **through the "5" box**, the man being kicked out. I could not see where
  the hole is. The "step left" in the label isn't drawn either: the QB's path goes straight right.
- **Fig 3 (scrape exchange): the RB is drawn with the thick, solid "ball carrier" arrow** even though the QB keeps. The legend says
  "thick dark arrows are ball carriers", so the picture says the back has the ball. Make it a fake path (thin or dashed).
- **Fig 9 (QB power): the green ring is on R.** Two pages earlier, "the green ring follows the ball", so I first read this as the
  back carrying. Use a different highlight colour for "now a blocker". The Q and R paths are also two close parallel diagonals; I
  needed the caption to tell which one was the run.
- **Fig 4 (inverted veer):** two green dashed lines leave the QB toward the RT, nearly on top of each other: the eyes line (thin)
  and the keep branch (thick). I couldn't separate them. The **give branch heads straight at the unblocked NB**, yet the caption
  says "the back is outside him with the edge open". Who handles the nickel? Either block him or explain why he's out of the play.
- **Fig 7 (triple, frame strip), frame 2:** the B-back is completely hidden under the 5, and the LT and LG labels are hidden under
  the 5 and 3 squares. Frame 2 is the moment the caption describes ("squeezes down on the B-back"), and the B-back can't be seen. In
  the same strip, **"W" means both the Will (orange) and the wide receivers (blue)**, which is confusing in tiny frames.
- **Fig 5 (speed option):** the "keep if the end widens to the back" label sits over the W and M, about 5 yards from the green
  arrow it names. The QB's path ends *inside* the end man, but the caption says he "attacks the end man's outside shoulder".
- **Fig 1 (EMOLOS), right panel:** the label "the 5 is now inside him" overlaps the M square, and it has no leader line to the 5
  it describes.
- **Fig 11 (chart):** the dark versus light bars mean something (2012–13 and 2018+?) but the caption never says what. The "2012
  wave" leader points at the 2013 bar.
- **Fig 2 (zone read strip) works well**: two rows, same snap, different end, and the notes under each frame tell the story. This is
  the model for the others. The only flaw: in frame 2, top row, R is hidden behind Q.
- **"Go deeper" box (p.20):** "The cell below shows the shape of that code". In the PDF there is no cell, because code is hidden
  in print. Add "(on the website)" or show a one-line description.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Hat math is re-explained four times**: on p.1 (option principle), p.4 ("Count the hats…"), p.12 ("Out-number it") and in the
  takeaways. It is also in the "You'll need" box. The p.4 paragraph is the useful one; trim the opening section's repeat, since 03-01
  already drew this exact picture (03-01's fig shows the read of the backside end).
- **Bash and arc are described three times**: in 03-02, in "The offense's replies", and again almost word for word in the Drill 1
  answer. The answer could just point back.
- **"The quarterback takes hits" is said about seven times**: in the costs callout, the zone read, the speed option, the triple
  costs, the QB-runs "Why it exists", "Hit the quarterback" and the takeaways. Each is true, but by the fifth time it's padding.
  And there is no number anywhere. One data point (QB injury or hit rates on designed runs, if they exist) would replace three of
  these sentences.
- Kaepernick's 181 yards is told twice (p.13 and the film room). Fine, but the first mention could be cut to a pointer.

## 5. Can I do each "You'll be able to…"? Drill attempts (made before opening the answers)

**Drill 1, my answer:** "The Will. It's a scrape exchange. Call bash or arc, or read or throw off the Will." This was correct. But
the drill is **too easy**: the setup says "both times the Will linebacker… met him", which answers "who probably makes the
tackle" for me. To make it a real test, show the Will's alignment and the end's 4-of-4 crashes, say the keeps were stopped for short
gains, and ask *who* and *why*.

**Drill 2, my answer:** "Give to the B-back on the dive. The dive key stayed outside, so the pitch key never matters." Correct, and
a good red herring. The diagram problem above (the arc through the Sam) made me briefly wonder whether the Sam was blocked.

**Drill 3, my answer:** "6 blockers v 7 in the box on a handoff. QB power makes it 7 v 7 (QB draw is plausible against man). The
defense brings the safety down." The first two parts were correct. On the third, I got the eighth man but could not have produced
"spy", because it isn't taught before the answer.

Objectives:

1. Find the read key and predict give or keep: **yes** for an end. There was no practice on reading the *play-side* end (inverted
   veer) or a 3-technique (midline), and no drill shows the read happening.
2. Hat math: **yes**, though the "eleventh blocker" line muddied it.
3. Tell zone read, inverted veer, speed option, triple and midline apart: **mostly**. Veer versus triple and inverted veer versus
   bash are fuzzy, and **no drill asks me to identify a play** from a picture. Add a drill such as "back sweeps wide, guard pulls,
   end left alone: which play and who's read?" That is the objective most in need of practice.
4. Designed QB runs as blocker-adders: **yes for power and draw**. For counter, **no**, because Fig 10 is unreadable on the play
   side. Sweep has no picture (acceptable).
5. Name the defensive answers: **yes** (I could name the gap exchange, but couldn't explain it beyond one line, which is fine given
   the forward link).
6. The NFL history and reading the data without being fooled: **yes**. The selection, situation and survivorship section is
   excellent.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- When the end slow-plays, what does a good quarterback actually do? (See leap 3.)
- With the pistol, "the quarterback could read either end". Who decides which end is read on a given snap: the play call, or the
  quarterback before the snap?
- How often do NFL teams really run read plays today, as opposed to plain handoffs from the same look? Is there *any* public flag
  (FTN charting?), or is it truly invisible? Say so explicitly.
- How risky is the mesh? A fumble rate on read exchanges or on pitches, versus ordinary handoffs, would turn "every exchange is a
  chance to fumble" into a number.
- Does the injury cost show up in data, such as QB injuries on designed runs versus scrambles?
- Why did designed QB runs fall in 2025, and why is 2006 high (Vick and Vince Young?)?
- Is there a shotgun or NFL version of the *triple* option (zone read plus a pitch man or bubble)? The chapter implies the triple
  is flexbone-only.
- What is the difference between bash and inverted veer, and what are "counter read" and "counter bash"?
- On the zone read keep, who blocks the Will if there's no arc? (The text says the quarterback "runs into the next defender";
  I'd like the picture to show that man.)
