# Beginner review: 08-01 What Beats What (concepts versus coverages)

Reviewer role-play: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read Parts 1–7 in order (so
smash, flood, dagger, mesh, Cover 1/2/3, Tampa 2, 2-Man, quarters, Palms, Cover 3 match, robber, fire
zone, EPA and success rate from 01-02 are all known). I read the PDF (`pdfs/08-01-concepts-versus-coverages.pdf`,
26 pages) start to finish and looked at every figure at 100 dpi.

Overall: this is a strong chapter. The four-step read, the frame strips and the "beaters are bets"
section land well. The vocabulary load is high, but nearly every term was taught earlier and is
linked. My main problems are **places where the chapter contradicts itself** (text vs matrix vs data
chart), **matrix cells that are never justified**, and **two drills whose pictures don't let me reach
the stated answer**.

## (1) Terms used before they are explained, or never explained

Almost all vocabulary is prerequisite and linked. The gaps are small:

- **"sit or go" seam** (Two-way section, "Choices inside one route"): used as if known. The gloss is
  enough, but a name in quotes suggests it is an established term; say "a seam route that..." instead,
  or link route conversion first.
- **"switch releases"** (Table 3, Cover 3 match row): I can't find it in Parts 4–6. Never explained
  here. Same for **"out-and-up"** (Palms row). It appears in 04-03 and 06-05, so it's fine, but it needs a link.
- **"half-field / combination call"**: italicised and defined in passing, which is fine. Consider a glossary
  link if another chapter owns it.
- **"a corner pressing with the wrong leverage"** (Alert section): wrong *for what*? Say which leverage
  invites which throw.
- **"Cover 3 match" / "seam-flat"** are fine. **Cover 6** is in Step 2 and the key table but has *no column*
  in the matrix, and the chapter never says how a quarterback reads a split-field coverage (pick a half?).
  I'm left not knowing what to do with it.
- Fig 1 caption: **"sky rotation"** is from 06-03, which is fine, but captions have no links, so add "(the strong
  safety rotates down, the free safety to the middle)".

## (2) Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **The opening hook doesn't add up.** "stares ... at the right-hand safety ... The safety takes two
   steps toward the middle. Then the quarterback ... throws to the left: ... a receiver standing all
   alone in the space the safety had been guarding." The right safety guards the right half. If the ball
   goes left, it lands in the *other* safety's area. Either throw right (behind the safety who moved
   to the middle) or make the stared-at safety the left one. I reread it three times.
2. **Self-contradictions between text, matrix and data. These hurt the most, because I'm told to
   "learn to rebuild" the grid:**
   - *2-Man.* The text says "Its weaknesses are short and outside", and Table 3's counter-counter is
     "quick outs". But the matrix grades **Quick game −** against 2-Man. Which is it? (Probably that stick
     and slant are inside throws. Then say so, or split the row.)
   - *Quarters.* The text says that against quarters "the quick game to the inside is dangerous". The matrix grades
     **Quick game +** against quarters. (Probably the quick *out* to #2. Say so.)
   - *Cover 3.* "almost nothing quick to the inside is free against it ... make slants and short digs a
     risk." The data chart one page later shows **slants against Cover 3 at 61%** (tied for the best slant cell)
     and **in/dig at 58%**, both above average. The text never acknowledges this.
   - *Smash's corner route.* Smash is the "textbook" Cover 2 beater, but the chart shows **corner routes
     against Cover 2 at 49%, below average**. The caution paragraph explains corner routes against *Cover 3*, not
     against Cover 2. A careful reader will hit this immediately and needs a sentence. Is it selection
     (the corner route is thrown only when the hitch was covered)? Is it sideline incompletions?
   - *Mesh/crossers against man.* The matrix gives mesh "++" against Cover 0 and Cover 1. The chart has crossers at 50% and 53%
     there, which is average. Not addressed.
   - *"a few percentage points"* (Fig 7 caption) and "most cells sit within ten points" sit beside
     hitch/curl at 63% against 38% and go routes at 28% against 43%. Those are 15–25-point swings. The headline "edges are
     small" needs reconciling with the biggest cells, for example: "within a coverage, most routes differ by a few
     points; the big swings are route-type effects."
3. **Matrix cells the chapter never justifies**, even though the caption promises "the reasoning behind
   every cell" (and so does objective 2):
   - **Levels / drive**: a whole row, never discussed in the body. Why "−" against quarters?
   - **Post-wheel ++ against Cover 3 match**: the *only* "++" in that column, and the main counter-counter in
     Table 3. There is no sentence or picture explaining why the wheel beats a seam-flat defender who carries #2.
   - **Flood + against Cover 2**, **Y-cross/Yankee ++ against Cover 3** (one sentence), **Four verticals + against fire
     zone but Dagger − against fire zone** (both send a seam, so why the difference?), **Yankee · against Cover 0 but
     Dagger/Scissors − against Cover 0** (the text says all three need 3+ seconds).
   - **Scissors**: one parenthetical ("make the safety and the corner cross and lose their leverage").
     *Why* does crossing them lose leverage? I couldn't explain scissors against quarters after reading it, which
     objective 2 asks me to do.
4. **If 2-Man is "the most orange column", why doesn't every defense play it every down?** And if Cover 3
   has *no orange at all*, why is it the NFL's most common coverage? The chapter raises both
   observations and walks away. I need 2–3 sentences: the run game, four-man rush limits, scrambles, the talent man needs,
   cost per coverage. Otherwise the matrix seems to argue that defenses are irrational.
5. **Palms rule is too compressed.** "the corner reads #2 instead of #1, and the safety takes #2 if #2
   goes vertical". Then the figure shows the corner sitting on **#1**. The missing link: *because* #2 went
   vertical, the corner is freed to jump #1 short. Spell out the if-then (and what happens if #2 goes to
   the flat).
6. **Treating ordinal grades as numbers** (Calling against a menu, Drill 3). As an analytically minded
   reader I immediately ask whether "++ = 2" is twice "+". One sentence acknowledging this is a heuristic
   would preempt the objection.
7. **"Over a season of about 600 dropbacks, a few points per throw is a great deal."** Show the arithmetic:
   3 points × 600 is about 18 extra successful plays, which is roughly X EPA, or a few drives.
8. **Drill 3 answer ignores the chapter's own point 4** ("open receiver two yards short of the sticks").
   On 3rd-and-7, mesh's 5–6 yard crossers are short of the line to gain. That is the real reason mesh's
   "safe floor" isn't safe here, and the answer should say so.
9. **How does the quarterback actually see the safeties from behind 6'5" linemen**, mid-drop? Step 2 assumes
   he just does. One sentence on where he looks (and why the shotgun helps) would help.

## (3) Diagrams: decoding problems, and captions that don't say what to notice

- **Fig 1 (QB read strip), panel 4**: the caption says the strong safety arrives "a step late", but the drawing shows the SS
  (ringed) essentially touching Z at the catch. It reads as a contested or covered throw. Move the SS 2–3 yards
  back, or say "arrives as the ball does; the catch is made, the tackle is immediate".
- **Fig 2 (look-off), panel 4**: same problem. "about three and a half yards short", but the ringed SS
  sits at the edge of the green window right next to H. At print size it looks like a near-pick. Also the hook
  describes a *two-high safety* look-off, but the figure is a *Cover 1 robber*, so the hook's play is never drawn.
- **Fig 5 (flood)**: the caption says "whichever of the two short routes ... the other one is open", but the sail
  is 13 yards, not short. Panel 4: the corner is directly above the catch point, so it looks tight. The "eyes"
  label in panel 3 sits on the nickel's ghost outline.
- **Fig 8 (mesh two-way), bottom (Cover 3)**: Y's crosser ends **on top of the nickel ($) marker**, and the
  green window is drawn over him, because $ is drawn at his pre-snap spot while his drop arrow is tiny.
  I read it as "Y sits on a defender". Shift the window or draw $ at his landed spot. Top (Cover 1): the
  dotted man-assignment lines the caption tells me to look at are barely visible (some are hidden under
  routes). The **Mike's solid orange rush arrow** is never mentioned. Why is M blitzing? Also "The corner
  routes give a high-low on the outside in both pictures": against Cover 3 the corners drop with them, so a
  high-low on whom?
- **Fig 9 (Cover 3 match)**: the "$ runs to his curl spot" label sits beside the CB, not the $. I first attributed
  it to the corner.
- **Fig 7 (data heatmap)**: the caption says cells under 100 targets "are left blank", but the 2-Man/Corner cell
  prints "n=55". Matrix columns (Cover 3 match, fire zone) and chart columns (Cover 6) don't match. Say
  so in one clause.
- **Fig 12 (Predict 2)**: the Mike is dead centre (w ≈ 0.6) at 1.1 s, so the question "where will the ball
  go?" has no answer from the picture. The answer says "the seam away from the Mike", but there isn't one yet. Show the Mike
  leaning to one seam (or put the picture at 1.6 s).
- **Fig 11 (Predict 1)**: Y is labelled a TE but stands about 3 yards outside the tackle, a step off the line.
  "Detached trips" is fine, but the picture looks like a wing. The CB's "inside shade" is invisible at this
  scale. Add a note or arrow.
- Layout: page 4 and page 7 are about 50% blank, and page 15 about 55% blank, because figures and tables float to
  the next page. This is a print layout issue worth a check.

## (4) Drag and repetition

- **"You'll need" box** (page 2) is a ~30-link wall that re-defines nearly all of Part 6. It's useful as
  reference, but it is the densest page of the chapter and comes before the hook pays off. Consider trimming it to the
  concepts and coverages *actually drawn* here and moving the "Reading the diagrams" paragraph out of it.
- **"Putting it together: identify, then predict"** (p. 19) and **"Watch for it"** (p. 21) are near-duplicates
  of each other and of Step 2. Keep one as the full routine and make the other a short checklist.
- "one second after the snap ... how many safeties" is stated at least 5 times.
- **Seahawks film room**: the Julian Love injury split (46% / 54.8%, 8–0, 15.1 ppg) is team trivia that
  doesn't teach a concept against a coverage. "29.0% first-down rate against split-safety" means nothing without the
  single-high comparison. "the first season at or above 38%" is awkward (why 38?). Say "the highest
  in the NGS era (since 2016)".
- History section: good, but "Each famous beater produced a counter" repeats Table 3's message.

## (6) Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. Why defenses play Cover 3 and 2-Man at all, given the matrix (see 2.4). How often does each coverage
   actually occur in the NFL today? A one-line rate per column (from FACTS / nflverse) would anchor the
   "menu" section.
2. How does a quarterback read split-field coverage (Cover 6)? Pick a half pre-snap? Read the side the concept goes to?
3. Who decides: does the QB change the concept at the line when he sees the shell, or only the side?
   How often does a QB actually check out of a called play? (Check-with-me is mentioned but not tied back.)
4. How does a receiver on an option or conversion route know the coverage, and how often do they disagree?
5. Where do play-action and RPOs sit in the matrix? Yankee is the only PA row. Does play-action change
   every column by freezing the eye-readers?
6. What does the look-off look like against the *two-high* safety in the hook? That play is never drawn.
7. Given the data shows small edges, how much of "great against that coverage" on broadcasts is
   hindsight? A sentence that teaches skepticism of the broadcast phrase would fit the chapter's thesis.
