# 07-03 Zone Blitzes, Fire Zones, and Simulated Pressure: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher, never played, has read Parts 1–6 plus 07-01 and 07-02 in order.
Read from the rendered PDF (`pdfs/07-03-zone-blitz-and-simulated-pressure.pdf`, 21 pages, built 2026-10-07 21:47,
rasterized at 60 and 100 dpi) and the .qmd source.

**Overall:** This chapter is strong. The idea that you rush the man the protection didn't count and drop the man it
did comes through clearly, and "Count to eleven" is the most useful tool in it. The history is fair about the
disputed origin, and two of the three Predict-the-play drills can be answered from the chapter alone. Four problems
need fixing. (1) One logical hole recurs in the chapter's main picture: *why does the QB throw hot when he has
enough blockers?* (2) Some captions contradict what the figure shows. (3) The text says two incompatible things
about what the FTN proxy counts. (4) It runs long: about 10,800 words in the PDF against a 5,000 target, and the
"hot throw into the dropper" image is restated six or seven times.

---

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

- **"back-shoulder throw"**: used in the Fig 3 discussion ("a quick hitch or a back-shoulder throw to Z") and again
  in Predict 3's answer ("or a back-shoulder fade"). The term index gives it to **10-03**, which I haven't read. The
  glossary link is the only help. It needs an inline gloss ("a throw to the receiver's outside shoulder, away from
  the defender, that he turns back for").
- **Offensive "T" and "F" in Fig 1 (Steelers plate).** The I-formation backs are labelled F and T. "How to read the
  diagrams" says only "R the running back", and **T means defensive tackle** in every other figure of this chapter.
  I had to guess that these were fullback and tailback. Either relabel them (F/R) or add them to the key and the
  caption.
- **"N" and "B" (nose, outside linebacker) in Fig 1** are explained only inside the long caption, not in the
  diagram key. That's acceptable, but "B" for an OLB is odd. Why not "O" or "OLB"?
- **Green rings in Predict 3 (Fig 10).** The key defines purple rings (droppers) and red rings (rusher to watch).
  Green rings on both corners and the nickel are never explained. I guessed they meant "look at their technique".
- **"Hole" means four overlapping things.** There is the 06-01 *hole zone* (also: rat zone), the "3 hole" and "hot
  hole" (introduced here as the hook in front of the deep middle safety), the red "hot-zone hole" in the Fig 3 key,
  and the generic "holes in the coverage". In "The middle player sits in the hook, often called the '3 hole' or
  'hot hole'", the hot hole is a spot the *defense* fills. Two paragraphs later "Where the holes are" uses "hole"
  for spots the defense *can't* fill. That tripped me up. Pick one meaning for "hole" and call the other something
  else.
- **"Is a creeper a zone blitz?"** The relationship is never stated. The opening vignette is a four-man rush (one LB
  backs out, the DE drops), which is a sim by this chapter's own definitions, yet the analyst says "zone blitz".
  Later, simulated pressure is presented as "one step further" than the fire zone. Add one sentence: "zone blitz"
  is the umbrella for any rusher/dropper exchange; a fire zone is the five-man form and a sim is the four-man form.
- **FTN** is still never glossed as "a charting company whose staff watch every play". 07-02 also assumed it. "FTN's
  charters, who record…" helps a little.
- **"three-down-lineman fronts"** in "it counts some plain three-down-lineman fronts as sims". I couldn't parse
  this. It presumably means a 3-4 where a stand-up OLB rushes as the fourth man, but that needs saying (see also
  §3, data contradiction).
- Fine as taught earlier (no action needed): #2 receiver, wall, carry (06-03); bail, spot-drop, pattern matching
  (06-01); 1-/3-technique (03-01); Mike ID, slide, half-slide, check-release (04-02); mug (07-02); EPA (01-02); the
  sticks (01-02). Glossing "the sticks" again would be nice but isn't required.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **Why does the QB throw hot when he isn't outnumbered? This is the biggest gap.** 04-02 and 07-02 taught me that
   the hot throw exists for when rushers outnumber blockers. In this chapter's key examples the QB throws hot
   *without* being outnumbered:
   - Fire-zone strip (Fig 2) and text: "His protection can handle it: six blockers for the six likely rushers… At
     the snap he sees exactly what he feared: both linebackers coming. He doesn't count blockers; he trusts his rule
     and throws hot." Only five come against six blockers, so the rule as I learned it would *not* tell him to throw
     hot. What rule is he trusting?
   - Opening vignette: four rush (one LB backs out, the DE drops) against six, and "the quarterback sees the blitz
     he expected, flicks the ball… hot."

   My guess is that the hot is keyed to a specific defender ("if the Will comes, H breaks inside now"; a sight
   adjust), and that nobody can count six rushers in half a second. The chapter never says so, and the whole
   zone-blitz mechanism depends on it. One paragraph is needed, early in the dropper section: hot reads key on *one
   defender's* movement, not on a full count; in the first 0.5 s the QB sees a linebacker come and can't yet see the
   end drop; that is why the dropper wins.
2. **Creeper strip (Fig 5): was the nickel supposed to be free or the back's man?** The caption says "H is the
   quarterback's hot receiver, because his defender, the nickel, is rushing". That implies the protection
   *conceded* the nickel by design. It then says the back "waited for a Will who never came, [and] is late to the
   nickel", which implies the nickel was the back's second man. Which is it? If the back's rule was "Will, then
   nickel", say so. As written, I can't tell whether the protection broke or worked as designed with a bad hot.
3. **Fig 4 caption overclaims.** "Five rush from where the protection didn't look". But the Will and both tackles
   come right where the protection was looking, and so does the right end. Only the nickel is a surprise. Say "one
   rusher from where the protection didn't look, plus two blockers with nobody". The surprise is the empty blockers,
   not five unexpected rushers.
4. **Third down, 4–6 yards (Fig 7).** "Man was most dominant of all at 4 to 6 yards… the distance a quick throw
   against an underneath zone converts most easily." Wouldn't a quick throw convert 3rd-and-2 even more easily
   against zone? Why is 1–3 *less* man-heavy than 4–6? I suspect run threat or the run/pass mix, but the chapter
   leaves it hanging just after making a clean "short = man, long = zone" story.
5. **"Offenses of that era often kept six or seven men in… so five rushers aren't an overload at all."** Good, but
   the chapter never says what happens when the *right* five come against a seven-man protection. Is anyone free?
   It's implied that the RB/TE "may" be assigned to the Mike or SS "or to nobody". A sentence on what a blocking
   rule does with an unaccounted safety would close it.
6. **Who does a dropper cover if nobody throws hot?** "He isn't covering a receiver. He is standing in a lane." What
   happens at 2.5 seconds, when the QB hasn't thrown and a receiver runs through the end's zone? "The price of the
   creeper" hints at it ("a real coverage liability… with room to work") but never shows it.
7. **Pattern-match fire zones.** "Saban and Belichick's pattern-matching rules… gave the three underneath defenders
   a better way to handle routes than spot-dropping." That's a big claim made in passing. One sentence on what the
   three underneath actually do with a 2x2 set (seam-flat, hook, seam-flat: each carries #2 vertical, the hook takes
   #3) would turn it into knowledge.

## 3. Diagrams and captions

- **Fig 1 (Steelers plate):** Readable and well labelled. The issues are the unexplained offensive T/F (above) and
  the colours: two of the three underneath players are ringed purple while the third, the W, has no mark at all. A
  reader counting "three under" has to find the W via the "inside LB to the weak flat" label. OK.
- **Fig 2 (fire-zone strip):**

    up, or caught and tackled? The vignette promised an interception. Show the ball in E's hands, or say "ball
    knocked down" in the panel title.
  - The thick orange line at about 8 yards (the line to gain) isn't in the strip legend. I remembered it from earlier
    chapters, but it sits right where the dropper ends up, so a one-word label ("line to gain") would help the
    third-down story.
  - In panels 2–4 the five rushers are piled onto the five linemen, so I couldn't see "six blockers handled five
    rushers perfectly". The caption claims it and the picture can't show it.
  - "deep middle" note touches the FS marker in panel 4.
- **Fig 3 (fire-zone variants):** The best figure in the chapter conceptually.
  - Panel B: the two "hole" ovals sit at 11 yards right beside the FS, overlapping the deep-third shading, and one
    label touches FS. That made me ask "isn't the deep safety right there?" The caption says "on either side of the
    deep middle safety". Move them a yard or two wider and shallower, or add a note: "the FS is at 16+ and watching
    for the deep ball".
  - The holes say only "hole". A route stub (curl, in-cut, hitch) in each would make the figure teach by itself,
    rather than depending on the following paragraph.
- **Fig 4 (protection bust):** Clear and large; the best static figure. Two caption errors:
  - "At the snap all three threats on the right never come: the Mike, the SS **and the left end**". The left end
    isn't a threat on the right. Reword to: "the two threats on the right (Mike, SS) and the left end the back was

  - "Five rush from where the protection didn't look" (see §2.3).
  - It isn't shown who blocks W, T, T, E. A thin assignment line for each would make "two of six blocked nobody"

  - Panel 1's title is "Six at the line", and the caption says "six defenders at the line". I count **five** (E T M T

  - The "C: no one to block" note sits next to R, about 4 yards from the center. I couldn't find the center among

  - Panel 4 has the same missing outcome as Fig 2: the nickel "arrives". Sack? Hurried throw? Say it.
  - The ringed E is purple in panel 1, before he has done anything. That's fine for teaching, but the title says

- **Fig 6 (rush-type chart):** Clean. The row label "Four linemen" doesn't match the text's "normal four-man rush".

- **Figs 8–10 (Predict):**

  - Fig 9: the W and its red ring overlap the T and C. The caption says the Will rushes "through the B gap", but he

  - Fig 10: green rings unexplained (above).

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items?

1. *Explain the zone-blitz trade and what the defense gives up.* **Yes.** I could explain it to a friend.
2. *Recognise a fire zone and point to the holes, and why they move.* **Mostly.** I can count 5/3/3 and find the
   dropper. For holes, I can say "next to the slowest dropper", but Fig 3 gives only "hole" with no throw, and
   Panel B's holes look like they sit on the deep safety.
3. *Tell a sim from a blitz by counting rushers; explain how four can beat six.* **Sim vs blitz: yes. Sim vs a
   normal four-man rush:** only via "who" (table rows are identical: 4/4/3), which the Watch-for-it section covers.
   I can explain "four beat six" in outline (blockers with nobody), but the creeper strip's nickel/back ambiguity
   (§2.2) leaves me unsure whether a sim makes a free rusher or just a mismatch.
4. *Narrate the protection-vs-pressure contest.* **Yes in outline** (show, commit, answer, one second). I can't
   narrate the QB's hot decision convincingly, because of §2.1.
5. *Read the pressure tells and add them to the checklist.* **Yes.** The Watch-for-it list and card are concrete.

**Predict-the-play, attempted before opening the answers:**

- **Predict 1 (six at the line, two deep).** My answer: two safeties plus two off corners means four soft/deep;
  "count to eleven" says sending six leaves one underneath, so four or five rush, one of M/W drops, and the QB should
  beware the hot slant into a dropper in the middle and throw outside instead. **This matched the answer.** I
  would not have produced the "fire zone with a safety rotating down" option on my own, because nothing in the
  figure or question hints at rotation. One sentence in the chapter connecting two-high to "a fire zone can still
  come by rotating a safety down (06-06)" would make that answerable.
- **Predict 2 (0.7 s after the snap).** My answer: four rushers (E, W, T, E) with the 3-tech dropping, so a sim;
  single-high with the corners bailing, so Cover 3; throw over or behind the dropping tackle on the right, not the
  quick slant inside. **Matched.** It was the best drill.
- **Predict 3 (third-and-2).** My answer: man, Cover 1 blitz, because of press corners, a tight nickel, one high
  safety, and the third-down chart. Beat it with quick man-beaters. **Matched the coverage**, but I couldn't name
  "back-shoulder fade" as an answer (not yet taught). The drill is a little easy: once the caption says "pressed…
  square to them" the answer is given away. Consider leaving the technique for the reader to spot in the picture
  rather than stating it in the caption.

## 6. What I still wonder (the chapter should answer)

- **How does a hot read actually work?** Is it keyed to one defender, a count, or a timing rule (§2.1)? This is the
  single most important missing piece.
- What does the dropper do when the QB *doesn't* throw hot? Show one beat later: a receiver running through his
  zone.
- What happens if the offense **runs** into a fire zone? I got one bullet ("a gamble against a run"), but no picture
  or example of the run gashing a pressure.
- **What does a two-high sim look like?** The text says modern sims come "with two-high coverage behind it as often
  as Cover 3", yet both drawn sims are Cover 3. Even a static thumbnail (4 rush, 5 under, 2 deep) would help. The
  Macdonald forward reference to 12-08 is fine for depth.
- How often do teams call fire zones at all? The data section measures only the sim proxy, with no fire-zone or
  zone-pressure share per game.
- Why is 3rd-and-4–6 the most man-heavy distance (§2.4)?
- How does the offense spot the dropper fast enough? Is it the QB's eyes, the receiver's sight adjust, or a
  pre-snap check? The chapter defers "zone-blitz beaters" to 08-01. A two-line preview of one beater (for example,
  hot routes that break *away* from the dropper, which is mentioned) would satisfy.
- **The data contradiction.** "What the charting data says" defines a blitzer as "a rusher who isn't one of the
  usual linemen **and edge rushers**", then says the proxy misses the case "an end drops and a stand-up outside
  linebacker beside him rushes" (consistent with that definition). It then says the proxy "counts some plain
  three-down-lineman fronts as sims", which only makes sense if stand-up OLBs *are* blitzers. The Fig 6 caption
  says a blitzer is "not a down lineman". These can't all be true. Pick one reading and make the two caveats agree.
