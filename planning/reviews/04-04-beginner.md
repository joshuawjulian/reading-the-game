# 04-04 Dropback Concepts — beginner-reader review

Reader persona: casual NFL viewer who has never played. Has read 01-01 to 04-03 in order, so
already knows man/zone, one-high/two-high, Cover 0–4 by name, Cover 1 and Cover 3 in outline,
the curl-flat and hook defenders, #1/#2 receiver numbering, U/H/Y/R lettering, the route tree,
high-low and horizontal stretches, the conflict defender, leverage, and slant-flat/stick/snag.
Source: `pdfs/04-04-dropback-concepts.pdf` (32 pp., built 16:42 from the current .qmd),
rasterized at 60 and 110 dpi; every page and figure examined.

Overall: strong chapter. The opener, the "why concepts" section, the time/EPA trade-off, the
heatmap and the rub legal/illegal pair are excellent. The main problems are (a) inconsistencies
in the family taxonomy and the "built to beat" claims, which undercut the chapter's key skill,
(b) four or five plates whose drawing contradicts their caption, and (c) drills that are too
easy because the diagrams give the answer away.

## 1. Terms used before they are explained, or never explained

| Where | Quote | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Flood body (p. 5) | "the same high-low you saw in curl-flat and slant-flat" | The curl-flat **concept** comes in the *next* section. The writer means the 04-01 curl-flat high-low, but the reader takes it as a pointer to something not yet shown. Say "in Passing Fundamentals' curl-flat" or reorder. |
| Drive (p. 8) vs Shallow cross (p. 8) | "One receiver runs a shallow cross, two to four yards deep" … then "**Shallow cross** uses the same two routes as drive" | "Shallow cross" is used as a **route** (drive, mesh, glossary for Drive) and as a **concept** name. | Shallow cross (p. 8) | "a dig or a 'sit' route at 10 to 12 yards" | "Sit route" is not defined anywhere. |
| QB section (p. 24) | "Each *hitch*, a short step up into the pocket" | Same word as the hitch **route** that carries smash. 04-01 taught "hitch step", but here it appears as bare "hitch" right after "on smash…". Say "hitch step" every time. |
| Conversion figure titles (p. 23) | "MOFC: stay vertical" / "MOFO: bend inside" | The acronyms appear only in panel titles; the body never uses them. The caption expands them, but a print reader sees the titles first. Spell them out in the titles or add "(MOFC)" in the body. |

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

1. **Post-wheel: who covers the post?** Body (p. 20): the Cover 3 cornerback is taught "to hand the
   post over to the free safety". Caption: "stay outside on the wheel and the post breaks open inside
   him, in front of the free safety." If the corner hands the post to the free safety, why is the post
   open? Say what the free safety is doing (held by something on the other side? too deep?). As
   written, the conflict doesn't add up.
2. **Scissors depends on a Quarters cornerback rule the reader doesn't have.** The body gives the
   *safety's* Quarters rule ("reads the inside receiver") but never the *corner's*. The caption says
   "the corner route is one-on-one against a nickel", so my question is why the Quarters cornerback,
   whose deep quarter the corner route runs into, doesn't simply take it. One sentence is enough:
   "the Quarters corner plays #1 almost man-to-man, so he follows Z inside on the post."
3. **Dagger: why do the nickel and the Mike sink with the seam?** Caption: "the seam pulls him deep,
   and the nickel and Mike sink with it". The safety's reason is given; the underneath defenders'
   reason isn't. A beginner thinks the linebackers would come *up*.
4. **Smash conversion against one-high.** "Against one deep safety, push it deeper toward the pylon"
   (p. 5). Why does deeper help? Against Cover 3 the next paragraph says the corner route "runs into a
   defender standing right on top of it", so pushing deeper looks like running further into him.
   Which one-high coverage is meant (Cover 1?), and what does the deeper route beat?
5. **Read order flips from concept to concept without a reason until page 24.** Levels is read high
   first (dig, then in); drive and shallow cross are read low first (shallow, then dig); mesh starts
   with the *corner* route even though it's "the purest man-beater". The reason ("a crosser who
   wins does so immediately"; "on third-and-8 start with the route that reaches the line to gain")
   only comes in the QB section, ten pages later. Give one clause at each plate, or put the "high to
   low or low to high" paragraph *before* the plates. For mesh in particular: why would the Air
   Raid look at the corner route first in a man-beater? That question is never answered.
6. **Drive: why is the Mike the conflict defender?** Both crossers finish on the *left*, through the
   Will's area, but the ringed defender is the Mike, and the text says "the pair passes through the
   hook zones of both linebackers". Why read the Mike rather than the Will (or the second one you
   meet)?
7. **Who covers the back in Cover 1?** In mesh (plate and animation) the Will covers R and the Mike
   rushes; in the angle-route plate the Mike covers R and the Will rushes; drill 2 has the Will on the
   back. Both are possible, but a beginner reads it as inconsistency. One line ("which linebacker
   takes the back varies; here…") would fix it.
8. **The rub figure swaps the mesh plate's assignments.** In the mesh plate and animation the **$ is
   on H** and the **SS is on Y**. In the rub figure right after it, the **SS is over H** and the **$ is
   over Y** ("the $ has to go around him"). The animation text then says "watch the strong safety
   chasing Y". Three consecutive figures, two mappings: I had to stop and re-check which defender
   chased whom.
9. **Is a pick inside one yard legal?** The green band says "first yard: contact allowed" and the text
   says such contact "isn't pass interference". So can receivers legally pick within a yard (the
   stack and bunch releases the text mentions)? The chapter implies yes but never says it, and that is
   exactly what a replay viewer needs.
10. **Heatmap: the −0.42 cell.** Throws behind the line to the middle are by far the worst cell. A
    curious reader asks what those throws are (screens? shovels? batted throwaways?). One clause would do.

## 3. Diagrams that were hard to decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **Families figure, panel 3 "Triangle" (p. 3).** The caption says "three receivers around two
  defenders", but the shaded triangle contains only **one** defender; the other sits outside it to the
  left. I couldn't see which route was "high" and which two were "low", because the triangle's corners
  don't sit on the route endpoints.
- **Curl-flat plate (p. 6).** Same problem at full size. The caption says the triangle surrounds the $
  and the Mike, but the **ringed Mike is outside the shaded triangle**, and the back's "hook" arrow
  ends almost on top of the Mike. Why would a route that settles next to the Mike be open? The
  progression bubble "3" also touches the Mike's ring.
- **Angle route plate (p. 17).** The back's route ends **on the Mike's box**, so the picture reads as
  "run into the linebacker". The Mike's reaction arrow goes right, but the route still terminates at
  his starting spot. The route also passes through the right end's (E) box on the release. Show the
  Mike displaced, with the route breaking behind him.
- **Drive plate (p. 8).** Y's "shallow at 2–4 yd" is drawn at about 1.5 yards and runs **through the
  E and T boxes**, so it looks like a receiver running through the defensive line. The "dig at 12 yd"
  label sits on the orange line-to-gain.
- **Shallow cross plate (p. 9).** The shallow line passes **through the Mike's box**, the defender
  you're told to read.
- **Y-cross plate (p. 12).** Y's crosser runs **through the $ box** at 5 yards. The caption says the
  cross climbs to "about 15", while the body says "15 or 18" and then "an 18-yard crosser needs three
  seconds".
- **Smash plate (p. 4).** The caption says the corner route "flatten[s] toward the sideline at about 16
  yards", but it is drawn as one straight diagonal from 9 to 17 with no flattening. Hitch "five or six
  yards" vs the CB box: the hitch arrowhead disappears into the CB.
- **The orange line.** Every plate has a thick orange line (at 8 yards on smash, at 10 on most others,
  at about 6 on angle and mesh) and no plate legend lists it. Earlier chapters taught the line to gain,
  but on a plate with no down-and-distance it reads as a mystery depth marker. Either add "line to
  gain" to the plate legend or give each caption a down and distance.
- **Drill 1 (p. 28): red rings on both cornerbacks.** The chapter defines a *green* ring as "defender
  in conflict". These rings are red/orange and unexplained, and they also give away the answer (see §5).
- **Mesh animation, frames 3–4 (p. 16).** At strip size the ringed SS and the trailing $ are
  readable, but you can't tell which receiver is H and which is Y once they've crossed (both labels
  sit beside defenders). The caption carries the story; the frames don't on their own.
- **Four-verticals animation (p. 19).** Frame 3 says "The QB looks right; the FS leans to Y", but
  nothing in the frame shows the QB's eyes, and the FS's lean is a fraction of a yard. The look-off,
  the point of the strip, isn't visible. Consider an arrow for the QB's eyes and a bigger lean.
- **Conversion figure, left panel (p. 23).** The caption says he "bends slightly away from the safety";
  the drawn route is straight.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Captions duplicate the body.** Most plate captions (smash, flood, dagger, post-wheel, scissors)
  are 80–110 words and walk through the same "if he does X, throw Y" branches that the next paragraph
  repeats almost word for word. Pick one place for the branch logic (I'd keep it in the caption and
  let the body give the *why*).
- **Print layout:** pages 9, 10, 12, 13, 16, 17, 18, 20, 21 and 24 are a third to a half blank
  because the plates float to the next page. It reads as a choppy 32 pages. Page 24 is mostly empty
  before the table.
- **Film rooms are general.** None names a findable game or play. "Yankee among them" (Rams 2018)
  doesn't give me a single clip to look up. One named game or play per film room would turn
  "what to notice" into something I can do tonight.
- The "families" idea is restated four times (families section, table, Watch-for-it, Takeaways)
  and, worse, inconsistently (see §5, item 3).

## 5. Can I do each "You'll be able to…"? (drills attempted before reading answers)

**Drill 1 (two high, corners squatting).** My answer: the corner route, reading the cornerback;
come off it if the corner sinks or the safety widens to the sideline. **Correct**, but it's a
near-copy of the smash plate (same formation, same defense, same routes), and the diagram **rings
both cornerbacks**, which hands me the defender to watch. Two caption details, "the nickel inside Y"
and "the Will walked out over H", are never used in the answer, and "safeties *well outside the
hash*" (which should make the corner route *harder*) isn't addressed. Either use those details in the
answer or drop them.

**Drill 2 (press man, third-and-4).** My answer: mesh, because press, a single high safety and a
defender travelling with motion all mean man. **Correct**, and easy, because the diagram **draws the
man-assignment dotted lines**, so the picture says "man" before I've read a cue. The motion, which the
answer calls "the clincher", is in the caption only. The answer also says "the defenders pressing H and
Y", but Y isn't pressed (the SS "stands across from Y" at 5 yards).

**Drill 3 (one safety over the left hash).** My answer: Y's seam on the right; look left to hold him.
**Correct.** Again the conflict defender is pre-ringed.

All three drills test recognition of a plate I just saw, not transfer. None asks me to choose
**between** two concepts that both look plausible (for example, two-high with the corners off: smash
or dagger?), to classify a concept the chapter didn't draw, or to read a post-snap picture.

**Objective by objective:**

1. *Say what a pass concept is and why* — **Yes.** Clear and convincing.
2. *Draw fourteen concepts and point to the conflict defender* — **Mostly.** I could draw 11–12. Shaky:
   scissors vs post-wheel ("post-wheel's mirror image" — it isn't a mirror; it's a different inside
   route), curl-flat (the plate's triangle confuses), Y-cross depth (15 vs 18).
3. *Sort any concept into a family and name the coverage it beats* — **No, not reliably**, because the
   chapter contradicts itself:
   - The section heading "Vertical stretches on the sideline: smash, flood **and curl-flat**", but
     curl-flat's tag and table row say **Triangle**.
   - Four verticals: the table says built to beat "**Cover 3, Cover 2**" and the body explains how it
     beats Cover 2; Watch-for-it and Takeaways list it as a **one-high** concept only.
   - The Watch-for-it replay rule ("one route above the other = vertical, side by side = horizontal,
     **crossing = man-beater**") misclassifies the chapter's own concepts. Drive and shallow cross
     cross, but they are "vertical"; scissors crosses but is "horizontal"; Y-cross crosses but is a
     "three-level vertical". Following the chapter's own rule gives the wrong answer.
   - Post-wheel is called a horizontal stretch ("inside and out"), yet its conflict is described as a
     cornerback choosing between a deep-outside and a deep-inside route at different times, which reads
     more like a high-low/timing problem. A sentence on why it counts as horizontal would help.
4. *Walk the progression for each* — **Yes, by memorizing the table**, but I couldn't *derive* one,
   because the high-first vs low-first rule comes too late (see §2, item 5).
5. *Spot a rub and say where it becomes illegal* — **Partly.** I know the one-yard line and "deliberate
   block". I don't know whether a pick inside one yard is legal, or which time limit (thrown or
   touched) applies (§1, §2 item 9).
6. *Explain tags and route conversions* — **Yes.** The tags/conversions section and the Madden callout
   are among the best parts of the chapter.

## 6. What I still wonder (and the chapter should answer)

- **Who chooses the concept, and when?** Drill 2 is framed as "the offense has three concepts on its
  call sheet", but can the QB switch from flood to mesh at the line after seeing the motion clue? It's
  a check-with-me (01-05)? One sentence connecting "read man pre-snap" to "and here's how the offense
  acts on it" closes the loop.
- **What happens to the grey routes?** If a "route outside the read" is wide open, is the QB allowed to
  throw it? Are they ever in the progression?
- **What beats these concepts when the defense blitzes?** The chapter says "he checks the protection
  and his hot route", but on a 3-second concept against a 6-man pressure, which route is hot?
- **Pre-snap, how do I tell smash from flood from post-wheel?** All three start as "a slot and an
  outside receiver on the same side". The Watch-for-it "find the pairs" tip (and "two slots on
  opposite sides = mesh or shallow cross") applies to *every* 2x2 snap, so it doesn't discriminate.
  Are there formation, personnel or down-and-distance tells?
- **How often is each concept actually called?** For an analytically minded reader: which of the 14
  are NFL staples, and which are college or situational? Even a rough ranking, or a pointer to the BDB
  route data you mention at the end, would help.
- **Field position and the hash.** Plates put the ball in the middle "to keep the picture symmetric",
  and the text says concepts usually go to the wide side. How much does the hash change smash or
  flood? A one-line note or a small inset would do.
- **Broadcast reality.** On the TV angle I can't see the safeties. How do I find the shell live?
  A pointer back to 01-06 would close this.
