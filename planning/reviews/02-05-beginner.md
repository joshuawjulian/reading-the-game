# 02-05 A First Look at Defense — beginner-reader review

Reader persona: casual NFL viewer, never played, has read 01-01 to 02-04 in order (so already
knows the positions, Sam/Mike/Will, the $ slot corner, man vs zone in one line each from 01-03,
and base/nickel/dime plus substitution matching from 02-01). Read the source start to finish and
looked at every page of `pdfs/02-05-a-first-look-at-defense.pdf` (21 pp, rasterised at 60 and 110 dpi).

Overall: strong, clear, and the "eleven players, one budget" spine works. The box and apex
sections are the best part; I could count a box after reading them. The main problems are: the
blocker count changes between figures and drills, so I got Drill 2 "wrong" by following the
chapter's own rule; one caption contradicts its own picture (the rush/blitz figure); the
light/fair/normal/loaded box words overlap; and a large part of the packages section repeats
02-01 almost point for point.

## 2. Leaps (a "why" that's missing or assumed)

1. **The blocker count is never pinned down, and the chapter breaks its own rule.** Fig 4: "blockers:
   6 (5 linemen + Y)". Watch-for-it: "five linemen, plus any tight end on the line and any
   fullback". Fig 9, four-man rush: Y *and* the RB both block, so there are 7 blockers. Drill 2
   answer: "six blockers (five linemen and the back)", which leaves out the attached Y. Following
   the checklist I counted 7 and got the drill "wrong". The missing why: **for a run you count
   linemen + in-line TE + fullback; for a pass you count who *stays in to protect*, which you can't
   know before the snap. A TE and a back may block or run routes.** Say this once, explicitly,
   in the box section or the blitz section, then make Drill 2 and fig 9 follow it.
2. **Light vs fair vs normal vs loaded are three overlapping scales.** Glossary: a light box is "no
   more defenders than the offense has blockers... usually six or fewer". Fig 4: 6 v 6 is "a fair
   fight". Text: "Seven... analysts treat it as the normal box". So is six light or fair? Is seven
   normal, or one too many? And against 12 personnel (seven blockers) is seven "light"? Make one
   scale: relative (box minus blockers: −1, 0, +1, +2), then say how broadcasters use absolute
   numbers (6 light, 7 normal, 8 loaded *against one-back sets*).
3. **Where the box ends when you can't measure "one step".** "From just outside one end of the
   offensive line to just outside the other" works in a figure. On TV, with a nickel back 1.5 yards
   outside a tight end, I don't know whether to count him. Drill 1 tests exactly this: the $ is
   about 3 yards outside U. Give a rule of thumb (for example "if he's outside the tight end by more
   than a yard or two, or level with the slot, he's out") and point out that the apex player is the
   usual borderline case.
4. **Why the Sam leaves for the nickel, not the Will.** Fig 1 rings the **W** in base as "the
   linebacker who must cover the slot", and the caption says nickel "replaces **that** linebacker".
   But the nickel panel still has a W (and an M). The Sam is the one who disappeared. 01-03 said the
   Sam is usually first off. So the picture contradicts the caption, and the reader is left to
   work out that the W slid back inside. Either fix the caption ("takes a linebacker off, usually
   the Sam, and the Will slides back inside") or explain why.
5. **Rushing three.** Fig 10 shows the offense's best result (+0.13 EPA) against three or fewer
   rushers, but the text never explains why defenses ever rush three, or why it does so badly.
   A numerate reader will notice the tallest bar.
6. **"Barely changes" is a stretch.** In the same panel, five rushers gives +0.07 against +0.03
   for four, more than double. The text says "the offense did about as well against five or six
   rushers as against four". Either give the uncertainty (sample sizes, or rough ± ranges) so I
   can see the gap is noise, or say plainly that five-man blitzes did slightly *worse* for the
   defense in 2025. As written, an analytically minded reader will distrust the sentence.
7. **Shell → coverage numbers mapping is half done.** We get Cover 1 and Cover 3 as one-high
   homes, but it never says that Cover 2 and Cover 4 are the two-high coverages. One sentence would
   make the "Cover N" paragraph and the shell section join up.
8. **Man/zone vanishes from the "five questions".** The intro lists five questions (Q4 is "man or
   zone, and one safety or two"), but Table 2 ("the pre-snap read of a defense") has Package / Box /
   Apex / Shell / Rush, with no man/zone row. It is also titled "pre-snap" yet includes Rush, which
   is post-snap. The Watch-for-it checklist then orders rushers before man/zone. Pick one list and
   use it in all three places.
9. **Drill 3, bullet 2:** "If he widens toward the slot... the box is six defenders against six
   blockers and the defense has one fewer man near the run". He was already outside the box
   (fig 4, the drill text: "six in the box"), so his widening doesn't change the box count. The real
   why is that he can no longer *arrive late* to the edge (the "count him as half" idea). Say that.

## 3. Diagrams and captions

- **Fig 9 (four-man rush vs blitz): the caption is wrong for its own picture.** In both panels Y
  has a blocking T-bar, and so does the RB. So the blitz panel shows 7 blockers against 5 rushers.
  The caption says "The offense has a **sixth** blocker, the running back... If the back had gone
  out on a pass route instead, the Mike would have been unblocked". With Y blocking, that isn't true
  (6 v 5). Either send Y out on a route in both panels (cleaner: then it really is 5 OL + RB v 5)
  or fix the caption. This matters because this figure is where "blitz math" is first shown.
- **Fig 1 (package plates):** W ringed in base, W still present in nickel (see Leap 4). Otherwise
  very readable. The four panels side by side work well.
- **Fig 7 (man vs zone strip):** decodable, and the labels "CB stayed with X / CB took H" in the
  last frame are what made it click. Two nits: (a) the zone caption says each defender "sinks into
  his area", but the zone CB comes *forward* to about 3.5 yards by +1.5 s; (b) "the cornerback
  guards the short area near the sideline and the nickel back the area inside it, about 8 to 10
  yards deep" reads as if both are at 8–10. Say which depth belongs to whom. At +1.5 s the X/H
  trails cross right under the $ in the zone row and are hard to separate at print size. A
  +2.0 s frame, or slightly wider spacing, would help.
- **Fig 6 (apex):** excellent; the tick marks make "halfway" concrete. The left E sits right on
  the shaded box's left edge, so I wasn't sure the E is "in". Nudge the box edge out by half a yard.
- **Fig 8 (shells):** clear. The ovals plus "the other safety joins the box" make the trade
  visible.
- **Fig 10:** the darker bar for "4" has no legend or caption mention. Add "(darker: the
  norm, four rushers)".
- **Drill figures:** clean and uncluttered, which is good for a drill. Drill 3's picture is the
  same as the apex figure without labels, which is fine, but see the "too easy" note below.
- Print layout: pp. 3, 7 and 13 are each about 40% blank because a table or figure was pushed to
  the next page. Not a content issue, but noticeable.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **The packages section and "Why nickel is the modern base" largely repeat 02-01.** 02-01
  already gave: base/nickel/dime with DB counts; "match bodies to bodies"; the sideline staffer
  shouting the package ("Someone on every defensive staff is watching... calls out the grouping"
  ↔ 02-05 "A defensive staff has a person on the sideline whose job... is mostly this"); base at
  ~30% in 2025 against ~20% in 2023; the 12-personnel bind ("run at nickel / throw at base", with
  its own figure); Emmanwori as a 220-lb nickel; and the arms-race paragraph almost word for word
  ("offenses got bigger... defenses answered by playing more base in 2025"). I felt I'd read
  "Why nickel is the modern base" before. Fix: open the section with "You met these in
  Personnel; here's what that chapter left out" and cut the re-told stats. Keep the trend chart
  and the Seattle film room, which are new.
- The 4-3/3-4 explanation and the "3-4 rushes four" misconception partly repeat 01-03's
  edge-rusher section. It's shorter here and earns its place as a misconception callout, but
  "they are edge rushers who happen to be listed as linebackers" is 01-03's point again.
- "The box is a budget" / "eleven players, one budget" / "the shell and the box are two readings
  of the same budget" / "spent their eleven players on purpose": the metaphor is good, but it
  appears about six times. Use it at three key points.
- The intro paragraph after the five questions ("A real defensive call bundles... front...
  coverage... pressure") and the Madden callout say the same thing about package vs call. One can
  shrink.
- Hamilton film room: the second paragraph (ESPN slot snaps, the quote) is good. The "By this
  book's count... 76 percent, five times the league rate (15 percent)" stats plus the Macdonald
  path to Seattle plus the Seattle film room earlier make Seattle/Baltimore/big nickel come up
  three times.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. When the TV shows a defense, how do I tell nickel from base quickly if I missed the
   substitution? (The checklist says "count the defensive backs when you can", but how do you
   spot a DB versus a LB at TV resolution? Size? Depth? Numbers? 20s/30s/40s versus 50s.) One
   practical tip would help a lot. Jersey-number ranges were covered in 01-03? If so, link back.
2. Why does the Sam (not the Will) leave in nickel? (See Leap 4.)
3. What does a 3-4 team's nickel look like? Is it 2-4-5, and why do the table's alternatives
   (3-3-5, 2-4-5) exist?
4. Why rush three, and why did it go so badly in 2025 (fig 10)?
5. If the box is "a budget", where do the other players go in a typical 7-man box? A
   one-line census (7 box + 2 CB + $ + FS = 11) would make the arithmetic concrete. Fig 4 right
   panel almost does this; label it.
6. What do broadcasters mean by "stacked box" or "eight-man front"? (Common TV phrases; worth
   one line as synonyms for loaded box.)
7. How does a defense decide man or zone? Does it depend on shell, personnel, down? The chapter
   says one-high is the "natural home" of man, but then most one-high is Cover 3 zone. How
   should I weigh one-high + press against one-high + off corners? (This is the same gap as
   Drill 2.)
8. The hook's "they showed blitz and dropped out of it": is that simulated pressure or just a
   bluff? The chapter separates these at the end (show-and-drop versus sim pressure) but doesn't
   point back to the hook. Close the loop.
