# 04-03 beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: a casual NFL watcher who has never played, is analytically minded, and has read
01-01 through 04-02 in curriculum order. From those chapters I know: personnel and formations, 2x2 and
trips, #1/#2/#3, the $ symbol for the nickel back, man vs zone, one-high vs two-high, Cover 0/1/2/3 as
names (02-05), the box count, EPA and success rate (positive EPA, 01-02), the route tree, sluggo, option
route, timing route, drops, the conflict defender, the high-low and horizontal stretches, the curl-flat
defender and hook zone, aDOT/YAC (04-01), and protections, hot routes, sight adjusts, time to throw and
the pressure-by-time chart (04-02). I have **not** read 06-01 (press/off/cushion from the DB's side),
06-02/06-03, 07-02, or 04-04.

Sources: `chapters/04-the-passing-game/04-03-quick-game.qmd` (lines as `L###`) and
`pdfs/04-03-quick-game.pdf` (23 pages, rasterized at 80 dpi and viewed; pages as `p##`).

Overall: this is a strong chapter. The "one defender, two receivers, throw to the one he doesn't take"
spine works, and I came away able to do most of the "You'll be able to…" list. The answers print at the
chapter end correctly (p22–23). The main problems are (a) a few internal contradictions a careful reader
trips on, (b) noticeable repetition of 04-01 and 04-02, and (c) the drills are too guided by their
captions to test anything. Length is about 8,600 words of prose against the ~5,000 target.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

1. **"success-rate yardstick (did the play gain enough to stay on track?)"** (L503–505, p2). That is
   the old yardage definition. 01-02 taught me that success = positive EPA, and this chapter's own
   footnote [^ttt25b] says "Success means positive EPA." The gloss contradicts the book's definition.
   Fix the gloss to match 01-02.
2. **"seam"** carries two meanings in this chapter. Spacing: "puts a receiver in the **seam** between
   each pair of them" (L862), meaning a gap between zones. Hitch-seam and the speed out: "seam" is a
   route (L966, "seam holds the safety" label on p10). Then "can't be in both **seams** at once" (L967)
   mixes them. Use "gap" or "window" for spacing.
3. **Defender-movement verbs: widen / sit / sink / jump / drive / bail.** They are used as if they are
   defined ("Did he widen, sink or sit?", L1505), but each appears once in passing. "Sits" in slant-flat
   (L680) means staying put. "Sits under the stick" (L784) means moving inside. "Sinks" (L690, L1004)
   means dropping deeper. "Jumps" (L1005) means driving forward. "Bails" (L724) is never glossed. A
   four-line box ("what the read defender can do and what each move gives you") would turn the
   Watch-for-it item into something I can actually do.
4. **"tag"** in "Many offenses **tag** the now screen onto ordinary plays" (L1210). This is jargon with
   no gloss. Write "attach" or gloss it.
5. **"back pylon"** in the snag caption (L815, p8). I don't know the pylon, so "toward the back pylon"
   tells me nothing. Say "toward the back corner of the end zone / the sideline."
6. **"pumps"** in "the quarterback often pumps as if throwing it" (L1155). A gloss would help:
   "pump-fakes: starts a throwing motion without letting go."
7. **"hot version"** in "Every quick concept has a 'hot' version" (Madden callout, L1390). 04-02 taught
   hot routes, but not what a concept's "hot version" is. Is it the same routes, thrown earlier? Or a
   different route?
8. **"press-man" / "press man"** appears in a figure title on p11 ("Double slants vs press man", L988)
   one section *before* press is defined (L1022). 02-05 did gloss press, so this is mild, but the
   chapter should say "press (lined up right on him; defined below)" at first use, or reorder.
9. **"Head-up … We'll come back to it."** (L644–645). The chapter never returns to head-up alignment.
   Either come back to it in the release section (what does a head-up press corner want, and which
   release beats him?) or cut the promise.
10. **"BDB-format tracking"** in two captions (p6, p16). I know "Big Data Bowl" only vaguely. One
    clause at first use would help ("the NFL's public tracking-data format").
11. **"illegal contact or defensive holding"** (L1037) are named and footnoted but never explained
    in the text. A half-sentence saying what each costs (5 yards and an automatic first down) belongs
    in the body, because it explains *why* press stops at five yards.

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

1. **Three different "quick" clocks.** The definition is "2.5 seconds or less" (L428). Rhythm gives
   "from the shotgun, about a second to a second and a half" (L576). The stopwatch says "gone before
   you finish 'two'" (L1499) and then "half … out before two-and-a-half" (L1500). The signature
   animation's ball is out at 2.1 s, with the catch at 3.2 s (p16). I can't tell whether a 2.1-second
   slant is "on rhythm" or late. I need one sentence reconciling these: rhythm release is about 1–1.5
   s; 2.5 s is the analysts' outer line, which allows for press, hitches and the time measurement
   includes; the stopwatch "two" is a rule of thumb. The pressure play also needs a sentence saying
   *why* this slant came out at 2.1 and not 1.2: the jam cost him a step, which is the press section's
   own point.
2. **The five-man pressure play: "five blockers face five rushers," yet one is free** (L1290 caption,
   L1330 note "Five rush, five block … Nobody can block the $"). Two paragraphs earlier I was told five
   or six blockers is "still enough to pick up everyone" (L1280). So why is the fifth rusher free when
   the numbers match? In the frames the five linemen are all on the four linemen. The answer is
   presumably that the protection slid or set inside and the edge blitzer was outside the tackle's
   reach. 04-02 gave me the vocabulary for this (slide, free rusher), so say it in one sentence.
3. **"Seven concepts … Each one picks a defender, puts two receivers on opposite sides of him"**
   (L1012–1016) contradicts "Not every quick throw is a two-man read. The speed out is a single
   receiver's route" (L904). Spacing has "no single defender to read" (L891) either. The Takeaways
   (L1641–1643) repeat the claim and silently drop the speed out. Either qualify it ("five of the
   seven…") or give the speed out and spacing their own line.
4. **Snag's read order.** The figure's read box (p8) gives a fixed order: "1. CB sits in the flat?
   Corner. 2. $ widens? Snag. 3. $ sits? Flat." The prose (L848–850) says the quarterback reads "from
   the top or the bottom depending on the coverage." The figure is Cover 3, where the prose says read 1
   is "usually covered." So against the coverage drawn, what does the QB actually look at first? And
   against Cover 3, how is snag better than stick if it comes down to the same $ read? The "why snag
   over stick" question goes unanswered.
5. **Why is the speed-out corner *inside*, and why "two-high"?** The leverage section taught that a
   corner plays inside leverage when his help is outside (L627–628). The speed-out figure is "a
   two-high look" with the corner inside and backing up (p10). In two-high, where is his outside help,
   and why would he align inside? As it stands I just have to accept the look.
6. **"A slant needs an inside release"** (L1105), but panel 3 of the release figure (p12) shows a
   receiver releasing *outside* and then breaking *inside*. The panel is about stem and stack, but it
   reads as a contradiction. One clause would fix it: "on longer routes the release and the break can
   go opposite ways; on a 3-step slant there's no time."
7. **Hitch-seam "against Cover 3 … the deep defender in the middle, who can't be in both seams at
   once"** (L966–967). The figure shows one seam on one side. Where is the second seam? It needs "if
   the other side also runs a seam."
8. **Double slants "against zone, that is a high-low on the hook defender"** (L953–954). The figure
   shows only the man version, so the zone claim is asserted, not shown. That's fine if the caption or
   text says so, but the reader can't check it.
9. **Leverage figure's "taken away" routes** (p4). In the left panel the conceded "out" is drawn as a
   45-degree diagonal ending in an X, which looks like a corner route, not an out. In the right panel
   the taken slant is a diagonal too. Draw the actual out and slant shapes, greyed, so I learn the
   shapes and not just "left/right."
10. **Chiefs aDOT story** (p19). The chart shows KC's depth falling from 2018 to 2019, 2020 and 2021,
    *while Hill was still there*, and rebounding to 7.8 in 2025. The text attributes the change to "after
    the Hill trade." A data-minded reader sees that the trend started three seasons earlier. Say so,
    and say a word about 2025, or the chart undercuts the claim.
11. **Is blitzing good or bad for the defense overall?** The EPA chart (p17) compares quick vs held
    *within* rush counts. "A blitz is a coin flip" (L1382) begs the obvious question: what is EPA per
    dropback against 5+ rushers vs 4, all outcomes pooled? The same data gives it in one line.
12. **"His pre-snap read was 'if the $ comes, the ball goes to X'"** (L1341). How does the QB know the
    $ isn't bluffing? The drill-2 answer mentions bluffs, but the main text should connect "creeping
    toward the line" to a post-snap confirmation.

## 4. Drag and repetition

1. **Walsh/Carter history repeats 04-01.** 04-01's "A short history: Walsh and the three-step game"
   (04-01 L874–884) already told me Walsh, Paul Brown's Bengals, Carter's weak arm, the 1971
   completion title, San Francisco in 1979 and three Super Bowls. This chapter retells all of it
   (L533–555) without acknowledging that. The new material (Greg Cook, Rice's 22 TDs, the Carter–Machol
   EP paper, the 1978 rule) is good. Open with "You met Walsh's Bengals in Passing Fundamentals; here's
   the rest," then keep only the new material.
2. **fig-ttt-pressure (p2) repeats 04-02's fig-pressure-by-time** (pressure share by time to throw,
   2024–25). The new parts are the air-yards and completion row. Acknowledge 04-02 ("you saw pressure
   climb with the clock; here's the other half of the bargain") and make depth/completion the headline.
3. **Spacing (L857–900) re-teaches 04-01's horizontal stretch,** including a near-duplicate figure.
   Build on it instead: "04-01 showed the stretch. As a called concept, what's new is that the QB picks
   a side pre-snap and throws on his foot." That cuts a figure or makes it show something new (e.g.,
   the pre-snap side choice, or spacing vs man).
4. **The slant-flat animation** is structurally the same as 04-01's curl-flat high-low animation (ringed
   $, two branches). That's acceptable since it's the concept, but say "same read as the curl-flat in
   04-01, now at 3 steps."
5. **The double-slants rub-legality parenthetical** (L959–962) is a 60-word aside in the middle of a
   concept description. Move it to a footnote or one sentence.
6. **"Why it exists, and what it costs" callout (L518–529) and the takeaways** restate the
   depth-for-certainty trade a third time (also L488–492). The chart paragraph can lose its last
   sentence.
7. The three film rooms are good but long, and none names a **single play** (game, situation) I could
   look up, which AUTHORING §2.6 asks for "ideally." One concrete clip (a Montana-to-Rice slant, a
   Kelce stick on a specific third down) would beat a paragraph of season stats.

## 5. Can I do each "You'll be able to…"? (drills attempted before opening answers)

- **Say what the quick game is for and what it gives up:** Yes. The three jobs and the costs are clear.
- **Draw the seven concepts and name the defender each binds:** Mostly. Slant-flat, stick, hitch-seam
  and double slants: yes. Snag: I can draw it but can't say which defender to read against Cover 3 vs
  Cover 2 with confidence (§2.4). Spacing: there's "no defender," so the objective's wording doesn't fit
  (§2.3). Speed out: I can draw it, but the bound defender is the corner, and the "why inside" is
  missing.
- **Read leverage and pick a side:** Yes for off corners. For press corners only partly, since head-up
  is never resolved (§1.9).
- **Follow the press fight:** Yes conceptually (release → stem → stack → break → catch point → double
  move), but the release panels don't show the jab/sell (§3.3).
- **Spot now and bubble screens:** Yes. Numbers/leverage/grass is excellent and directly usable.
- **Time a quick throw:** Yes, but which threshold (§2.1)?

**Drill 1 (pick a side):** My answer was the left side, reading the $, because the left CB is off and
outside, which concedes the slant, while the right CB is pressed inside and the SS is already in the
flat. **Correct.** But the caption itself lists every relevant fact ("CB off, outside", "CB pressed,
inside", "SS … almost in the flat already"), and the field labels repeat them. I matched labels; I
didn't read a picture. Let the picture carry the leverage and keep the caption to the situation.
Also, the release section said a corner pressing with inside leverage invites the fade or quick out
(L1106–1107), so the right side arguably has its own answer. The answer should say why the left is
still better.

**Drill 2 (six at the line):** My answer was a zero blitz, so a hot/quick throw under two seconds,
probably a slant, or a quick screen. **Mostly right.** The answer ruled out the screen with a reason I
could follow (no cushion). But "to whom?" isn't really answered. Both corners are identical, so which
side? (The side of the unblocked rusher? The QB's preference?) The answer also never addresses the
question I most wanted answered: with *nobody* deep, why not throw the fade or go behind the press?
I couldn't see the outside-shoulder leverage the answer relies on (§3.1).

**Drill 3 (count them):** My answer was a bubble to the trips side. **Correct.** But the figure prints
"3 receivers, 2 defenders" on the field, so the counting the drill title asks for is done for me.
Remove the bracket, or move it into the answer.

Net: all three drills are recall checks, not prediction tests. Each needs one ambiguity the reader has
to resolve from the picture.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. If the quick game beats the blitz this well (+0.23 vs −0.10), why do defenses blitz 27% of the
   time? What does the blitz buy them overall? (One pooled number, plus a pointer to 07-02.)
2. Against Cover 0 with nobody deep, when does an offense take the fade/go shot instead of the slant?
3. How does the QB choose *between* sides when both look equally good (drill 2), and who decides: the
   QB, the play call, or a signal from the sideline?
4. What does a defense do to take away slant-flat specifically? The chapter mentions Cover 2 squat
   corners, but a sentence on "robbing" the slant (a defender sinking under it) would round out the
   read.
5. How do I tell a quick-game throw from a screen or an RPO throw on a broadcast in real time? They all
   come out in about one second.
6. Head-up press: which release beats it (§1.9)?
7. Is a 2.1-second slant against press still "on rhythm"? What does a *late* quick throw look like
   from the couch, so I can spot one?
8. The Chiefs' aDOT was falling before the Hill trade. What else changed in 2019–21 (§2.10)?
