# 11-03 The College Laboratory: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read only the chapters before
this one in curriculum order (Parts 01-10, 11-01, 11-02). Read the .qmd start to finish and looked
at every figure in `pdfs/11-03-the-college-laboratory.pdf` (24 pages, built 2026-10-08 05:40, after
the last .qmd edit), with 200-dpi zooms where labels were small.

Overall: a strong chapter. The "four forces, then the filter" frame sets up everything that follows,
and the box-count figure (Fig 4) and the four-backfield figure (Fig 3) are the best teaching in it.
The main problems are (1) Fig 5, the chapter's signature play, where the give and keep paths look
as if they belong to the wrong players; (2) Drill 1, where the prompt and the answer count the box
differently; (3) a handful of internal miscounts and mislabels; and (4) length, about 9,800 words
of prose and captions against a 6,500 target, with the same rules re-explained four or five times.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

Most vocabulary is either taught in earlier chapters (wildcat 02-04, Kick Six 04-06, now screen
04-03, zone read / read option 03-04) or glossed where it first appears. What remains:

- **"two 4-techniques on the tackles' inside shoulders"** (Tite and mint, ~l.1093). 03-01 and 05-03
  taught that a 4 is head-up on the tackle and a **4i** is on his inside shoulder. 05-03's glossary
  entry and this chapter's own Fig 8 / Drill 1 both say **4i**. As written, a careful reader thinks
  he misremembered 03-01. Change the text to "two 4i-techniques".
- **Technique numbers in the figures.** "How to read the diagrams" explains 5 and 3, but the figures
  also use **2i, 1, 4i, 7** (Figs 2, 4, 8, 10, 11). They were taught in 03-01, but the legend picks
  two examples that don't cover what's drawn. Add "2i/4i = inside shoulder of the guard/tackle;
  1 = the center-guard gap; 7 = outside a tight end", or point to 03-01.
- **"waggle"** and **"the bootleg"** (Wing-T paragraph, ~l.686; Table 1). The trap and counter are
  familiar from Part 03, and the buck sweep is linked, but the waggle isn't glossed anywhere here.
  One clause: "the quarterback fakes the sweep and rolls out the other way to pass".
- **"hat math"** (~l.777) is a bare link. One clause ("count blockers against defenders") would let
  the reader keep going without clicking through.
- **"picture boards"** (Tempo, ~l.969; Helmet communication, ~l.1356). Never described. "Big
  posters on the sideline whose pictures code the play" would do it.
- **"packaged plays"** (Fig 1 row label "RPO (packaged plays)"). The term never appears in the text.
- **"NAIA"** (~l.719), **"the service academies"** (~l.583), **"Big 12" / "SEC"**. These are minor,
  but an NFL-only viewer doesn't know the college map. A parenthetical each: "NAIA (a small-college
  association below the NCAA's divisions)", "Army, Navy, Air Force".
- **Two "SS" labels in one defense** (Fig 8, Fig 10 / Drill 1: "SS, FS, SS"). The reader learned
  that there is one strong safety. Labelling both outside safeties SS without comment reads as a
  typo. Either explain it in the caption or label them LS/RS.
- **"F" means two different players in one figure** (Fig 4). In the left panel F is the fullback; in
  the right panel F is a slot receiver. The legend allows both, but side by side in one figure the
  label is ambiguous. Use "FB" on the left.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

- **"Five answers matter"** (~l.1004). The section has four defensive answers (3-3-5, quarters,
  tite/mint, three-high). The fifth subsection, "The defensive coach who switched sides", is about
  Saban hiring *offensive* coordinators, which isn't a defensive answer. Either say "four" or move
  the Saban story to "Crossing over" (it fits "the spread won the argument" there).
- **Drill 3 never says why "still not touching anyone" matters.** The prompt plants the detail, and
  04-06 taught the exception (a lineman engaged at the line may stay engaged past a yard), but the
  answer never cashes it in. Add one sentence: "Had he still been blocking the man he engaged at the
  line, the NFL would allow it; he wasn't, so it's a foul."
- **"NFL coaches ran out of reasons to resist"** (~l.493, and "We'll come back to why"). The chapter
  never names the reasons. Why did the NFL run from under center until 2012? Ball security on the
  exchange, the downhill angle for the back, play-action from under center? "Players carry ideas"
  answers only half of it. Give two or three concrete reasons, one sentence each.
- **"the passing game gives up the clock"** (Why it exists callout, ~l.712). This is obvious to a
  coach, not to this reader. Add a clause: "incompletions stop the clock, so a passing team can't
  sit on a lead".
- **The zone read "receded by 2014"** (~l.1233), but Fig 9 shows shotgun runs still climbing after
  2014. The reader sees a contradiction. One sentence: "the shotgun *handoff* stayed; the
  quarterback *keep* didn't, and this flag can't tell them apart" (the data caution at ~l.1290 nearly
  says this, but it comes a page later and isn't connected).
- **Fig 9's "RPOs: Eagles win Super Bowl LII" annotation points at the 2017 dip** in shotgun runs.
  It reads as "RPOs made shotgun running fall". Either move the leader to 2018 or explain the dip.
- **Wing-T to NFL "2017, 67 years"** (Fig 1). Calling the jet-motion era the Wing-T's NFL arrival is
  the chapter's biggest interpretive stretch, and the text gives it one clause ("its *logic* did").
  A sentence on *what* the 49ers or Rams ran that is Wing-T series football (for example, jet sweep,
  then the same motion with a handoff the other way, then a boot off it) would earn the diamond.
- **Why the NFL took only the personnel from three-high** (~l.1181). It's asserted, not explained.
  Better NFL quarterbacks punishing the underneath zones? NFL offensive lines that make a five-man
  box costly? Give one reason.
- **"three-yard rule … throw deeper"** (Table 2). How deep three yards of leeway lets you throw is
  only answered in Drill 3. A forward pointer from the table row would help.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **Fig 5 (zone read with the bubble attached) is the most important figure, and it misleads.**
  With R at w=-1.6 just left of Q, the dark "give" path is drawn from R's spot, but it disappears
  behind Q's marker, so it appears to start at **Q** and run right. The green "keep" path starts at
  Q and passes immediately through **R's** spot, so it appears to start at **R** and run left. My
  first decode was "the back keeps left, the quarterback runs right", which is backwards. Other
  problems in the same figure:
  - The keep arrow then runs straight up through the ringed 5-technique.
  - Two greens (the light read line and the dark keep path) cross each other at the end.
  - The caption says the quarterback reads the end first "(dashed green line)", but the only light
  green line runs from Q *through* the end to the nickel, so it isn't clear which read it shows.
  - The bubble is a tiny backward hook from H that is hard to see.

  Fix: separate R and Q (R 2+ yards to the side, or offset in depth), and start the paths from the
  markers' edges. Draw two distinct read lines (Q to end, labelled 1; Q to nickel, labelled 2).
  Bend the keep around the end's crash point, and lengthen H's bubble.
- **R and Q markers touch** in Fig 3 (spread panel), Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 12 as well. They read as
  one blob, "RQ".
- **Fig 3, spread-option panel.** The "2" key sits about half a yard from the top edge of the window
  (convention c), and the bubble is again a short stub that is hard to see as a "third threat".
- **Fig 2 (wishbone).** The "pitch" label box butts against the SS square. Otherwise excellent, and
  the 1-2-3-4 legend box is a model.
- **Fig 6 (frame strip).** At print size the markers are about 2 mm, and the letters inside them are
  unreadable. The rings carry the story, so it still works, but frame 3 of the top row ("Q runs")
  only works if you can find the green ring. Consider a larger lateral zoom (the right side of the
  window is empty), or two rows of two.
- **Page 10 is two-thirds blank**, because Fig 6 is pushed to the next page. This is a layout
  issue, not a reader one, but it looks unfinished.
- **Fig 7 (3-3-5).** The figure works. The caption is accurate but long. Put "what to notice"
  first: "Same picture, different five rushers."
- **Fig 8 (three-high).** The Will and Sam are drawn at w=±5.2, *inside* the slots (H/F at ±8),
  yet the caption and Drill 1 say they are "walked out over the slots". This feeds the Drill 1
  problem below. In the pass panel, the brown arc to the back's flat is the "settle for a short
  throw", but nothing on the figure says so. A small "check-down" label would help.
- **Fig 10 (Drill 1).** The two "SS" squares sit on the yellow line-to-gain, which collides with
  them. The hash ticks drawn at college width are faint. Since the drill is partly "notice it's
  college", label one ("college hash").
- **Fig 12 (Drill 3).** The sideline isn't marked as a sideline: the catch arrow just meets the
  window edge, so "caught with one foot in bounds" points at nothing visible. Draw or label the
  sideline. The defensive linemen's squares sit inside the "NFL limit: 1 yard" strip and make it
  hard to read. The throw arc passes over the right 5-technique.
- **Fig 1 (timeline).** It works. The "never as a system" and "college only" italics are clear.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Length.** About 9,800 words of prose, captions and tables, against a 6,500-word spec. Several
  captions run past 120 words (Figs 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8) and re-narrate the body text.
- **The three-yard ineligible rule is explained five times**: §3 (~l.403), the RPO paragraph
  (~l.995), Table 2, the rules discussion (~l.1326) and Drill 3. Keep §3 and Drill 3, and cut the
  rest to back-references.
- **The college clock stopping on first downs is explained four times** (~l.420, ~l.978, Table 2,
  ~l.1332).
- **The hash-mark numbers appear three times** (~l.386, Table 2, ~l.1320). **Picture boards and
  helmet radios appear twice** (Tempo, Helmet communication).
- **"Ideas cross in people"** is stated as the people test, at BYU, at the Air Raid ("again,
  people"), in "Players carry ideas" and in the Takeaways. That's on-theme, but by the fourth time
  it reads as a refrain. Cut the Air Raid "again".
- **"How to read the diagrams"** packs about 20 label conventions into one paragraph. Split it into
  offense / defense / line styles as a short list, or give each figure its own small legend.

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? Drills attempted before opening answers

- *Four forces*: **yes**, cleanly.
- *Trace the lineages and "point to their DNA in an NFL play"*: **half**. I can trace the lineages.
  But no NFL play is ever walked through: every film room is a college game, and the NFL appears only
  as names in Table 1. Add one NFL film room (2012 Washington's pistol zone read with a bubble, or
  the Eagles' RPOs in Super Bowl LII) and point at the inherited pieces.
- *Defensive answers and the problem each solves*: **yes** for the 3-3-5 and three-high, **mostly**
  for tite/mint (linked out, no figure; that's fine), and **weakly** for pattern matching, which is
  named but not shown.
- *When and how each crossed*: **yes** (Table 1 is excellent).
- *Read a college broadcast with the rules in mind and predict how each one changes what teams do*:
  **yes** for the hash marks, one foot, three yards, DPI and the clock. Overtime, targeting and the
  ball-carrier-down rule get a table row but no "so watch for…".

**Drill 1 (count the Saturday box).** My attempt: the prompt says three down linemen, a Mike, and
"the Will and Sam walked out over the slots", so I counted **4 in the box against 5 linemen** and
predicted a zone read or RPO. The defense is counting on a safety coming down. The answer says
**five** ("the Will is right on its edge over the short-side slot"). The prompt and the answer
describe the Will differently, and the figure draws him inside H, at the box's edge. I got the
prediction right but the count "wrong" because of the text. Fix the prompt ("the Will is cheated
in toward the box on the short side; the Sam is out over the slot"). Also, "five in the box against
five blockers … a blocker or a read for every man, with the running back as the extra runner"
muddles the arithmetic: five linemen plus the read is *six* hats for five men. Say it as "five
blockers plus the read against five: the offense is plus-one."

**Drill 2 (the wishbone count).** My attempt: #1 = the 5, #2 = the Will, #3 = the SS, #4 = the CB.
The 5 squeezes, so the quarterback pulls; the Will takes the pitch, so the quarterback keeps. **The
quarterback carries.** That's correct, but it's too easy: the on-figure labels "squeezes the dive"
and "takes the pitch man" do the reasoning, and the numbering was given in Fig 2's legend. Remove
the labels (the prompt already says it). Or make it harder: the 5 squeezes, but the SS has been
filling the alley outside the Will. Who's unblocked now, and does the lead halfback's block change?

**Drill 3 (one play, two rulebooks).** My attempt: in college, legal (2½ < 3, the pass crosses
the line, one foot counts), a 17-yard gain. In the NFL, illegal man downfield, 5 yards, and with
one foot it isn't a catch anyway. **Correct**, from Table 2. A good drill. As noted in §2, the
"not touching anyone" detail is planted and never paid off.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. **The hook never closes.** The opening NFL version is "a little quicker, the line looks a little
   different". Tell me *why* in one closing sentence (the one-yard rule keeps NFL linemen at home, so
   the throw comes out before they climb). Drill 3 nearly says it; tie it back to the opening scene.
2. **Why did the NFL refuse to run from the shotgun for so long?** The data show 2-14% of runs from
   1999 to 2011. What did NFL coaches believe?
3. **What is crossing right now?** The chapter ends its NFL story around 2019 and defers to 11-05.
   One forward-looking sentence (what 2024-26 NFL offenses or defenses are borrowing from Saturday)
   would make the "laboratory" feel live.
4. **The run-and-shoot's college roots** (Mouse Davis at Portland State) are absent, although it's a
   textbook small-school equalizer that crossed into the NFL, and 11-02 tells only its NFL side.
5. **The NFL has used "spread to pass" for decades** (the 2007 Patriots, and the passing line in
   Fig 9). Is the NFL's spread mostly an NFL invention, then, with only the *run* side imported? The
   text half-says this. One explicit sentence would sharpen the chapter's thesis.
6. **Why the 3-3-5 stack, so dominant at West Virginia, failed to stick even in college** outside
   spread-heavy conferences. It's touched on ("struggled against tight ends"), but who beat it, and
   how?
7. **Small fact to double-check:** "the Air Raid with Mahomes in 2017 and 2018". Mahomes sat behind
   Alex Smith for most of 2017 and started in 2018.
