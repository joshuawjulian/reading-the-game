# 09-04 Officiating, Replay, and the Rules of the Ball: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher who has never played. Has read Parts 1-8 and 09-01 to 09-03 in order,
so knows the spot, forward progress and chains (01-02), eligible receivers (01-03), the game-management
staff (01-04), pre-snap fouls (01-05), throwaways and the tackle box in brief (04-07), muffs and touchbacks
(09-01/09-02), and penalty enforcement plus the 2019 PI experiment, replay assist's 2025 list and the sky
judge (09-03). Read from the PDF (`pdfs/09-04-officiating-replay-and-rules-of-the-ball.pdf`, 26 pages,
build of 2026-10-08 03:06). Figures checked at 60 dpi, with pages 5, 12, 13, 16-18, 20, 22 and 24 at 130 dpi.

Overall: the content is rich, and the parts on the rules of the ball (down or fumble, fumble out of
bounds, forward or backward, grounding) are clear. The timeline figure (Fig 6) and the three-fumble panel
(Fig 7) are the best teaching pictures in the chapter. The main problems:

- **Length.** The prose is about 9,700 words against a 4,500 target.
- **The catch section contradicts itself** on the ground.
- **The replay flowchart contradicts the text** on replay assist.
- **A fact in this chapter conflicts with 09-03.**
- **Repetition.** Several stories are told two or three times.
- **The drills test only three of the six "You'll be able to" items.**

## 2. Leaps: a missing or wrong "why"

1. **The catch section contradicts itself on the ground. This is the biggest problem in the chapter.**
   - The rule text says: "the ground can't help him: if he goes to the ground before he has finished step
     3, he must keep control when he lands."
   - The history section then says the 2018 version "**deleted the going-to-the-ground clause**".
   - The misconception box adds: "The ball is allowed to touch the ground during a catch".

   To a beginner, the deleted clause looks like it is still there. The real difference is this. Under the
   old rule, going to the ground **cancelled** any steps or reaching he had done. Under the new rule, a
   football act completed *before* the fall finishes the catch, and only a receiver who falls *before*
   making one still has to survive the ground. The chapter needs that sentence, said plainly, right
   after the Bryant bullet. Without it, the Bryant explanation ("the reach is an act common to the game,
   which finishes the catch before the ball touches down") looks like special pleading.

2. **Calvin Johnson under the 2018 rule?** He is listed as one of the three plays that "made it
   untenable", but only Bryant and James are said to count now. Would Johnson's catch count today? I
   can't work it out, and that is the natural test of whether I learned the rule.

3. **Jesse James "went to a knee at the 1" and kept going.** Down by contact isn't taught until the next
   section, so on first read I wondered why the play wasn't over. Add "untouched, so not down (more on
   that below)", or move "Down, progress and the whistle" before "The catch".

4. **The flowchart (Fig 3) contradicts the text on replay assist.**
   - The chart sends a play to replay assist only if it is *not* a reviewable ruling (catch, down, in or
     out of bounds, the spot, recovery).
   - The text says replay assist fixes exactly those things: "the ball was actually spotted a yard short,
     the runner's knee was down before the ball came out, the receiver's second foot landed out of
     bounds".

   This raises a question the chapter never answers: if the booth fixes a clearly wrong spot for free,
   why would a coach ever spend a challenge on a spot? The answer is presumably that replay assist only
   covers what is *immediately* clear, before the next snap, and anything that needs a long look goes to
   a challenge. Say so, and redraw the chart so that replay assist runs alongside the first two doors
   rather than sitting below them.

5. **"Stands" vs "confirmed": does it matter?** The text says "the distinction matters for your
   predictions". It never says whether it matters for *anything else*: the outcome, the coach's timeout,
   statistics. The footnote then says nflverse doesn't even separate the two. Either explain why the
   referee bothers to distinguish them, or say plainly that the play's result is the same.

6. **"Free timeouts" (1999 bullet).** "So coaches couldn't use challenges as free timeouts when the clock
   matters most." A failed challenge costs a timeout, so how is it free? The missing piece is that a
   review stops the clock, and a *successful* challenge costs nothing. Add that clause. It also sets up
   10-04.

7. **Why a dropped backward pass stays live.** The "why" offered is "otherwise the backward pass would
   be a free way to move the ball sideways with no risk". But an incomplete forward pass is also a
   no-risk way to move the ball, so the argument doesn't convince. A better frame: a backward pass is
   treated like a handoff through the air, so the ball stays live; the *forward* pass is the special
   case, with its own one-per-play limit and the incompletion as its price.

8. **Reversal rates in Fig 4.** "Ruled out of bounds 89% reversed" and "Ruled in bounds 68%" read as
   "officials are usually wrong". The caption explains the selection effect for booth reviews only.
   Coaches' challenges are selected too: coaches challenge only when they're fairly sure. One sentence
   would stop a beginner drawing the wrong conclusion.

9. **"Most of what makes a game flag-heavy is the game: the teams ..., the noise, the weather, the
   number of plays."** This is asserted with no data, right after a careful data section. Either show
   one number (for example, the team-level year-to-year correlation for comparison) or soften it.

10. **Fig 6 says "dead at the spot of the knee".** The ball is spotted where the *ball* was when the knee
    touched (forward progress), not where the knee was. A beginner will take the wording literally.

11. **The chapter never answers its own opener.** It opens with three questions: "Who decided that? Why
    couldn't the coach challenge? What did 'control with both feet down' have to do with it?" Nowhere
    does it come back to them. A three-sentence payoff after Door 2 (or in the takeaways) would close the
    loop.

12. **Consistency with 09-03 (a factual conflict; flag for fact-check).** 09-03 (Debates, "Replay for
    judgment calls") says replay assist may overturn the 2025 list and that "**roughing the passer and
    pass interference are not on the list**". This chapter says replay assist could wipe out "a
    roughing-the-passer flag" from 2024, along with grounding and hits out of bounds. A reader of both
    chapters gets two different answers. FACTS-current §6 lists only the 2025 five. The spec also says
    replay assist was "introduced 2023, verify"; this chapter says 2021. One of the two chapters has to
    change, and the 2021 start and the 2024 list need verifying against operations.nfl.com.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. **A fumble that stays in the end zone.** What if the offense falls on it there (a touchdown?), or the
   defense does (a touchback?). And what about a ball fumbled backward out of the offense's *own* end
   zone (a safety?). The chapter covers only out-of-bounds outcomes, so I can't finish the logic of "the
   end zone belongs to the defending team".
2. **What happens to the clock after a review** in the final two minutes, including the 10-second
   runoff. This is exactly what the booth controls, and 10-04 builds on it.
3. **Where the ball goes, and whether the clock runs,** when an incompletion is reversed to a catch.
4. **The simultaneous catch:** both players control it, and the tie goes to the offense. This is a
   classic fan argument and a natural part of "the catch".
5. **Who the officials are.** Full-time or part-time? How are crews built, graded and picked for the
   playoffs? The crew-data section assumes I know a crew stays together for a season. It also shows a
   "Shawn Hochuli" a few pages after Ed Hochuli without saying how they're related (son).
6. **Who asks for a measurement,** and can a coach request one?
7. **Which other leagues have a sky judge.** "Other leagues have tried versions of it" invites the
   question. Name one (the UFL or the CFL).
8. **An illegal forward pass:** what happens when a pass is thrown from beyond the line, or a second
   forward pass is thrown? The line judge watches for it, but the foul is never priced.
9. **The inadvertent whistle:** what happens to a live ball when an official blows the whistle by
   mistake? It is the obvious follow-up to the 2008 story.
