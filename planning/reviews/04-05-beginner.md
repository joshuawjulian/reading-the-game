# 04-05 Play-Action, Bootlegs, and Screens: beginner-reader review

Reader persona: casual NFL viewer who has never played. Has read 01-01 to 04-04 in order, so
already knows personnel and formations, Sam/Mike/Will and the nickel, technique numbers, gaps,
outside zone and the unblocked backside end (03-02, which already drew a naked boot), the
waggle (03-05), pass protection, max protect, the launch point, hot routes (04-02), now and
bubble screens (04-03), and flood, yankee, crossers and progressions (04-04).
Source: `pdfs/04-05-play-action-boots-screens.pdf` (24 pp., built 16:46 from the current .qmd),
rasterized at 60 dpi, with figure pages (7-18, 20-24) re-rasterized at 100-120 dpi. I read every
page and looked at every figure.

Overall: a strong, persuasive chapter. The linebacker's dilemma, Fig 1 (dropback vs play-action
at the same instant), the "establish the run" data section, the naked-boot bet (Fig 5), swing
vs screen (Fig 9) and the screens-vs-rushers line chart (Fig 13) are excellent, and I came away
able to do most of the objectives. The main problems are: (a) the chapter says the defensive line
"is not fooled at all", but the bootleg depends on a defensive end who is fooled; (b) after the
slow screen made a big point of the one-yard rule, the tunnel, jailbreak and middle screens seem
to break it, and the chapter never says how they don't; (c) three frame-strip or plate details
contradict their text; (d) length: about 9,000 words of prose against a 5,000-word target, with
several things explained two or three times; (e) the Predict drills are mostly re-runs of the
chapter's own plates.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **"The defensive line is not fooled" vs. the bootleg's whole bet.** On p. 3 the chapter says:
   "Second, **the defensive line is not fooled for long** … he feels pass protection the instant the
   blocker stops moving forward." The *Why it exists* box says "the defensive linemen are not fooled
   at all". Five pages later, the bootleg and the naked boot depend entirely on the **backside end, a
   defensive lineman**, chasing the fake. This was the biggest contradiction I hit. The missing
   sentence is that the backside end is *unblocked*, so he has no blocker to feel; he reads the
   backfield like a linebacker does, and that is exactly why he can be fooled. Put it in both places.
2. **The tunnel, jailbreak and middle screens vs. the one-yard rule.** The chapter teaches the rule
   three times: linemen "release *flat* … and turn upfield to block only once the ball is in the
   air." Then:
   - the jailbreak linemen "sprint out at the snap" and Fig 10 draws their blocks ending 3-4 yards
     *past* the line;
   - the tunnel's RT and RG "release outward" and end about 2-3 yards upfield;
   - the middle screen's guards and center "release straight upfield at the linebackers" (Fig 11
     shows about 3.5 yards).

   Is that legal? I assume the ends of those block lines are where the blocks happen *after* the
   throw, but nothing says so, and on the middle screen "straight upfield" reads like a foul. Add one
   sentence to each: "they get no more than a yard deep until the ball is out, which on a jailbreak
   is about a second", or similar.
3. **What actually separates a tunnel screen from a jailbreak?** The text says the jailbreak is
   "quick, thrown in about a second" but never gives the tunnel's timing. Do the tunnel linemen
   pass-set first, or leave at the snap? Then: "Some teams call the tunnel screen a jailbreak." I
   finished the section unable to say which features define which. A two-row mini-table would fix
   it: who catches it, where he catches it, do the linemen sell pass first, and when the ball comes
   out.
4. **Under center vs shotgun: which fake is "better"?** p. 5 says the shotgun fake "looks less like a
   run, and the defense knows it." Then Table 1 shows the shotgun fake's *boost* was bigger (+0.09 vs
   +0.05). The paragraph that follows hedges in both directions, and the Takeaways close with
   "Under-center play-action … was the most productive dropback." I can't reconcile "looks less like
   a run" with "adds more". If the answer is that shotgun non-fake dropbacks include all the obvious
   passing downs, which drags their baseline down, say so in one plain sentence. Otherwise the reader
   leaves with "shotgun fakes are worse" when the table says the opposite.
5. **The opener and Predict 1 argue for "establishing the run" right after the chapter debunks
   it.** The opener has "The offense has run the ball on six of its last eight snaps"; Predict 1
   says "has run the ball six times in its last eight plays … has stopped the last three runs". Both
   lean on run history, yet p. 3-4 says run history barely matters (Hermsmeyer: linebackers bite as
   hard on the twelfth fake as the first). The text does say that run *frequency* matters a little
   (+0.24), and the opener's point is about the linebackers' habit, but the reader needs that link
   spelled out. Otherwise Predict 1 reads like the very announcer logic the chapter just knocked
   down.
6. **Bootleg read: who is "the deep defender"?** "the over route if the deep defender has gone with
   the clear-out". In Fig 4, is that the boot-side corner or the free safety? Name him, and ring him
   in frame 3.
7. **Predict 2's answer assumes something it never states.** "An end who stays home on the boot is
   not chasing the outside zone run from behind either." That is true only if the end can't tell
   boot from run and so plays every snap the same way. Say that is the reason. Without it, the
   reader asks: why wouldn't he chase the real run and wait on the fake?
8. **The shot play: "the deepest defender that matters is a safety … a deep throw needs only that
   [a step or two]."** Why only two steps? Give the arithmetic once: the safety starts at 13 yards,
   the post breaks at 20, a step forward plus his turn costs about a yard and a half of cushion, and
   that's the window.
9. **"Six or seven men in coverage" (shot play gives up).** Fig 7 has eight in or near the box,
   leaving three deep. Where do six or seven in coverage come from? Probably linebackers dropping
   after the fake. Say so, or the number contradicts the picture.
10. **Sacks.** The text claims play-action leaves the quarterback "a little more exposed". The code
    computes `SACK` (sack rate with and without a fake) but the number never appears in the text. It
    is exactly the evidence that claim needs. Use it, or soften the claim.
11. **"The gap was there in every one of the four seasons" (p. 3).** `by_season` is computed but
    never shown. One short four-row table or inline list would let me see it.

## 5. Can I do each "You'll be able to…" item?

1. *Explain why a run fake moves linebackers even against a team that can't run, and what the data
   says about "establish the run".* **Yes.** This was the clearest part of the chapter. It's dented
   only by the contradiction in §2.1.
2. *Tell under-center from shotgun play-action, and a boot from a naked boot, keeper, leak and shot
   play, from the first two seconds.* **Partly.**
   - Under center vs shotgun, and boot vs naked boot (look for a blocker on the boot side): yes.
   - Keeper: only after the fact, which is fine.
   - Shot play and leak: no. Nothing tells me what to look for in the first two seconds. The *Watch
     for it* box has no shot-play cue (count the receivers who release: only two go out and eight
     stay in) and no leak cue (the tight end on the fake side blocks, then releases late). Add both.
3. *Draw the slow, tunnel, jailbreak, TE and middle screens, and tell any screen from a swing or
   flare.* **Mostly.** Slow, TE and middle screens, and swing vs screen: yes; the "linemen leave
   their rushers" rule is excellent. Tunnel vs jailbreak: no (§2.3).
4. *Explain screen timing, OL release, and why screens get better with more rushers.* **Yes**, with
   the one-yard-rule confusion for the quick screens (§2.2).
5. *Read a team's play-action rate and efficiency from charting data, and say who leans on deception
   most.* **Yes.** Fig 14 is easy to read. One quibble: "Some good offenses barely fake" sits beside a
   bottom five that includes NYJ, NO and CIN, which weren't good, so name the good ones (KC, TB).

**Predict-the-play drills: my attempts before opening the answers**

- **P2 (the end who won't bite):** I said "he's taken away the naked boot, so call a protected boot,

- **P3 (name it after the snap):** I said "slow screen left; they wait because of the one-yard rule".

## 6. What I still wonder: gaps this chapter should answer

1. **How does a defense fight back?** 03-02 had "how defenses fight zone"; this chapter has no
   matching "how defenses fight play-action, boots and screens" section. I wanted at least:
   - linebackers taught to read *through* the guard to the backfield, or to give ground on certain
     downs;
   - an end assigned to "boot" or "contain" (stay home on every outside-zone look);
   - a defensive lineman peeling off to take the screen back when he feels the tackle release;
   - "rally" or "screen" calls on third-and-long.

   Forward links to 05-04 and 05-05 are fine, but give one paragraph here. The chapter is otherwise
   one-sided.
2. **When the screen is sniffed out, what does the QB do?** "turns into a loss, or worse". Can he
   throw it into the ground? (Is that intentional grounding when the target is behind the line?) One
   sentence.
3. **Does play-action raise sacks?** The code has the number (§2.10).
4. **Where do I actually see the linebackers' feet on a broadcast?** The *Watch for it* box says
   "watch the linebackers' feet, not the ball". On the standard TV angle, are they in frame at the
   snap? A note on using the replay or the end-zone angle would make the drill doable.
5. **What is the "run look"?** The *Watch for it* box says "Two tight ends, a back seven yards
   deep…", while the Rams film room says the Rams faked from **11 personnel**, tight-split
   formations. Is the run look about personnel, or about the splits and alignment? Reconcile in one
   line.
6. **How does FTN decide what counts as "play-action" or a "screen"?** Does a shotgun ride count?
   Does a jet-sweep fake? It matters for reading Fig 14, and one footnote line would cover it.
7. **Is a screen to the back "a pass" in the stats** (completion %, passing yards)? This is curiosity
   rather than a requirement, but beginners ask it.
