## 2. Leaps (a step whose "why" is missing or assumed)

1. **Who blocks the play-side end on outside zone? The chapter says two things.**
   - Reach-and-overtake (p.6) and Fig 4/5: the Y gets his hands on the 5-technique and climbs; the RT
     overtakes and ends up with the end.
   - The runner's read (p.10): "If **the tight end** gets his head across the end and pins him inside,
     the edge is open: bounce."
   - In the Fig 5 caption the RT has the end, and the result is bang.
   So when does the end ever get pinned for a bounce, and by whom? I can't reconcile these. I also can't
   picture any bounce on outside zone against this front.
2. **The rules have no edge cases, and the drills depend on them.**
   - The last man on the line (Y vs a 9-technique, U vs a backside 9) has no neighbor on the side where
     the defender is shaded. p.9 says Y "has to reach that man by himself" and U "hinges". Neither
     follows from the three rules as written.
   - Two uncovered linemen side by side (Drill 2: with the end moved out to a 9, the RT has nobody on him
     or in his play-side gap, and neither does the RG). The rules say an uncovered man helps the
     "covered man on his backside", but his backside neighbor is also uncovered. Does he climb straight
     away? I couldn't draw Drill 2's blocking, so I couldn't really reason about the crease.
3. **Which lineman of a double team climbs?** Rule 3 says "one of the two linemen comes off the double
   team and climbs" when the linebacker commits. Which one, and how does he decide? Every figure has the
   uncovered man climbing on inside zone. Is that the rule, or does it depend on where the linebacker
   goes?
4. **The chapter's founding claim is never shown.** The opening "why" is that zone exists because
   defenders slant, stunt and blitz, and rules pick up whoever arrives. No diagram shows a slant or a
   linebacker blitz being picked up by zone rules, and that is the one picture that would make the
   chapter's thesis concrete. Fig 11 shows movement *beating* zone, which is the opposite case.
5. **Inside-zone key, without the reason.** "He reads the first defensive lineman play-side of the
   center" (p.10). Why that man, and not the linebacker or the end? What does he tell the back that
   nobody else does?
6. **"If a linebacker fills the play-side gap early … the back cuts behind him"** (p.4). Behind whom,
   and in which direction? I couldn't turn this sentence into a picture.
7. **Stretch against a tight end.** "When the defensive end lines up tight, inside the tight end, a team
   can reach him" (p.8). By the rules, an end inside the Y is shaded to the Y's *backside*, so he belongs
   to the RT. The RT then has to reach a man who is already play-side of him, which the chapter just
   called "close to impossible" alone. So why is this the front that *invites* stretch?
8. **Arc vs boot vs split zone after the snap.** All three send a tight end at the backside end. The
   chapter separates split zone (hits him) from boot (slips past into the flat), but arc (loops around
   him to the next man) never gets added to that "how to tell" list. On a broadcast, how do I tell
   arc from boot?
9. **The bash paragraph (p.14)** compresses the zone read, the defense's squeeze-and-scrape counter to
   it, and bash's counter to that into four sentences, with no diagram of the middle step. Fig 10's
   bash panel shows only the bash itself. I came away remembering "back goes the other way" and nothing
   about why it works.
10. **Data proxy choice.** The chapter cites ESPN: the 49ers ran zone on 55% of runs and LaFleur's Packers
    on 70.3%. Then it uses the 49ers as "the obvious" zone proxy. Why not the team that ran more zone?
    It also says the 49ers ranked 9th, which doesn't support a scheme story, and that tension isn't
    discussed.
11. **Negative EPA isn't explained until it's too late.** Every value on the chart is below zero, and the
    49ers' runs come out at about −0.04. My first reaction was "the famous run team has a bad run game?"
    The explanation (the average carry loses expected value) comes at the end of the fourth paragraph.
    It needs to come before the chart.
12. **Why didn't the U's cut-off stop the end in Drill 3?** The drill says the backside end "crashed flat
    down the line … chased the back from behind" twice. But outside zone assigns the U to cut him off
    (Fig 4, Fig 12 left). Was the U beaten, or is the end supposed to be free? The premise contradicts
    the assignment I just learned.
13. **Fig 1 vs later figures: the unblocked end.** Fig 1 and the text make a point of the left 5 being "not
    on anyone's list". Then in every two-tight-end figure the U blocks him, and nobody says that the
    second tight end exists *to* handle him. That would also explain the legend's claim that
    "two-tight-end sets are where zone teams live".

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. What does zone look like when a defender **does** slant or a linebacker blitzes at the snap? That's
   the chapter's own reason for existing, and it's never drawn.
2. Who picks inside vs outside zone on a given snap? Can the QB or center **check** between them, or out
   of zone, at the line (01-05 taught me checks)? What happens when the defense shows 8 in the box?
3. How deep does the back line up for inside vs outside zone, and is depth a pre-snap tell?
4. On a broadcast, where do I look in the first half-second: the line's helmets, the back's feet, or the
   backside end? I can't watch all three. The Watch-for-it box lists 9 things to check per snap.
5. How common is zone in 2025? The only league-wide number is ESPN's from 2019. Is it still most runs,
   or has the gap/duo comeback that 03-03 is about changed that?
6. Do zone teams run to the tight-end side or away from it more often, and why?
7. What does "reach" look like physically (footwork, hands)? I understand who reaches whom, but not why
   it's so hard that it takes two men.
8. Is inside zone the same play as the "HB Dive" in Madden? A one-line Madden bridge for inside zone
   would help. The only bridge box is about stretch.
9. What actually happened on the opening play's 12-yard run? The cutback section says "here is the play
   from the chapter's opening", but Fig 8 isn't explicitly the hook play: there's no second-and-7, and no
   hole "near the center". Close the loop on the hook in one sentence.
