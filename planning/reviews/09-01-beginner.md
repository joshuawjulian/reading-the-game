# Beginner review: 09-01 Field Goals, Extra Points, and the Punting Game

Reviewer role-play: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read Parts 1–8 in curriculum
order. Eligibility (01-03), field position (01-02), zone and man coverage, seal blocks and overloads
are all known. I read `pdfs/09-01-kicking-and-punting.pdf` (23 pages) start to finish and looked at
every figure at 100 dpi.

Overall: a strong chapter that delivers on the hook. The add-18 arithmetic, the operation-time race,
the vice and gunner economics, the who-touched-it-first flowchart and the fakes puzzle all land. Two
problems stand out. First, **the formation names (tight, spread, shield) contradict each other**, so I
finished not knowing what the NFL actually lines up in. Second, **a few rules statements contradict
each other or the chapter's own formulas**: downing versus "the ball stays live", and net average
arithmetic. There is also one dense data paragraph that loses me.

## (1) Terms used before they are explained, or never explained

- **Shield vs spread (the big one).** Fig 6's caption calls the spread punt one of "Two older punt
  formations". The text under it says "The NFL's standard is the spread family". The next
  paragraph says "The shield punt (also called the spread punt; the names are used loosely and
  overlap)". The glossary makes "Spread punt" an alias of "Shield punt". Predict 2 then says "its
  usual spread punt" and draws exactly Fig 6's "older" formation. So is spread old or standard? Is
  it the same as shield or not? What I need is one sentence: "In the NFL today most teams line up
  in X. It differs from Fig 6 in Y (where the wings and PP stand)." On the visible difference, the
  text says the NFL shield is "the two wings and the personal protector", but Fig 6's spread *also*
  has two wings and a PP. The distinguishing fact (wings a yard off the line vs wings ~9 yards
  deep beside the PP) is never stated in words.
- **"within a yard of the line"** (gunner-release rule): the shield players line up 9+ yards behind
  the line, so "stay within a yard of the line" reads as nonsense until you see that it means
  "may not go more than a yard *past* the line". Reword.
- **Who is eligible on a kick formation.** Punt safe "covers every eligible receiver". The Seahawks
  fake says Gilliam "had lined up as an eligible receiver". "Every punt-team formation is an
  offensive formation with eligible receivers." I know the 01-03 rules, but I can't apply them to
  a punt or field-goal unit. Which players are eligible: the gunners, wings, PP and punter? How does
  a No. 79 tackle become eligible (reporting)? One sentence plus a link to 01-03 would fix this. Fig 11
  (punt safe) draws dotted man-coverage lines without saying which blue players they are on.
- **"C", "S", "L" markers in Fig 11 (punt safe)** are never decoded. I guess corner, safety and
  linebacker, but neither the caption nor the text says so.
- **"stack"**: "the gunner trying to swim past or 'stack' one vice". Never explained here, and I
  don't recall it from earlier.
- **"variance"**: "take the one with less variance". This is a stats word in a Level-2 chapter. Say
  "the safer, more predictable one".
- **"waved off"** (Fig 16 / Predict 3): "The returner has waved off the ball". The chapter taught
  me that waving means a *fair catch*. Is "waved off" a fair-catch signal or a wave to teammates?
  It matters for the answer, so say "has decided to let it go and is waving teammates away".
- **"(A related rule from 10-04: ...)"** (kick-catch interference paragraph): a bare chapter ID
  for a chapter I haven't read. Use the title, or drop the attribution.
- **"Downed" box in Fig 12**: "a kicker catches or holds it". "Kicker" reads as *the* kicker.
  Write "a kicking-team player".
- **"block unit"** appears in the objectives and the Fig 4 caption ("a ten-man block unit") before
  it is introduced on p7. This is minor and guessable.
- **"competition committee", "AFL"**: unglossed. Minor.

## (2) Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **First touching "stays live", yet downing "stops play".** Rule 3 says first touching leaves "the
   ball stays live", then that downing *is* first touching and "play stops". The missing link is
   that when a kicking-team player *possesses* the ball, it is dead. Without it the two sentences
   contradict each other, and the flowchart's "Downed: dead there" adds to the confusion.
2. **The net-average arithmetic doesn't match its own formula.** Definition: "gross distance minus
   return yards, minus 20 yards for every touchback". Misconception box: "A 50-yarder from the
   opponent's 45 that rolls into the end zone nets only 25." By the formula, 50 − 20 = 30. You get 25
   only if a touchback's gross is measured to the goal line (45), which the chapter never says. A
   beginner who does the math will stop here. Either state the convention or change the example.
3. **The weather/selection paragraph** (pp. 4–5) packs in about 14 percentages. Two things are
   wrong with it:
   - It quotes **55–59** numbers ("43% from 55–59 ... against 57% from 55–59 in warm") while the
     chart's last bin is **55+** (about 41% vs 51%), so the text and picture disagree.
   - The core logic ("fewer long kicks attempted in the cold ⇒ the chart *understates* the cost")
     is stated but not shown. A two-line example would make it click: in the cold, coaches only
     let the kicker try the 55-yarder when the wind is at his back.
4. **"Selection" means two different things two pages apart.** In "Kickers keep getting better", a
   bullet says "Selection. There are only 32 jobs" (roster selection). Under the weather box,
   "selection" is "the most important trap in sports data" (selection bias). Rename the first
   bullet ("Competition for jobs").
5. **The holder's distance.** "move it farther back and you get a longer, safer kick that rushers
   have more time to reach." "Safer" and "more time to reach" sound contradictory. The real
   trade is that a longer snap and a longer kick give rushers more time *and* reduce accuracy,
   while closer means a lower ball at the line. Split the sentence.
6. **"Kickers said new balls flew like rocks"**, two bullets after "a cold ball feeling like a
   rock" (bad). "New balls" also reads as "the new 2025 balls". Say "unbroken-in balls felt like
   rocks, so letting teams break them in helped".
7. **Extra-point blocks went from 4 to 11 a season after 2015, with no explanation.** The snap and
   the hold are the same distance from the line, so why more blocks? Is it because defenses can now
   score 2 on a return and so rush harder, or because of the lower trajectory? I want one clause.
8. **Two-point chart rationale.** "by 8 before you score (you will need two)" switches from "after
   a touchdown" framing to "before you score" framing within one sentence, and I had to reread
   it. "Down 14 late: go for two first, so you know..." says *what* happens but not *why* it's
   better. One sentence would cover it: make it and the next TD plus a kick wins; miss it and you
   still have the next TD to go for two and tie. Deferring to 10-04 is fine, but say it's deferred.
9. **Fig 5 caption orientation.** "a block unit that overloads its left side". The overload is on
   the image's left, which is the *offense's* left and the defense's right. Say "the kicking team's
   left".
10. **Why would the NFL use a shield at all?** The text says the shield's point is that "interior
    linemen don't have to hold their blocks for long ... get into coverage". Then it says NFL interior
    linemen *can't* release before the kick. So what does the shield buy an NFL team? This follows on
    from the formation confusion in (1).
11. **"Why it exists: the holder"**: "usually the punter, who ... is already on the field". He's
    only on the field *because* he holds. Drop "already on the field" or say "is already
    dressed as a specialist and practices with the kicker daily".
12. **Hook nit:** "flags ... snapping toward him". The coach is on the sideline, so wind blowing
    toward him is a *crosswind*. Write "snapping back toward the kicker".

## (6) Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- **Blocked kicks:** who can pick up and run with a blocked punt or field goal behind the line, or
  past it? The chapter says blocks are huge swings but never gives the rule (the PAT section only
  mentions the defense returning one for 2).
- **Returning a missed field goal:** a short kick that falls into the end zone can be fielded and run
  back. I've seen it on TV ("kick six") and the chapter never mentions it. Is the returner's option
  the same as on a punt?
- **Does the six-per-side limit apply on punts?** Predict 2 shows five on one side, which made me
  ask.
- **Fair catch then muff:** if the returner signals and then drops it, can his team still advance?
  Can the kicking team recover?
- **Bad snap over the punter's head:** what are the options, and when is it a safety?
- **What does the NFL use today:** shield or spread, and why, given the release rule?
- **Two-point rate dipped to 41% in 2024.** Noise, or something real?
- **How a fair-catch kick is actually run** (held, no rush?). It's mentioned as a curiosity with no
  picture of it.
