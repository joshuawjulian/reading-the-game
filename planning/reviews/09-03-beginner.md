# 09-03 Contact Rules and Penalties as Strategy: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher, never played, has read Parts 1-8 and 09-01/09-02 in order
(so: 01-05 pre-snap fouls and free plays, 03-01/03-02 blocking vocabulary and cut blocks, 04-02 pocket,
04-04 rub vs pick, 04-05 screens, 04-06 one-yard rule, 06-01 press and the five-yard rule, 06-02 banjo).
Read from the PDF (`pdfs/09-03-contact-rules-and-penalties.pdf`, 24 pages, build of 2026-10-08 03:03),
with figures checked at 130-150 dpi.

Overall: this chapter is strong. The "price list" frame works, the rules for a receiver by depth and
timing are clear, the DPI spot-foul argument with data (fig-deep-dpi, fig-penalty-epa, the by-down table)
is excellent, and the enforcement flowchart helps. The problems: one claim in the opening section is
wrong by the chapter's own rules; one big confusion (screens) goes unanswered; three sections largely
repeat earlier chapters; several figures fail at the one thing they're meant to show; and the drills only
check recall of things the body has just said.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or wrong "why"

1. **The opening section's example contradicts the chapter's main point.** In "The rule book as a price list":
   "A cornerback who knows that grabbing a receiver costs five yards will grab him on third-and-20,
   when five yards don't matter, and won't on third-and-2, when they decide the series." Defensive
   holding carries an **automatic first down**. On third-and-20 it wipes out a near-certain punt, which
   makes it about the *worst* time to grab. The chapter itself says so three times ("The automatic first
   down is the expensive part. On third-and-12 it erases the down"; point 1 of "Four things stand out";
   the by-down table). The example only works for a foul with no automatic first down (offside, say), or
   it should be turned around. A careful reader catches this on page 18 and stops trusting page 2.
2. **Screens versus "a receiver who blocks downfield before the throw is OPI".** The one-yard-limit
   section says "a receiver who blocks downfield before the throw is OPI too". Then fig-low-blocks
   panel 3 shows Z blocking a cornerback 5 yards downfield on a screen *before* H catches it, and 04-03
   and 04-05 taught receivers blocking on bubble and tunnel screens. Why isn't that OPI? The chapter
   needs one paragraph on passes caught at or behind the line (what the PI rule covers, and how it
   interacts with 04-05's statement that the NFL one-yard rule covers every forward pass). Without it,
   the chapter's own figure looks like a foul.
3. **Why spot-of-foul for offensive fouls on runs but previous spot on passes?** "From which spot"
   states the rule ("generally, for fouls by the offense beyond the line on running plays") with no
   reason. What happens to a hold *behind* the line on a run? This is the one enforcement idea a fan
   gets wrong most often, and it's needed to do the "Enforce a flag yourself" objective.
4. **Why a hold in your own end zone is a safety, not half the distance.** Half the distance is
   explained just before it. A reader asks right away: why doesn't the ball move halfway to the goal
   line from inside the end zone? The text says only "is a safety". Add one line: the enforcement spot
   is behind the goal line, so the rules award the safety.
5. **"A hold away from the point of attack, or on a double team the defender didn't split, is
   generally not called."** Why? Presumably because it doesn't affect the play. Say so. It's the key to
   the Madden callout that follows.
6. **NFC Championship film room:** "The Saints kicked a field goal instead of running out the clock
   from a first down inside the 10." A beginner can't see why a first down lets you run out the clock.
   Spell it out: the Rams had one timeout, so three kneel-downs or runs would have taken the clock to
   about :10 before the kick. One clause would do it.
7. **Drill 2's second reason is weaker than the answer claims.** DPI anywhere gives an untimed down
   at the spot. A foul at the 10 also gives one, from the 10. The end zone matters mostly because only
   a touchdown wins. The DPI point is "the deeper the foul, the better the untimed down." Phrase it that
   way, not as a reason specific to the end zone.
8. **The EPA chart's "point 1" seems contradicted by its own bars.** "The automatic first down is
   worth more than the yards." Yet "Illegal use of hands (hands to the face)" (5 yards plus an automatic
   first down against the defense) shows +0.49, *below* Offside (+0.68) and Neutral zone infraction
   (+0.80), which carry no automatic first down. The caption explains that these bars mix both teams'
   fouls, so the average blends offensive minus and defensive plus. That makes the bar a price of
   neither, and a reader comparing bars draws the wrong conclusion. Split them by team, or drop the
   three mixed categories.
9. **Free-play figure and 01-05's neutral zone infraction.** In fig-free-play the ringed E lines up
   right over the LT and "jumps into the neutral zone". 01-05 taught that a jump that makes a blocker
   flinch, or gives the defender a free run at the QB, is a neutral zone infraction: whistle, no play.
   Why is this one a free play? One sentence on offside versus NZI would connect the two chapters.
10. **Tush-push section:** "Pushing the runner has been legal since the NFL removed its ban in 2005."
    Only the footnote says pulling is still illegal. The body should say it, because "what exactly is
    legal?" is the obvious next question.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Five-yard history and "what the zone does" repeat 06-01 almost word for word.** 06-01's "The 5-yard
  rule" already has Blount, 1977/1978, the 2004 "Ty Law rule", and "All of the contact has to happen in
  those first 5 yards, about the first second of the route, so press is about the release ... pressed
  corners nearly always have a safety behind them ... stacked receivers ... on third-and-long many
  defenses don't press". This chapter repeats every one of those clauses ("Press is about the release.
  All of the corner's legal contact has to happen in the first five yards, about the first second..."). The
  Patriots-Colts film room is new and earns its place. The rest should be cut to one "as you saw in 06-01"
  sentence.
- **The one-yard strip repeats 04-04's "The rub, and where it becomes a pick"**, including the
  stacks/bunches explanation and a legal/illegal two-panel figure (04-04's fig-rub is nearly the same as
  fig-pick-yard). Keep only what's new here: the three defensive answers and the enforcement.
- **The free play is taught in 01-04, in 01-05 (with the Rodgers film room and a drill), and again here.**
  Here it should be a short pointer plus whatever is new (the jump versus NZI distinction above).
- **The DPI spot foul is explained five times:** the hook, the spot-foul text, the "Why it exists"
  callout, the deep-throw section, fig-dpi-enforcement and fig-dpi-movie (the same deep shot a third
  time). The movie adds little that Figures 1 and 3 haven't shown. Consider cutting it and using the
  animation budget on a hold or a pick.
- **"Third down, incomplete, offensive hold: decline"** appears in the accept/decline bullets, in the
  Watch-for-it box ("expect a decline and a punt") and as Drill 1. Three times.
- **"The automatic first down is the expensive part"** is said about four times (after Figure 1, point 1,
  under the table, the takeaways). Twice would be enough.
- The helmet-rule paragraph's nflverse-label aside ("then vanishes from the labels after 2021 ...
  categories move") is useful for the analytics reader but breaks the flow of a safety section. It fits
  better in the Go-deeper box on penalties in EPA.

## 6. What I still wonder (that this chapter should answer)

1. Why can receivers and linemen block downfield on screens without OPI or illegal man downfield? (§2.2)
2. How do officials decide the QB has "left the pocket", which turns illegal contact off? (Tackle box,
   with a link to 09-04.)
3. Why are offensive fouls on runs walked off from the spot, and what if the hold is behind the line?
4. Can a *defender* hold a blocker, for example on a stunt or twist (07-01)? The glossary entry for
   defensive holding says "or any offensive player who is not the ball carrier", but the text only
   covers receivers.
5. What happens when the same team commits two fouls on one play? (Only one is enforced, so which one?)
6. If a defensive foul happens on a touchdown, is it just lost, or can it be carried to the kickoff or the
   try? ("The offense declines and keeps the touchdown" suggests the yards vanish.)
7. Why does DPI in the end zone give the ball at the 1 rather than a touchdown? The obvious fan question
   after "a penalty should give back what the foul took away".
8. What gets a player ejected (versus fined), and who decides? The 2026 replay consult is mentioned, but
   not the standard.
9. Exactly what is still illegal about the tush push (pulling), so I can spot the foul?
10. Is roughing the passer called differently once the QB scrambles outside the pocket, as illegal
    contact is? When does he stop being a "passer"?
