# 04-06 Run-Pass Options: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL viewer who has never played, read 01-01 to 04-05 in order (incl. 03-04 Option, 04-03 Quick Game, 04-05 Play-Action/Screens), nothing after. Read the qmd start to finish and the rendered PDF (pdfs/04-06-rpos.pdf, 20 pp, rasterized at 110 dpi).

**Overall:** strong chapter. The three-play comparison (fig 1 plus table) is the best "what is X" opener in Part 4. The bubble-count figure explains the logic of pre-snap RPOs at a glance. And "the read is a block" lands. I could do most of the objectives. The problems are mostly **contradictions**: one with the chapter I read just before this one, three inside the chapter, and one reversed caption. A careful reader will catch all of them and stop trusting the text.

---

## 0. Must-fix (contradictions a careful reader will catch)

1. **The rule for passes behind the line contradicts 04-05, and contradicts this chapter's own data section.**
   - 04-05, which I read just before this, says (l.1114): "The one-yard limit on linemen downfield applies only to passes that are thrown *beyond* the line of scrimmage. On a pass caught behind the line, the linemen can be ten yards downfield … and on a screen they are." 04-05's Drill answer repeats it (l.1792).
   - 04-06 l.900-902 says: "a pass caught behind the line … doesn't trigger either rule at all. **The NFL rule has no such clause; it applies to any legal forward pass.**"
   - 04-06 l.1165 then says: "A throw behind the line lets the linemen keep run blocking for an extra beat without risk of the five-yard flag". That only makes sense if 04-05 is right.
   - l.950-952 is also muddled: "Many of the throws, the bubble and the now screen, land at or behind the line" is offered as a *consequence* of the one-yard rule. Under this chapter's own reading of the rule, that wouldn't help.
   - Two chapters in a row give opposite answers, so I can't tell whether linemen on a bubble RPO may drift downfield. Resolve it with the fact-checker and make 04-05 and 04-06 agree. If the NFL really does exempt passes behind the line, the "NFL has no such clause" sentence goes, and the NFL-vs-college difference shrinks to 1 vs 3 yards plus entire body vs any part.
2. **Fig 4 (slant RPO) caption has upper and lower reversed.** The caption says the safety coming "forward and inside to help against the run (upper red arrow)" and staying or widening "(lower red arrow)". In the rendered figure the arrow toward the line is the **lower** one, labelled "fits the run: throw". The "walls the slant: hand off" arrow is the **upper** one.
3. **The two charting sources disagree on success, but the text says they agree.**
   - FTN: RPO success rate 42.8% vs 43.5% for other plays, so RPOs are slightly *worse* (l.1177-1178).
   - PFF: RPOs "succeeded more often (52.3% against 48.1%)".
   - The text then says "Both series agree on the shape" and concludes RPOs are "**staying-on-schedule** machines". The book's own numbers don't support that conclusion. Either reconcile them (different success definitions; footnote pffusage3 hints at this, so say it in the body) or soften the claim.
4. **Who climbs to the Will: the right guard or the left guard?**
   - Glance section, l.719-720: "the right guard, who would normally have had to climb to the Will". The Will is on the *left*, backside, and the run goes right.
   - Drill 3 (l.1358-1359): "The left guard double-teamed the 3-technique … on his way to the Will".
   - Fig 6 caption: the right guard stays on the double team and climbs to the *Mike*.
   - Pick one: the LG climbs to the Will, the RG to the Mike. Fix l.719-720.
5. **Can a safety from deep get into the run fit in time? The chapter says both yes and no.**
   - Fig 8 (make the read lie): the free safety starts about 13 yards deep and "comes downhill at the snap into the backside of the box". The run "meets the safety".
   - The next bullet but one, l.1230: "Its weakness is the run itself: a safety arriving from ten yards deep arrives late."
   - Explain the difference: in fig 8 he triggers at the snap on a called rotation, while in quarters he reads first. Otherwise this reads as a contradiction.
6. **The stick RPO contradicts 04-03's stick.**
   - 04-03 defines stick as stick + flat + clear and reads the nickel: if he **widens**, the stick is open.
   - 04-06 says "Stick … puts a receiver at about six yards … and another to the flat". But fig 5 has **no flat route**: Z runs a go. And here a nickel who **widens** means *hand off*.
   - In 04-06 "widen" means the opposite of what it meant one chapter earlier. Add one sentence saying that in the RPO version the run replaces the flat route as the second option. Either draw the flat route or drop "and another to the flat".
7. **"Three choices on one snap" claims knowledge I don't have yet.** l.865-867: "They are rarer in the NFL, for two reasons you already know: … every extra beat of reading is a beat in which the linemen are run blocking further and further from the line." The one-yard rule is taught in the *next* section. Move this section after the rule, or say "for a reason the next section explains".

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

- **"window" / "mugging the window" / "sits in the window"** (l.703 strip note, l.730, glance text). "Window" as a passing-game term (the throwing lane between defenders) isn't defined anywhere I've read. "Mugging" is opaque. One gloss, please.
- **"playing *slow*"** (l.730). 03-04 taught "slow-play". Link it.
- **Air Raid / Air Raid "tags"** (l.987). The Air Raid has only been name-dropped (02-02, 02-04 link forward to 04-08). "Tags" is never defined: is a tag a route added to a play call? One clause would do.
- **Safety "rotates down" / "post-snap rotations of Disguise and Rotation"** (l.733, l.1222-1224). Rotation is 06-06, well ahead of me. The link is there but it says "it's built on the post-snap rotations of …" as if I'd read it. Gloss it in a line: "a safety who shows deep, then drops down at the snap".
- **"the quarters family of 06-05"** (l.1227). Forward reference with a glossary link but no gloss. Add "(a two-deep zone where the safeties also fit the run)".
- **"fits / fit the run" as a verb** (fig 4/5 labels "fits: throw", l.1226). "Run fit" was used before, but "fits" as a single-word label on a diagram reads like jargon. "Plays the run: throw" would be clearer.
- **"trigger" / "triggers on the run"** (stick bullet l.793, "a late trigger" l.1235). Gloss once.
- **"slant-bubble / slant-out pairs"** (l.782). These are bolded, which signals a taught term, but they have no glossary entry and no diagram. Unbold them or define them.
- **"hybrid players"** (Why-it-exists box). Fine in context. Flagging only because it isn't linked.
- **The yellow/orange line in the drill figures** (line to gain). It isn't mentioned in "How to read the diagrams". It was probably taught earlier, but add three words.

## 2. Leaps (a missing "why")

- **Why does NFL RPO blocking stay at the line instead of the offense simply throwing faster?** l.1165-1167: "a throw beyond the line has to leave so fast that it can't be far beyond it." Why can't a fast throw go far? The real reason is probably that deep routes take time to develop and the ball must be out in about a second. Say it.
- **The rule's rationale** (l.873-875: "a play the defense cannot fairly defend"). Spell out the unfairness in one more clause: linebackers would have to treat every run block as a run and could never drop, so the pass would be blocked for.
- **Why does the NFL keep 1 yard while college keeps 3?** College's tabled proposal is covered; the NFL's thinking isn't. Even "the NFL has never loosened it; the league's officiating emphasis is …" would help.
- **The three-choices play has the QB throwing on the run.** If he has crossed the line when he throws to the overhang's receiver, isn't that an illegal forward pass? (01-0x taught that passes must come from behind the line.) Say that the throw comes before he crosses, or that he's running laterally.
- **"Hit the quarterback … defenders who hit him after the throw within the rules"** (l.1238-1240). I thought hitting the QB after the throw was roughing. Why is it legal here: because he's a ball-handler at the mesh, or a runner faking? One sentence.
- **"The run is the default"** (l.562-564). Why it's the default is explained ("a hesitant throw is the worst outcome"). But the Drill 2 answer leans on "shoulders toward the run, eyes in the backfield", and neither the prompt nor the figure gives me that. A drill picture can only show steps. Say in the prompt that his shoulders turned.
- **EPA "looks great" / "looks bad"** (l.1174-1176). +0.06 EPA per RPO throw and −0.05 per handoff mean nothing to me without the comparison. What do ordinary passes and ordinary runs average? Add those two numbers. Otherwise "great" is assertion, and +0.06 doesn't sound great.
- **Defensive answers: the objective says "say what each one gives back."** "Squeeze the clock" and "Hit the quarterback" name no cost (l.1235-1240). What does slow-playing cost: the run gets a man-short fit? What does an edge pressure cost: a vacated rush lane, a quick throw behind it?

## 6. Knowledge gaps (what I still wonder)

1. **How do I tell an RPO from play-action on TV in the first half-second?** The table says play-action's line "pass-blocks, made to look like run blocking". What's the visible tell: helmets popping up versus firing forward, linemen stepping back? Add it to Watch-for-it. It's the single most useful broadcast skill this chapter could give.
2. **Can I actually see the QB's eyes on the broadcast angle?** Watch-for-it says "find the defender he's looking at". From the high sideline camera, is that realistic, or is it an all-22 / end-zone replay skill? Tie it back to 01-06.
3. **Who picks the read defender: the play call, or the QB at the line?** "He picks one defender before the snap" (Madden box) suggests the QB chooses. The glance section implies the call fixes it (the backside LB). Which is it, and can he change it?
4. **Does anyone besides the QB know which branch happened?** Do the receivers on the give branch just run their routes as decoys, and does Z still block the corner on a bubble-RPO handoff? (That would make it a run play with a WR block 5+ yards downfield. Is that legal?)
5. **How often do NFL QBs get the read "wrong", and what does a wrong read cost?** The Why-it-exists box says "a wrong read is not a disaster". Is there any data (FTN `read_thrown`?) or a film example of a bad read?
6. **Why don't NFL offenses that love the zone read (Hurts, the Ravens) dominate the RPO leaderboard?** The Ravens aren't mentioned at all, and BAL sits mid-pack in fig 7, which surprised me given their QB run game. One sentence.
7. **Why do teams like SF and LA barely use RPOs?** The answer given is "under center, wide zone". Does that cost them anything, or does play-action do the same job for them? A link back to 04-05 would close the loop.
