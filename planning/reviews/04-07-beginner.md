# Beginner-reader review: 04-07 Quarterback Play

Reviewer role: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read 01-01 through 04-06 in order.
Read from `pdfs/04-07-quarterback-play.pdf` (24 pages, rasterized at 60 and 110 dpi) plus the .qmd for links.

**Overall:** strong chapter. The clock → progression → pocket → off-script → endings → numbers arc is
the right order, and most sections answer "why". The problems are concentrated in a few spots: (a) the
word **hitch** is used for two different things; (b) the scramble-drill rules have no tie-breaker, and the
drill-3 answer contradicts the chapter's own reasoning; (c) a few data claims an analytical reader will
challenge (selection bias in the out-of-pocket numbers, "red and blue in every corner", the
anticipation case resting on a scripted sim); and (d) some figure conventions (rings, orange line, read
badges) aren't explained.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **First hitch: is it read 1 or read 2?** Step 2: "the first read is… thrown as he sets his feet **or on his first hitch**." Step 3: "If the first read is covered, he takes… a hitch… and moves his eyes to read two." Fig 2 panel 3 says "Hitch: read 2". So which read does the first hitch belong to? The rhythm the chapter is selling ("Last step, read one; hitch, read two") breaks on this point.
2. **"About one hitch."** "Each hitch is a little under half a second" (p.2). Then: "The difference is seven-tenths of a second, about one hitch" (p.5). Those two numbers contradict each other.
3. **Checkdown timing.** The checkdown is the *last* look (after reads 2 and 3), yet Fig 1 shows it leaving at the same median as "later read" (2.9 s). The explanation, "because it is the last of the quick looks", doesn't resolve it. Why isn't it later? Is it often thrown early, as a "bad checkdown" (which the misconception box warns about)? Say so.
4. **Dagger vs one-high.** 04-04 taught me dagger is "built to beat two-high coverages" with a 15–18-yard dig. Fig 2 runs dagger against a **one-high** coverage with a 14-yard dig and never says why. As a careful reader of 04-04, I stopped and wondered whether the play was a bad call.
5. **The case for anticipation is a scripted sim.** "In the simulation the early throw is caught with the nearest defender 2.6 yards away… That is the whole case for anticipation." The author scripted when the defenders react, so the result is built in. An analytically minded reader will notice. Add one real-data anchor (for example, NGS completion % by separation, or tight-window rates), or say plainly that the sim illustrates rather than proves.
6. **Anticipation throw vs timing route.** The text distinguishes them in theory, but the example (ball out "as Z breaks", on a hitch, to a dig) looks exactly like 04-01's timing route. Show what makes this one a *judgment*: what the QB saw in the Mike that told him to let it go.
7. **Out-of-pocket sack rate is selection-biased.** The misconception box: "quarterbacks were sacked on 8.3% of dropbacks once they were out of the pocket, against 6.2% in it". This is offered as evidence that leaving a good pocket causes sacks. But QBs mostly leave *because* the pocket is already failing, and the footnote notes that rollouts are mixed in. The number can't separate "leaving causes sacks" from "pressure causes both". Acknowledge the confound, or narrow the claim.
8. **Fig 10 vs Fig 8: is leaving the pocket good or bad?** Fig 10 ranks "QB scramble" first (+0.49) and "Scramble-drill throw" (+0.31) far above the checkdown. Fig 8 says off-script costs value overall (+0.011 vs +0.049). I couldn't reconcile these until I guessed that the sacks, throwaways and interceptions that happen outside the pocket are pulled out of the scramble bars. Spell that out, and say why scrambles look so good (the caption gives only "when the coverage has left open grass", a selection effect again). As written, Fig 10 tells me to scramble more.
9. **When should he run?** "QB scramble (he runs)" is the single most valuable ending in Fig 10, but the chapter never teaches the run-vs-throw decision once he's out of the pocket. Answer 3 says "run, if there is grass to the line to gain", which is the first time it comes up.
10. **Who does the scramble-drill QB look at first?** Answer 3: "He looks deep first (H, or Z if the middle is empty)". The chapter never taught a scramble look order. Its own reasoning points the other way: "a quarterback throwing on the run cannot make a 40-yard throw accurately", and in Fig 7 the ball goes to the come-back receiver. See §5.
11. **"Passing plays vary more than running plays."** The evidence offered is the SD of *per-player average* EPA (QBs 0.110 vs RBs 0.075). That's a different claim from play-level variance. Also, "59 points over a season" means little until it's converted to wins (even a rough "~30–40 points ≈ a win"), and "above average" isn't "above replacement". One sentence on each would help.
12. **Purdy: "for the charge".** "NGS… ranked his completion percentage over expected only 5th that season." Fifth in the league reads as *great*. Why is it evidence for the system-QB charge? The real point is the *gap* between the two models (1st vs 5th). Lead with that.
13. **Throwaways vs CPOE.** The chapter praises throwaways, then offers CPOE as the accuracy measure. Does a throwaway count as an incompletion against his CPOE? I suspect it does (nflverse `cp` doesn't exclude them), so a smart throwaway habit lowers CPOE. This is worth a sentence in the CPOE cautions.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **Fig 1 (clock):** the vertical grid lines and the dashed line at ~3.2 s run straight through the band labels ("Set, hitch: read 1…", "Off schedule: climb, slide, escape…"), which makes the text hard to read in print. The dashed line is never explained. Is it "end of schedule"? Label it.
- **Fig 2 (progression strip):** the numbered read badges stay at the receivers' *alignment* behind the LOS while the receivers run 10–15 yards away. In panel 2, the green "1" sits below the line while Y is far upfield, so I had to work out which receiver each number belonged to. Put the badges on, or beside, the moving receiver. Also, what does the ringed Mike in panel 3 have to do with Z, who is still on the right sideline? The caption says "about to break in behind the Mike", but the geometry isn't visible until panel 4.
- **Fig 3 (two release times):** in the *left* panel the FS is ringed and sits right on top of Z at the catch. Visually, "room to spare" looks about as tight as the right panel. The 2.6 vs 1.1 yd labels carry the whole message. Consider a separation circle or bracket, and explain what the ring means here.
- **Fig 4 (three pocket moves):** decodable, but tiny in print (three narrow panels). The "Escape" panel doesn't make clear which blue dot is the driven-back right guard. Label it.
- **Fig 8 (in vs out of pocket):** three or four blue (better-outside) dots are unlabeled. Since the takeaway is "for a few QBs it adds value", I want to know who all 11 are. Hurts sits below the diagonal even though the history paragraph names him as an off-script quarterback. That's worth a sentence.
- **Fig 11 (CPOE dumbbells):** the caption says "Two quarterbacks with the same completion percentage can be far apart once difficulty is counted", but doesn't name a pair. Name one (find two filled dots at the same x with different CPOE) so I know where to look.
- **Fig 12 (style map):** the caption says "there are blue and red dots in every corner", and the text repeats "every corner has both colors in it". **This isn't true in the figure.** The only clearly red dots (Flacco, Smith, Ward) are all in the bottom half. The top-left and top-right corners have no red, only blue or near-white. Fix the claim or the color scale. Also, Stafford is in the **top-left, "quick and deep: the rhythm shooter"** corner (2.80 s, left of the 2.85 line), but the text says only that he "sat in the top half". After being told the rhythm shooter is "the rarest type", I wanted the chapter to say that the MVP is one. Purdy "sits deep in the bottom-right corner" overstates it: his aDOT (~7.6) is just under the average line.
- **Fig 13 (drill 1):** decodable. "The quarterback… eight yards deep" is hard to verify because the grid has no yard marks. Fine.
- **Fig 15 (drill 3):** clear.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- The **"How to read the diagrams"** paragraph is a ten-line wall before the first section. Split it into a short list, or move the moving-picture conventions to the first animated figure.
- **"Climbing the pocket. Climbing the pocket means…"** (p.6): a bold lead-in followed by the bolded term reads as a stutter.
- **+0.011 vs +0.049 EPA** appears twice (misconception box p.7, Off-script value p.10). **Allen +0.31/+0.20** appears three times (Fig 8 text, Film room, takeaways), **Mahomes +0.30** twice.
- **"Style is not success / every corner has both colors"** appears in the Fig 12 caption, after the four bullets, and in the last line of the section (and it's inaccurate, see §3).
- The third-down checkdown paragraph (p.13) packs six numbers into five sentences. 24% / 43% / 58% / −0.20 / −1.79 is a table's worth. A tiny inline table would read faster.
- Film room has six boxes. Brady/Manning, Mahomes, Eli/Tyree, Allen, Burrow and Purdy are each fine on their own, but together they take three pages. The Allen box mostly repeats Fig 8. Trim it, or merge it with Mahomes as "the off-script pair".

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? Drills attempted before opening the answers

**Objectives:**
1. *Narrate a dropback*: **yes**, modulo the first-hitch confusion (§2.1).
2. *Count reads on a replay*: **mostly**. The checklist is good. But the broadcast angle rarely shows the QB's eyes live, and the chapter mentions look-offs (eyes deliberately lying) only in Connections. A one-line warning in Watch for it ("his eyes can lie: see look-off in 08-01") would save confusion.
3. *Climb vs slide vs escape*: **yes**. This is the clearest section.
4. *Predict scramble-drill routes*: **partly**. See drill 3.
5. *Throwaway / checkdown decisions*: **yes**.
6. *Profile a QB from four numbers*: **yes on the chart**. But I don't know where to look these numbers up for this Sunday's QB (NGS leaderboards? rbsdm.com?). "Why QBs dominate value": I can repeat the argument, but see §2.11.

**Drill 1 (pocket at two seconds).** My answer: ends are pushed past, so climb, and the right interior is caving, so move up *and left*, a climb plus a slide. **Matched** the answer. The prompt's choice list ("back, up, left, right, or out") makes a combined answer feel like cheating, but the answer rewards it. Fine.

**Drill 2 (third-and-9 at the 31).** My answer: throw it out of bounds past the line, legal because he's outside the tackle box; take the field goal. **Matched.** I didn't know what "spy" meant, but the picture made "no running room" clear anyway.

**Drill 3 (he breaks left).** My answer: X (deep, on his side) comes back down the left sideline. H (short) goes deep. Z (far-side curl) comes across deep. Y? Y is on a 12-yard dig that has reached the middle. Is that "short" (so he goes deep), "deep" (so he comes back), or a "crosser" (so he keeps crossing toward the QB)? **The chapter gives no precedence among the rules**, and in Fig 6 the crosser was *shallow*. I guessed "keep crossing", which matched, but it was a coin flip. Z's 12-yard curl has the same problem: deep or backside? Backside won, but no rule said it would.
*Where does he look first?* I said **X coming back**, because the chapter told me a QB on the run "cannot make a 40-yard throw accurately" and the toward-sideline throw is easy, and because Fig 7's completion went to the come-back receiver. The answer says **deep first (H or Z)**. That isn't taught anywhere, and the chapter's own reasoning argues against it. Either teach a scramble look order in the scramble-drill section, or change the answer.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- **Whose fault is the sack?** "A sack after four seconds is usually on the quarterback" (Watch for it) is asserted, not shown. The standard stat (pressure-to-sack rate, or sacks per pressure) would fit perfectly in "Measuring a quarterback" and would quantify the "pocket presence" the chapter calls one of the few clear separators.
- **When does he take off running?** (See §2.9.) The best-valued ending is never taught as a decision.
- **Does the progression ever change after the snap?** The chapter fixes read 1/2/3 by the call, and 08-01 is promised for coverage. One sentence would help: "the coverage can flip which side he reads first (half-field reads, 04-01)". Manning's "pre-snap read… told him which side to work" hints at it without connecting it to the progression.
- **Where do I find these numbers?** Name the public sources for time to throw, aDOT, CPOE and EPA per dropback (NGS site, rbsdm.com/stats) in Measuring or Go deeper, so the profile skill is usable this weekend.
- **How many points is a win?** This is needed to make "59 vs 17 points" meaningful (§2.11).
- **Does a throwaway hurt CPOE / completion %?** (§2.13.)
- **Spike vs grounding**: why is a spike legal? (One clause, or a forward link to 09-04.)
- **Who tells the receivers he has escaped?** Do they watch him, or is there a verbal or visual cue? "Get into his line of sight" implies they're watching him, but on a deep route your back is to him.
