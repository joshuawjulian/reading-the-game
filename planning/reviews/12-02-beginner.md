# 12-02 Beginner-reader review: Case Study: Belichick and Game-Plan Football

Reviewer persona: a casual fan who has never played. I have read 01-01 through 12-01 in curriculum order and none of
Parts 13 to 15. I read the qmd start to finish and looked at the rendered PDF (22 pages, built 07:20 today, newer than
the qmd). Line numbers refer to the qmd. "p." means PDF page.

**Overall:** strong. The three-questions frame (l.566-569) is the best device in the chapter. It makes every Super
Bowl section readable, and the side-by-side table (p.10) works. The XLIX strip tells its story in print. The main
problems are three internal contradictions between figures and text (XXXVI man coverage, the LIII rush count, the
Gronk bracket caption), one wrong clock statement, an undefined ring colour, and a lot of material retold from 08-03,
08-04, 10-03 and 10-04.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **XXXVI man vs not-man contradiction (l.721, l.795-796, fig-xxxvi bottom).** The text ends the section with
   "Crennel's staff guessed the Rams would be ready for man again, and they didn't give it to them." The bottom panel
   of fig-xxxvi draws dotted **man assignments** on X, H, Z and Faulk, and its caption says "Dotted lines are man
   assignments." So what *did* they play? I finished the section unable to say what coverage New England ran in
   XXXVI. One sentence is needed (for example, zone with a DB matched on Faulk), and the figure has to agree with it.
2. **XXV clock logic is wrong (l.594-595).** "A no-huddle offense lives on completions: they keep the clock stopped
   (an incompletion stops it too, but gains nothing)". 01-01 taught me that a completion in bounds does *not* stop the
   clock. As written, this reads as an error, and it undercuts the clock argument in the next bullet. Probably what's
   meant is "completions move the chains and keep the tempo." Rewrite it.
3. **LIII: figure vs chart vs text on the rush.** fig-liii-plan's bottom panel is titled "same six, four rush, zone
   behind". The chart right after it says five or more rushers on **54%** of dropbacks (versus 32% in the season), and
   the text calls that "the 'rush' went the other way from what the coverage suggests". So the drawn "passing look"
   is the *minority* call. And why would coverage "suggest" anything about the rush count? The diagram should either
   show the typical call or say outright "this is one of their calls; more often a fifth rusher came."
4. **LIII: "six or more DBs" 44% vs "two linebackers walked up".** If six defensive backs are on the field almost
   half the time, who are the six on the line? Defensive backs walked up? The chapter never reconciles the personnel
   bar with the "six-man line" story.
5. **LIII: "The ending was the plan in one snap" (l.935).** The plan was zone and disguise after a season of man. The
   last snap was all-out Cover 0 **man**. Why is that "the plan in one snap"? Say the missing step: after a night of
   zone, Goff was ready for zone, and the surprise was now man.
6. **Text vs figure on Jonathan Jones (l.828-830 vs fig-liii-plan).** The text says he was moved "into a safety-type
   role". The figure has him as the slot nickel ($) dropping from a blitz look. Which is it?
7. **XXXVI: did the Rams take the concession?** "daring ... Mike Martz, to run" (l.723), and Faulk had only 17
   carries. The obvious question is why Martz didn't run more. That second-guessing is part of this game's legend,
   and the chapter never says whether the concession was declined.
8. **2018 "pass-first team" yet second in 21 personnel (l.1265-1267).** As a beginner I read 21 personnel as a run
   grouping, so this looks contradictory. One clause explaining it (Develin, play-action from 21) would fix that.
9. **XLIX "not the right matchup for us to run" (l.990-991).** Three WRs against three DBs sounds even to a
   beginner. Spell out that the goal-line package is eight heavy run defenders against Seattle's lighter run-blocking
   group.
10. **"Go deeper: was the throw a blunder?" (l.1083-1093) stops just before the punchline.** It says three tries
    instead of two made the pass defensible, but never computes it. With the chapter's own numbers: two runs give
    about 1 - 0.45^2 = 80%, and pass then two runs give about 1 - 0.49 x 0.45^2 = 90% (before interceptions). One
    line of arithmetic would turn "defensible" from an assertion into a demonstration.
11. **Predict 3 answer arithmetic (l.1552-1553).** "two or three of those [8-12 yard throws] reach the opponent's
    35". From your own 30 you need 35 yards. Two throws get you to about midfield, and only three long ones get
    there. Say "three or four".

## 3. Diagrams I struggled to decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **fig-xxv top (p.4):** "corners jam, then sit" is drawn next to corners aligned about 5.5 yards off the ball. You
  can't jam from there. Either press the corners or change the label.
- **fig-xxxvi (p.6) and other under-center figures:** the Q marker sits on top of the C, so the center's letter is
  half-hidden in every under-center diagram (fig-xxxvi, fig-liii, fig-gronk, Predict 1-2). The top caption says "the
  nickel defense fits its gaps", but nothing in the panel shows a fit, so the caption describes action I can't see.
- **fig-liii bottom (p.8):** the busiest figure in the chapter. The SS and $ drop arrows cross each other, and it took
  me several looks to find which defender the "crosser meets a zone defender" leader means. Consider ringing that
  defender, or naming him in the caption.
- **fig-xlix strip (p.11):** it works as a story. But in frames 2 and 4 the Z / Browner / H / Butler markers pile on
  top of each other: in frame 4 Butler's CB box is hidden under H and the ball, so the decisive frame is the hardest
  to read. Spread them slightly or add a leader label "Butler". The undefined green ring is on Butler here too.
- **fig-gronk (p.13):** the Panel C caption says "a **cornerback** underneath and outside and a safety over the top",
  but the rings are on the **nickel ($)** and the SS. The caption also says "nobody deep in the middle" while the FS
  stands near the middle of the panel, and the panel's own label says "no robber" (a different claim). In Panel A,
  "three linebackers on the field" is placed in the *offense's* backfield next to Y and R, so it reads as offensive
  information.
- **fig-chameleon (p.14):** the caption claims New England's games "spread unusually far from one another; ... only
  one team ... varied more", but the picture doesn't let me check that. There's no other team to compare, and most NE
  dots sit inside the grey band. Either show one steady team's dots in grey for contrast, or cut the visual claim
  back to the text. The "2018 playoffs: run first" label doesn't name the game, though the caption says it's the AFC
  Championship.
- **fig-fingerprint (p.16):** the middle panel's cloud of teams sits mostly to the **right** of zero (the top receiver
  gets *more* of the ball against most defenses?). That's puzzling and unexplained. The text says "Against most
  defenses, that share hardly moved." The text also names Pittsburgh as "right behind" in panel 1, but only BAL is
  labelled. The caption tells me "negative is better" for panel 1 but gives no direction for panels 2 and 3, and the
  good end flips from left to right in panel 3.
- **l.1365, "(a target or so)":** -1.3 percentage points of a team's roughly 35 targets is about half a target, so
  this overstates the effect.

## 4. Drag and repetition

The chapter retells a lot that the prerequisites have already told me, sometimes with the same quotes:

- **Super Bowl XXV:** 08-03's film room already gave me the plan, "Thurman can have 100 yards", the Hall of Fame
  sheets and "make them beat you left-handed". 08-04 gave me the 40:33 and 20-19. The new material here is the
  diagram, the 2-4-5 and Banks's "helmet in his ribs". Trim the retelling and say "you met this in 08-03; here's the
  drawing."
- **The Lombardi quote** (l.533) is verbatim from 08-03.
- **The Super Bowl XLIX play:** 10-03 already told the jam, the jumped slant and the **same Butler quote** ("I
  remembered the formation..."). The new value here is the clock reasoning and the practice rep, so cut the repeated
  quote.
- **"Situational mastery" bullets (l.1293-1312):** XXXVI's Madden drive, the 2003 Denver safety and Bradshaw are all
  told in full in 10-04, with the same details. The chapter admits this ("told in full"). As a reader it felt like
  re-reading. Shorten each bullet to one line and spend the space on the measurement and on a "test it on Sunday"
  method (see §5).
- **The "Common misconception" callout** (l.693-698) repeats the "What to notice" paragraph just above it (135
  yards, the box score says the run defense failed). Keep one.

2. **Break down the three Super Bowl plans, taken vs conceded:** XXV and LIII yes, mostly. XXXVI only partly: I can

5. **Recognize situational habits and "test a team for them on Sunday":** no. The Sunday callout is entirely the
   take-away test. Nothing tells me what to watch for in the situational part: for example, what a team does with
   0:58 and two timeouts before half, whether it deferred, how it manages the clock. Add two or three situational
   checks to the Watch-for-it box, or narrow the objective.

**Predict-the-play, attempted before opening the answers:**

- **P1 (key turned around):** I said "draw or screen to the offset back, because dime plus a crashing end leaves a
  light box." That was correct. The label "dime: one linebacker in the box" plus the prompt "the defense has been
  right all game" almost gives it away. To make it harder, drop the box label and ask the reader to count.
- **P2 (frozen in nickel):** I said "run right at the $, with Y blocking; no huddle to freeze personnel." That was
  correct, but the caption **answers the question for me**: it says "hurried to the line without a huddle, so the
  defense can't change personnel", and the leader label reads "a 190-pound nickel back on the edge". Move both into
  the answer. The purple ring ("a defender the plan moved or gave a special job") is also a stretch for a victim.
- **P3 (58 seconds):** I said "attack with short throws for a field goal, because of the double score after
  deferring." That was correct, but it's recall from the section above rather than a read of the picture. The
  formation adds nothing. Make the picture matter (for example, where are the soft spots against this shell?), or
  give the reader a scoreboard decision to make.

## 6. What I still wonder, and what this chapter should answer

- What coverage did New England actually play in Super Bowl XXXVI, and why didn't Martz just give Faulk the ball 30
  times?
- In LIII, who were the "six on the line" when six DBs were on the field, and what was the typical rush on a
  dropback: four or five?
- Malcolm Butler first appears at l.971 (the LII benching), *before* I meet him as the XLIX hero. The irony is lost.
  Move the LII paragraph after XLIX, or add "(the hero of XLIX, below)".
- "The great quarterbacks who beat New England... later Patrick Mahomes" (l.1388): when did Mahomes beat them? The
  2018 AFC title game in this same chapter is a Mahomes *loss*. Name the game or drop him.
- How did a plan get taught in a single week? 08-03 covers the week in general. One concrete Belichick detail (how
  many new calls per week, a walkthrough or a scout-team habit) would make "players smart enough to absorb them"
  concrete.
- Did the method decline after Brady (2020-2023)? The chapter jumps from 2019 to Belichick's UNC season. A sentence
  on whether the game-plan defense still worked without a great offense would test the chapter's central claim.
