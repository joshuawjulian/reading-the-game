# 06-05 Quarters and the Two-High Match Family: beginner reader review

Reviewer role: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read 01-01 to 06-04 in order
(plus 05-05). I read the qmd start to finish and looked at all 22 PDF pages (60 dpi, plus 110-130
dpi crops of Figs 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and Predict 1-3).

Overall: a very good chapter. The hook pays off, "quarters safeties play the inside receiver, not a
zone" sticks, the read-safety triptych (Fig 2) is the best single teaching figure in Part 6 so far,
and the charting-source-break discussion is exactly what an analytically minded reader wants. The
problems are (a) two places where the chapter contradicts itself, (b) two diagrams (the boot flood
and the RB wheel) that I couldn't decode without the code, (c) Fig 1 not showing the "four over
two" it claims, and (d) length: about 9,900 words of non-code text (front matter and the You'll
need box included) against a 6,000 target, with clear repetition to cut.

## Must fix (a careful reader will trip on these)

1. **"Never fooled" vs the bootleg flood.** Run-fit section: "The called rotation arrives sooner;
   the read arrives later but is never fooled into abandoning the deep field on a pass." The run
   support box: "every pass is met by two safeties who never left." Then the bootleg section shows
   exactly that: the FS "reads U's run block and steps up; the over route lands behind him." The
   reader feels lied to. Say instead: the read is never fooled by a *pass that looks like a pass*;
   play-action that sells the run, including #2's fake block, is the read's weak spot (and say that
   this is why the boot section exists).
2. **Who owns the flat? Two rules contradict.** Fig 1 label: "$: wall #2, then the flat"; text:
   "Then they take the first receiver who comes to the flat." The Palms section: "a quick throw to
   #2 breaking toward the sideline lands in front of everyone." If the apex defender's rule *is*
   the flat, why does the quick out to #2 land in front of everyone? Missing why: the apex is
   walling #2 from *inside*, 4-5 yards inside the out-breaking #2, so he can't widen in time on a
   one-second throw. One sentence (and ideally an arrow in Fig 7, panel 2, showing the $ too late)
   would fix it.
3. **Fig 1 doesn't show "four over two" on both sides.** The text says "The apex defenders, here
   the nickel back on the left" and then "Count the defenders against the two receivers on one
   side. The corner, the safety, the apex defender and, a step inside, a linebacker." On the right
   (tight-end) side of Fig 1 there is no apex defender: CB, SS, M and the E. Who walls Y and who
   takes the flat on that side? I can't do "You'll be able to" #1 for the TE side. Either label who
   does it (the Mike? the end?) or say that against an attached TE the "apex" is the linebacker
   or the end and why the count still works.
4. **Who covers the running back?** Fig 1 and the text: "The linebackers ... take the running back
   if he releases." Fig 10: "the nickel ($) takes him as the first man to the flat." Reconcile in
   one line: back to the flat = the apex's "first man to the flat"; back into the hook/middle = the
   linebacker.
5. **What counts as "deep"?** MOD is defined as man "if #1 runs a deep route," then "breaks his
   route off short, about 5 yards or less." Fig 10 then labels a 12-yard dig "#1 went deep: stays
   on him." A beginner thinks a dig is an intermediate in-cut, not "deep." Say outright: for MOD,
   anything past about 5 yards counts as "deep," including digs and curls. Same gap for the safety:
   what counts as #2 "going vertical"? Palms has a 5-yard threshold ("out in the first 5 yards");
   the read safety's vertical/short threshold is never given (is a 10-yard curl by #2 vertical?).
6. **Fig 10 (RB wheel) can't be read as drawn.**
   - The back's route runs from R across the backfield and then *up from Z's spot*, so it looks as
     if Z runs the wheel.
   - The brown ball path, the nickel's dashed path and the corner's dashed path all converge on
     one point about 11 yards deep outside the numbers, so the picture says "the nickel is at the
     catch point," the opposite of the caption ("chasing a running back from behind ... empty deep
     quarter in front of them").
   - The ball is caught at about 11 yards; the wheel arrow goes to 15+.
   - The corner's path goes outside and up first, then inside, while the caption says "follows him
     inside."
   - R and Q markers touch.
   - "#2 inside: SS helps inside": helps on *what*, the slant or the dig?
7. **Fig 9 (bootleg flood) is hard to decode.**
   - The defense is suddenly a base 4-3 with an "S" box (the Sam) right next to the "SS" box. "S"
     is not in the "Reading the diagrams" key, and S vs SS invites confusion. The caption never
     says the defense switched to base personnel because the offense is in 12.
   - The trailing right corner's long dashed path runs past the SS marker, so it reads as the SS
     running across the field.
   - "Three receivers at three depths" isn't labeled. Add small "deep / ~12 / flat" tags on X, Z
     and U, since that's what "flood" means.
   - "U fakes a block": nothing in the picture shows a fake block; U just releases.
8. **Fig 5 (run fit) shows the wrong case for its main lesson.** The text says "The other safety
   went with his receiver ... That is the offense's main weapon against quarters' run defense" and
   "Offenses ... run the ball *away* from the tight end for exactly this reason." But the run in
   the strip goes *toward* the tight end, so removing the backside FS costs the defense nothing:
   the SS still makes the tackle. I had to work out the logic myself (run toward the side where #2
   releases, so the play-side safety leaves). Spell it out in two sentences, or flip the strip.
   Also, after the handoff the ball covers the R label, so in panels 3 and 4 I couldn't tell which
   blue dot was the ball carrier.

## Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

- **Why would the QB fall for Palms if the tell is visible?** Palms section: "He threw it because
  the corner looked like a quarters corner, 7 yards off and playing the outside receiver." Three
  paragraphs later: "the corner on the Palms side tends to be a yard or two shallower ... inside
  #1, with his eyes turned to the slot." And the Predict 3 caption makes the tell obvious. Add a
  line: the difference is a yard and a head turn, often disguised until the snap, and the QB has
  already decided on a rhythm throw.
- **MEG's benefit is never shown.** Fig 4 shows only MEG's cost ("corner route: FS alone"). In the
  MEG panel the nickel *also* drives on the slant, so nobody looks "freed." The text says MEG
  "frees everyone else," but the figure gives me no reason anyone would choose it here. Either show
  the freed defender doing something (the FS leaning to the other side, the $ taking the flat) or
  say in the caption that this panel shows the cost, and that the benefit appears in 06-06's trips
  checks.
- **What does a read safety do with no #2?** "Quarters works beautifully when each side of the
  formation has two receivers. Formations don't always cooperate." Then straight to Cover 6. The
  obvious question, what the backside safety in plain quarters reads against 3x1 or a lone
  receiver, is never answered, not even with a one-line forward reference.
- **"If #2 and #3 both run vertical the strong safety can take only one of them."** But the Fig 6
  label already gives #3 to the Mike. So is anyone uncovered, or is it just a linebacker on a tight
  end? Say which: the issue is the mismatch, not an uncovered man.
- **Motion.** The Go-deeper box says offenses use "motion and route combinations designed to land
  exactly on the seam between two players' rules." Motion changes who is #2. A casual viewer sees
  motion on almost every NFL snap; one paragraph on what a quarters safety does when #2 motions
  away is the biggest missing piece.
- **Red zone MEG**: "MEG becomes more common because 'deep' barely exists." Why does that favour
  MEG? (Presumably MOD's "zone to the deep quarter" has no deep quarter to zone into.) Say it.

## Terms used before explained, or never explained

- **"prevent defense"**: used twice ("it would be a prevent defense"; "prevent defenses have used
  it for decades"). Owned by 10-04 (later). Needs a gloss and forward link.
- **"poach, solo, stubbie"**: dropped as a list with no meaning ("a set of answers to it (poach,
  solo, stubbie)"). Also "Palms, Stubbie and other per-side calls" in the history. Gloss at least
  one, or don't name them.
- **"dagger"**: "shows dagger and scissors, two concepts built to make a quarters safety's read work
  against him." Scissors is explained later (Answer 1); dagger never is. Give each a 5-word gloss.
- **"the 'now' throw to the flat"**: not explained, and it isn't linked to 04-03's "now screen."
- **"overhang defender"**: linked but not glossed in-line ("the overhang defender job"). Owned by
  02-05; a half-line reminder would help.
- **"4-2-5"** (TCU): not glossed. Owned by 05-03.
- **"charted / charting labels"**: used in Fig 8's caption before the text explains that charting
  is people tagging film ("Two different groups of people watching film"). Move that explanation
  up a paragraph.
- **"dropbacks"**: the reader may wonder why the rate is per dropback, not per play. One clause.
- **The orange line in the Predict figures** (line to gain) isn't in the "Reading the diagrams"
  key.
- **"U"** is explained in the Fig 9 caption, good, but it's not in the "Who's who" list.
- **"double move"**: used in the Palms box; "stop-and-go"/"slant-and-go" are clear from context.
  Fine, but 04-03 owns it, so link it.

## Diagrams and captions (beyond Must-fix 6-8)

  so the "bracket" doesn't read. The caption says "The apex defender or a linebacker takes the
  flat," but nobody in the panel covers Y's flat route. Lengthen the SS arrow and show who takes Y.
- **Fig 4, MEG panel**: the CB's path and the $'s path cross in an X right at the slant, which is
  hard to parse. The CB's path also continues past the slant's arrowhead.
- **Fig 7 (Palms strip)**: the text says "a defender coming downhill toward the sideline," but the

  the CB's ghost outline. In panel 2 the "#1"/"#2" tags sit under the receivers' snap spots, not
  where they are at +0.6 s.
- **Predict 1**: the orange line to gain (8 yards) runs straight through both CB markers.
- **Predict 3**: second-and-4, so the line to gain sits at 4 yards, right on top of H's 4-yard out.
  The route and the line merge. Change the distance (for example to second-and-7).
- **Fig 6 (Cover 6)**: good. The W on the Cover 2 side has no label; one small "W: hook" would
  complete the picture.
- Figs 1, 3 and 8 read well, and their captions say what to notice.

## Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items?

1. Diagram quarters vs 2x2 with each defender's rule: **mostly.** Corners MOD, safeties read #2,
   apex wall then flat, linebackers run then hook. I can't say who walls and takes the flat on the
   attached-TE side (Must-fix 3).
2. The read safety's three answers: **yes**, solidly. Fig 2 is excellent. I'm unsure of the
   vertical/short threshold.
3. MOD vs MEG: **I can tell them apart and name MOD's cost and benefit and MEG's cost.** I can't
   concretely say what MEG buys in a 2x2 call (Leaps).
4. Recognise Cover 6 and Palms: **Cover 6 yes; Palms post-snap yes, pre-snap shaky.** "A yard or
   two shallower" isn't something I can see on a broadcast. The chapter never says which of these
   looks are visible on the TV angle vs only on the All-22 or the replay (corners are often off
   screen pre-snap).
5. Explain the rise with data and caveats, and name the plays that beat it: **yes.** The
   source-break explanation is excellent.
