# 12-07 Beginner-reader review: The Lions, Ben Johnson's Offense and Dan Campbell's Aggression

Reviewer persona: casual NFL viewer, never played, has read 01-01 through 12-06 in order (so 13-xx is
*not* read yet). Read the PDF build (`pdfs/12-07-lions-ben-johnson-and-campbell.pdf`, 17 pages,
rasterized and inspected at 60 and 110 dpi) start to finish.

**Overall:** strong. The story arc works (Decker two-pointer, then rebuild, then four habits, then
fourth downs, then the break-up, then the Chicago "natural experiment"), Fig 1 (timeline) and Fig 3
(shift/set/motion strip) are excellent, and Fig 6 (transfer fingerprint) is the most convincing
picture in the chapter. The weak spots: the two jumbo/trick diagrams have decoding problems
(the ball-carrier convention, a crowded frame edge), the diagram key misses rings and NB, a few claims
lean further than the numbers shown, and the transfer analysis measures only two of the four habits.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **"Linemen first" isn't what the table shows.** p.2: "The old-fashioned part was the order of
   building: linemen first, on both sides of the ball." Table 1 has one offensive lineman (Sewell) and
   one edge rusher (Hutchinson) among eight players. The rest are a WR, a WR, an RB, an LB, a TE and a DB. As a
   reader I came away thinking the draft built *skill players*, which contradicts the sentence. Either
   add the linemen (e.g., other OL and DL picks or signings) or soften the claim.
2. **Under center: the "honest part" undercuts the claim it's meant to support.** p.5: UC +0.11 vs
   shotgun +0.10, then "Under center didn't win by itself; it made the run and the pass look alike, so
   that both worked." If UC plays were no better than shotgun plays, what number shows that "both
   worked" *because* of under center? As written, this is an assertion dressed as a data reading.
   The PA split (+0.32 vs +0.12) needs the same selection caveat the chapter rightly gives elsewhere
   (play-action is called in friendly downs). Without it, I'd naively conclude "always use play-action."
3. **League under-center numbers seem to move backwards.** p.5: league UC was "32%" in 2022, "barely more
   than a quarter" in 2023, and 29% in 2024, while p.11 has "27% in 2023 to 34% in 2025". A revival
   story with a five-point *drop* from 2022 to 2023 needs a sentence (a charting change? noise?).
   Otherwise the reader wonders whether the revival is real.
4. **Habit 2 never states its cost.** The objective says "say what each one buys and costs." Habit 1
   (slower true dropback), Habit 3 (illegal shift, play clock) and Habit 4 (the Washington game) all
   give a cost. Habit 2 doesn't: what does Detroit give up by putting a sixth lineman on the field
   instead of a receiver or tight end? The film room shows a procedural risk, but that's the cost of
   *reporting*, not of jumbo.
5. **"Diminishing returns" is asserted, not shown.** p.11: "That's what diminishing returns look
   like." Fig 5 (right) is a cloud: many teams with 40–60 attempts sit at 0.0–0.3, below the Lions.
   The cross-section shows no downward slope; the argument rests on comparing Detroit with two outliers
   (KC, PHI, and PHI is inflated by the tush push, as the caption admits). The real evidence is the
   26% vs 19% share of 4+-yard attempts. Lead with that, and either draw a trend line or drop the
   claim that the scatter "looks like" diminishing returns.
6. **If the league average go is worth +0.45 points over kicking, why does anyone kick?** Fig 5 right
   implies every team gains by going, on average. The selection caveat ("teams go when they like
   their chances") is the answer, but it sits a paragraph earlier and isn't connected to this
   surprising fact. Say it at the chart: "the average go looks good partly because teams only go when
   it looks good."
7. **"How opponents responded" describes no scheme response.** The "most effective response" is a
   game Detroit lost on five turnovers. What did Washington's *defense* do? Was it a scheme, or just
   the bad tail? The two-high evidence (36% vs league 39%, "23rd") is a three-point gap carrying the
   sentence "defenses more often brought a safety down." That's thin, and "23rd" leaves me unsure which
   direction is high. The takeaway "Opponents mostly couldn't scheme the offense away" isn't
   demonstrated.
8. **Fig 6 caption claim not checkable from Fig 6.** "Chicago's go rate rose about as much as the
   league's did": the panel shows only the 2025 league tick, not the 2024 one, so the reader can't
   see the league's rise. Add the 2024 league tick, or say "the league rose from 14% to 17% (Fig 5)."
9. **Table 2 vs FTN counts.** Fig 6 puts Detroit 2024 at 1.5 trick plays per 100 (roughly 15 plays),
   but Table 2, titled "The 2024 Lions' trick plays", lists seven regular-season plays. Footnote 12
   hints that sources differ, but the table title reads as complete. Call it "a selection" or
   reconcile the two.
10. **Cross-chapter inconsistency on the 2021 rank.** 11-05 says Campbell's Lions "went for it more than
    any other team in 2021, 2022 and 2023". This chapter says 3rd in the neutral go rate in 2021 (but 1st
    in raw attempts). The definitions differ, but a reader who just read 11-05 will spot it. Add one clause
    ("first in attempts, third in rate once desperate situations are removed").
11. **Goff's "castoff" story never pays off.** The setup (p.2, "a quarterback the league had written
    off") promises an answer to "how much of this was Goff vs Johnson?" The chapter gives Goff's 2024
    totals and nothing more. No before/after for Goff (Rams vs Lions, or with vs without play-action)
    the way Williams gets one.

## 3. Diagrams: decoding problems and captions

- **Fig 2 (jumbo, p.5): the running back's fake is drawn as the ball-carrier arrow.** The key says
  "thick dark arrows the ball carrier", and R's run fake is a thick dark arrow in *both* panels. A
  beginner reads it as R getting the ball. In the right panel it's worse: Q's dropback path is hidden
  under R's arrow, so the dashed ball line seems to start from R. I honestly thought R threw it until
  I read the caption twice. Draw the fake as a thin or dashed path, and make Q's set-up path visible.
- **Fig 2 right: the "space they left" is next to the free safety.** Z's crosser ends at about
  (16, -5) and the FS moves to about (17.5, -2.6), so the ball lands about three yards from the deep safety.
  The picture contradicts the caption's "space they left". Either end the crosser further to the
  sidelines or keep the FS held by X's post (he should drift *toward* X, to the left and deep).
- **Fig 4 (hook-and-lateral, p.9): the key action is crammed against the right frame edge.** In panels
  2–4, Z, H, CB, NB and SS sit on top of one another ("NB" and "CB" overlap and are unreadable at print
  size). In panel 4, H's marker touches the window edge, which breaks the ≥1.5-yard rule, and "H has the
  sideline to himself" can't be seen because the sideline isn't distinguishable from the frame. The
  window also shows only 9 v 9 (the left-side players are cut), with no note. Widen the lateral range,
  shift the formation inward, and draw the sideline.
- **Fig 4 caption:** it should say who is who in the real play ("Z = St. Brown, H = Jameson Williams"),
  since the text names them and the panels use letters.
- **Fig 3 (shift/set/motion, p.7): excellent.** Two small asks: panel 6's caption says the defense has
  "one fewer player than it had at the start" on the heavy side, but nothing in the panel lets me
  count that. Add a bracket or count label. Q's post-snap path in panel 6 loops oddly.
- **Fig 5 (p.10):** the right panel needs a trend line or a note (see Leap 5). The Bears are labelled
  without explanation (they matter later; say so).
- **Fig 7–9 (drills):** clean and readable. Fig 9 is labelled NB again (not in key). The ring on Q
  draws the eye to the quarterback, the very player the answer says is the decoy. That might be
  intentional, but with rings unexplained it reads as "watch this man."

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? Drills attempted before the answers

**Objectives**
1. *Rebuild and variance:* **Yes** for variance (the callout is clear). **Partly** for the
   rebuild: the "linemen first" claim confused me (Leap 1).
2. *Recognize four habits and their buys/costs:* **Yes** for recognizing under center, the reported
   lineman (listen for the announcement) and shift+motion, thanks to Fig 3. Trick plays as constraint
   plays: I get the idea. **Costs:** I can't state Habit 2's cost (Leap 4).
3. *Read the fourth-down data honestly:* **Mostly.** I can explain the go rate, the yardstick and its
   selection bias. I can't explain "diminishing returns" from the chart, only from the 4+-yards stat
   (Leap 5).
4. *2025 staff changes:* **Yes.** Fig 1 plus the text are clear.
5. *Transfer fingerprint and its limits:* **Yes** for what's shown, but Fig 6 measures under center,
   play-action and trick plays only. **Two of the four habits (jumbo with a reported lineman, and
   shift+motion) aren't measured in Chicago at all**, so "the style transferred completely" overreaches.
   Did the Bears report linemen? Did their motion or shift rate change?

**Drills (my attempt before opening the answer):**
- **Drill 1:** "Play-action pass. The safety walked down, so throw behind him. Maybe the leak to #70."
  → **Correct** on play-action behind the safety. I'd have picked #70 as the target, because the
  question is about him and the watch-for-it bullet primed "watch the safeties". The answer's
  "receiver crossing into the space" can only come from remembering Fig 2's bait panel, since the
  drill figure shows no receivers' routes. Fair, but the answer should say why the crosser beats the
  leak *here*: the SS is on #70's side, so the leak runs into him.
  Small point: the answer calls 2nd-and-5 "second-and-short"; earlier chapters treat 1–3 as short.
- **Drill 2:** "Go for it. It's the go zone from 10-04, and a 55-yarder at under two-thirds is poor.
  Probably a run from a heavy set." → **Correct.** But the answer's own data undercuts the drill:
  Detroit went 100% (6 snaps) against a **league 80%** in this spot. So this isn't where the Lions were
  distinctive, and the question "what would the Lions have done" has the same answer for every team.
  Pick a spot where the Lions and the league diverge (e.g., 4th-and-4 or 5 in the go zone, where 10-04
  says teams go only ~30%), which tests this chapter's point. The "what kind of play" part of the answer
  ("a run or a quick play-action throw") is too hedged to grade myself against.
- **Drill 3:** "The RB runs it in, the QB in the slot is a decoy or receiver, the trick is a QB
  throwback?" → **Partly.** I got the default and missed the trick (the RB *throwing*) and the
  tight-end target. The body never discusses direct-snap goal-line plays, so I had nothing to reason
  from. The ringed Q steered me wrong. Either add a paragraph or a Table 2 row on direct-snap/RB passes
  in the body, or make the drill about a habit the chapter taught.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- How much of the 2022–24 offense was **Goff**? (Leap 11)
- What did the **defense** (Glenn) contribute? The timeline gives the DC row equal billing, yet the
  defense is never discussed. One paragraph would do, or a sentence saying it's out of scope.
- **Who calls plays in 2026**, and has the early 2026 season shown the habits returning or not? The
  timeline says "not shown" and the text is silent. Even "too early to tell; watch for X" would help.
- Did **the Bears report linemen, shift and motion** like the Lions did? (Objective 5)
- What did **Washington** do in the playoff game, as a matter of scheme? (Leap 7)
- Why did Johnson's offense run **more motion than most but not the most**? What's the difference
  between his motion and the Rams' (who led)? The text says "the combination" but never shows a
  Rams-style vs Lions-style motion.
- Fourth-down cost side: how many games did a failed go plausibly **lose**? The chapter totals the
  points gained, but the "bad tail" is described only in words.
- What was the **referee procedure** that failed in Dallas? Must the lineman report to the referee
  specifically, and does the referee have to announce it? One sentence of the rule would make the
  film room's "lesson for a viewer" concrete.
