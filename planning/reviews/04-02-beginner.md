# 04-02 Pass Protection: beginner-reader review

Reader: casual NFL watcher, never played, has read 01-01 to 04-01 in order. Read the whole source
plus the rendered PDF (`pdfs/04-02-pass-protection.pdf`, 28 pp., pages rasterized at 60 dpi and the
figure pages again at 130 dpi).

**Verdict:** strong chapter, close to the pilots. The spine (protection is counting, inside-out, the
leftover man belongs to the QB) is clear and gets repeated in useful ways. The cup diagram, slide vs man,
the Mike-count bust, the hot-throw figure and the data section all work. The weak spots are listed below.
(a) Two of the frame strips don't tell their story in print (stunt switch, RB pickup).
(b) The idea of a "count" is used but never shown.
(c) Objective 2 (name slide vs man from the broadcast) gets no viewing cue.
(d) Drill 3's answer contradicts itself.
(e) There are small labelling inconsistencies.

## 2. Leaps (a missing "why")

1. **The Mike count** (above). The section explains *why* a reference man exists very well, but not *how* the reference produces assignments.
2. **Which end goes free in Drill 1.** The answer says the line blocks "the two A-gap linebackers and the two tackles, then **one end**". Which one, and why? With two free rushers (one end and the nickel) and only H as a hot receiver, who answers the other free man? If both free men are on the left (end and nickel), the line has to work *right* to the other end. Say so. This is the chapter's central skill, and the answer skips its hardest step.
3. **Drill 3's answer contradicts itself.** "Expect the line to slide the other way (left), so that the interior and left side are handled by linemen, while the tight end and back deal with 99." The chapter has just told me that a back on a great end is a mismatch ("now a 215-pound back has him") and that a chip exists "so that the tackle can catch up with him". If the line slides left, the RT isn't on 99, so whom is the chip helping? The answer then says "one of them staying in to block him with the tackle", which implies the RT *is* on 99, i.e. a half-slide left with the man side to the right. Choose one: a half-slide left (RT on 99, chips from Y and R) fits everything the chapter taught.
4. **"Good offenses slide *toward* the biggest threat in the middle"** (slide section). "The biggest threat in the middle" is muddy. Which way do they slide when the threat is an *end*? Drill 3 says *away* from him. One explicit rule ("slide toward the interior threat; help the end you slide away from") would settle it.
5. **What the back's alignment tells you.** The Watch list says "a back offset to one side usually means that side's edge rusher is getting a chip". But in Fig 3, Fig 5 and Drill 2 the back offset to the left is the *man-side blocker* for the Will, not a chipper. In Fig 6 the back on the right chips. As a viewer, how do I tell "back's side = his blocking side" from "back's side = chip side"? Can the back block across the QB's face? The chapter never says.
6. **Fig 5 text vs picture.** "Meeting him at the **line of scrimmage** keeps the collision away from the launch point". In the strip the back meets the Will 2.5–3 yards *behind* the line. Say "meets him in the hole, well in front of the QB", or move the meeting point.
7. **Why a full slide ever, if the half-slide exists?** The table says "Stunts, inside pressure". The body never makes that case. One sentence after the half-slide section would do.
8. **Bust or design, from the couch.** "Was he the widest man... That was the plan. Was he an inside man...? That was a bust." This is good. Add the cue the chapter almost states: *if the ball didn't come out hot, suspect a bust* (or the QB missed it). Today the reader can't separate "QB missed the hot" from "line busted".

## 3. Diagrams

- **Fig 10 (stunt switch strip): the story isn't readable in print.** In frames 3 and 4 the 3, the E, RG and RT sit in one overlapping cluster, and the end's loop is a faint trail. I can't see "RT takes the 3, RG picks up the E" in the frames; I take it from the notes. The view needs a tighter lateral zoom on the right side (roughly w 0 to 7), a frame between 0.8 and 1.4, and dotted green assignment lines in the final frame. Fig 11 (stay vs switch) carries the idea better than the animation does.
- **Fig 5 (RB pickup strip):** frames 3–4 stack W, R, LG and LT into one blob. It's readable at 130 dpi, mush at reading size. A 2-yard tighter window or smaller rings would help. The ring colours (purple W, green R) do the main work.
- **Fig 7 (max protect):** the linemen are deliberately unlabelled. That leaves a row of seven identical dots between U and Y, and I can't count "8 blockers" from the picture. The Q marker sits at the line, and the seven-step drop is a hairline dotted path. Label the linemen or ring the eight blockers. Also show Q at his launch point, or say "Q shown at the snap".
- **Fig 8 (hot throw):** the OL labels collide ("LT LG C RG RT" overprint each other and the block bars). The note "the blitzer is about 2 s from the QB" looks long for about 8 yards at full speed. If it's a real estimate, cite or justify it; otherwise say "well over a second".
- **Fig 13 caption:** "the area ... **more than doubles**". The curve goes from about 13.8 to about 26.5 sq yd (×1.9). Say "nearly doubles". "Holds at six to seven yards for two seconds": at 2.0 s it's about 5.7. Say "stays near six".
- **Fig 12 strip note:** "snap: the line is a straight wall" sits oddly beside the section title "a cup, not a wall". Fine if intended ("starts as a wall, becomes a cup"). Make that explicit.
- **Predict figures (16–18):** at full-field width the OL labels shrink and collide. The ringed defenders read well. In Drill 2 the shaded slide ovals are nearly invisible in print; the "slide right" text label carries it.
- **Fig 6 (chip):** the stars are small. The labels make the figure work anyway.
- Captions are good overall: almost every one says what to notice. Fig 1, Fig 2 and Fig 4 are excellent.

- **Mike ID partly re-teaches 01-05.** 01-05 already showed "54's the Mike", re-declaring when a nickel walks down, and throwing hot. 04-02's new material (the bust figure, Brady, the A-gap mug) is good, but the "can name the safety / re-declare" paragraph is a re-run. ### Predict-the-play attempts

These were made from the figure and question before reading each answer. Note: I reviewed from the
source, where the answer text sits below the drill, so the attempts were made blind but in good faith.

- **Drill 1:** my guess was "5 block, 7 could come, block inside-out, leave the widest two, throw hot to H off the nickel." **Correct.** I could *not* say which end the line blocks, or who answers the second free rusher. The answer doesn't say either (see 2.2).
- **Drill 2:** my guess was "Man side left: LT takes the end, LG the 1, the back takes the Will (inside), the nickel is free, throw to H." **Correct, confidently.** This is the best-calibrated drill.
- **Drill 3:** my guess was "Help goes to 99's side (Y chips, back chips), slide left, fewer receivers out, quick throw." **Correct in outline**, but the answer's own logic about who ends up blocking 99 is inconsistent (see 2.3), so I'm not sure what the "right" picture is.
- None of the drills tests a post-snap read (the chapter's first objective). Consider one drill in which a rusher came free and the reader decides whether it was a bust or by design.

## 6. Knowledge gaps (questions the chapter should answer)

1. What does a "count" look like, number by number? (the biggest gap)
2. How do I tell slide from man on TV?
3. Does the back block toward his own side or across the QB's face, and how do I tell from the alignment?
4. Who decides who's "hot" on a given play: the play call, the QB, or the receiver's own read? Does every pass play have a hot receiver?
5. Drill 1: with two free rushers, does the QB need two hot answers?
6. Hook: what did the QB shout, and why did it differ from the center's number?
7. Do linemen call stunts out to each other ("Tex", "switch")? Is any of it audible or visible?
8. Seven-man protection: how is it different from max protect?
9. Where does the 2-second / 3.5-second blame rule of thumb come from? Fig 14 suggests about 3 s.
10. (Fact-check flag, not a beginner issue.) FACTS-current.md says Stoutland "departed after 14 seasons". The chapter says 2013–2025 = 13 seasons, and its source headline also says 13. Reconcile FACTS.
