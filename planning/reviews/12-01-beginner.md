# Beginner review: 12-01 Case Study: Bill Walsh's 49ers and the West Coast Offense

Reviewer persona: casual fan who has never played, has read Parts 1–11 in order (not Part 13).
Read the qmd start to finish and the rendered PDF (`pdfs/12-01-walsh-49ers-west-coast.pdf`, 21 pp.,
built 2026-10-08 06:59, newer than the qmd). Overall it's strong: the hook pays off, the
"pass as a run" argument lands, and the sweep-vs-flat figure is the best single picture of the idea in
the book. The problems below are mostly contradictions with earlier chapters, a few figure-to-text
mismatches, and repetition of material I've now read three times.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained (or never explained)

- **Win probability / "competitive games"**: fig-fingerprint caption, "with the win probability
  between 10% and 90%", and the census box, "early-down throws in competitive games". Win probability
  belongs to 13-03, which comes after this chapter, so I haven't learned it. Add a one-line gloss and a
  forward link ("the chance, from the score and clock, that the offense wins; we drop blowouts").
- **Passer rating**: "his 1989 passer rating, 112.4, was a single-season record". It belongs to 13-02
  and isn't glossed here. I have no idea whether 112.4 is good. One clause would fix it.
- **nflverse**: "nflverse starts in 1999". It belongs to 13-01 and isn't glossed. Say "the free
  play-by-play data this book uses".
- **"Change Left Slot"**: "the full call reported as 'Change Left Slot – Sprint Right Option.'" The
  formation half of the call is never decoded. I read 04-08, so I want to know which word is the
  formation, which is the protection and which is the play.
- **"Spider 2 Y Banana"** (What survives) is quoted as the model WCO call and never decoded. A
  one-line decode, or better, the real call for the chapter's own drive diagram, would make the
  language point concrete.
- **Orange line in The Catch figure**: never explained. In every earlier diagram the orange line is
  the line to gain. Here it sits at the Dallas 3, so it's three yards short of the goal line. I
  first read it as the goal line and couldn't work out why Clark (panel 2) was "in the end zone".
  The caption needs: "orange = the line to gain at the 3; the grey area is the end zone." The prose
  also never explains why it's "third-and-3" and not "third-and-goal" (which Predict 3 *does* call
  it). Someone who learned downs in 01-02 will trip on this.
- **"two-high safety shell"** (How opponents responded) isn't linked, though 02-05 owns two-high.
  That's minor, but every other term in the section is linked.

## 2. Leaps and missing "whys"

- **Drive is read in the opposite order from 04-04, with no explanation.** 04-04 taught drive as
  "usually read **low to high**, because the shallow is open early or not at all": (1) the shallow,
  (2) the dig. fig-wco-drive numbers it (1) the dig if the Mike comes up, (2) the shallow if he
  drops. Either explain why Walsh's version reads high-to-low (five-step timing, the dig arriving on
  the hitch step?) or match 04-04. As written it looks like an error.
- **Slant break depth contradicts itself and 11-02.** The timing section: "a three-step drop for
  routes that break at 5 or 6 yards". fig-slant-flat-strip and fig-placement: X "breaks inside at
  about 2 yards". 11-02's fig-walsh: "X breaks inside at about 5 yards and catches the ball at 7."
  So is it the break or the catch at 5–6? Say "routes *caught* at 5–6 yards" and keep one number
  across the book.
- **The Catch: throwing it out of bounds is called both bad and good.** "A quarterback without a
  plan throws the ball out of bounds or takes the sack, and ... either one leaves the 49ers needing a
  touchdown on fourth down." Then, one paragraph later, the virtue of Montana's throw is that "If Clark
  couldn't, it would land out of bounds: no interception, and fourth down still to play." The real
  distinction (a throwaway gives up the down; Montana's throw kept the throwaway's safety *and* gave
  a scoring chance) is never stated. Say it.
- **"A case for the horizontal passing game as a running game, played backwards."** I couldn't parse
  this. Do you mean that the drive *ran* when Dallas expected a pass, the mirror image of passing when
  a defense expects a run? Spell it out.
- **The Walsh team ran plenty.** The chapter's premise is "a team that couldn't run the ball", yet
  Craig ran for 1,050 yards in 1985 and the winning drive in The Catch was built on runs. What was
  the 49ers' run/pass split compared with the league? Did the short passing *make* the run work later?
  Without that, the premise reads as only half true.
- **Why the tree is so big.** "He also coached the coaches" is the only causal sentence. What did
  Walsh do that produced head coaches (staff meetings, writing everything down, the fellowship)? The
  misconception box says "a way of working ... travels", but the mechanism is asserted, not shown.
- **Predict 3's answer relies on facts the drill never gave**: "with no timeouts to spare". The setup
  gives down, distance, score and time but no timeouts. In the real Catch the 49ers had one left.
  Either give it in the setup or drop it from the answer.
- **Predict 1's answer has two wrong-sounding lines**: "the eight players in the box are all on the
  wrong side of the line of scrimmage to chase it". They're on the *defense's* side, which is the
  right side, so this probably means "inside, near the ball". And: "keep eight in the box *and* press
  the corners, which leaves no deep help at all". The diagram's free safety is still deep, so there is
  one deep helper. "Only one deep helper" is what's meant.
- **Fingerprint EPA bars undercut the claim and go unaddressed.** "Every dropback" EPA (+0.08) beats
  the 1–5-yard throw (+0.07), and the deep bars are far higher. The obvious analyst's question is
  whether Walsh's short emphasis is *suboptimal* by modern EPA. The chapter answers success rate
  vs runs, but never this. One paragraph would do.
- **"Caught" vs "thrown."** The takeaway says "a pass **caught** one to five yards past the line
  succeeded more often than a designed run". The bar is throws by intended air yards, incompletions
  included (the body says so). The takeaway overstates by conditioning on the catch. Use "thrown".

  - **The colour contradicts the text.** The text: "The Shanahan branch ... Through his son Kyle, Gary

  In the panel he's about 1.5–2 yards past it. That's small, but it's the exact claim the caption

- **Print layout**: page 9 is about two-thirds blank (The Catch strip pushed to p. 10) and page 14 is
  about three-quarters blank (the tree pushed to p. 15). Two near-empty pages in a 21-page chapter

  sit back to back on their own line (qmd lines 1035–1036) and render as a single "1819". Merge them

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items?

1. *Explain the horizontal game as a run substitute and its cost*: **Yes.** That's the chapter's
   best work.
2. *Timing, YAC and placement as design goals, and spotting each on a replay*: **Mostly.** Placement
   I can spot (A vs B). For timing, the chapter never says what a timed throw *looks like on TV*
   ("the ball is in the air before the receiver turns his head"). The film-room box asks me to watch
   it, but not what the cue is.
3. *Scripting and practice*: **Yes, but thin.** It's all secondhand via a 1990 book review. I can
   explain *why* a timing offense needs rehearsal; I can't describe a Walsh practice beyond "four
   formats".
4. *Break down The Catch*: **Mostly.** Call, design, slip and why it worked are clear in text. The
   figure doesn't show the rub or the crossing at the moment the note claims (see 3).
5. *Trace the tree*: **Partly.** I can name the branches, but the McVay colour contradiction and the
   tangled 4th-generation lines mean I can't reliably trace a coach from the figure alone.
6. *West Coast census*: **Yes, the procedure is clear.** But I can't interpret my result for a
   *tree* team, because the chapter never says whether tree teams actually throw shorter than
   non-tree teams. The 2025 extremes are CHI 46% and PIT 64%. Is PIT a tree team? If tree teams
   aren't above the league figure, that's worth saying (and it fits the "tree ≠ playbook" box).

### Predict-the-play attempts (answered before opening)

- **Drill 1 (eight in the box)**: I said throw quick outside, a flat to a back or a hitch, because the
  corners are 8 off and there's one deep safety. **Correct.** The answer's "wrong side of the line"
  and "no deep help at all" lines confused me (see 2).
- **Drill 2 (press man on the backs)**: I said backs on linebackers, flat/swing or option-style routes.
  **Correct**, but easy: the Craig film-room box just primed it. A harder version would make me pick
  between the back and a crossing route against press, or say what to do about the unassigned Mike.
- **Drill 3 (first option gone)**: I said the second option high to the back line, or throw it away;
  worst is a sack or an INT. **Correct**, and trivially so, since it's The Catch again ("You know this
  play now"). It tests recall, not transfer. Better: change something real (a defender sitting on the
  end line, or the QB rolling left) so I have to apply the principle. It also needs the timeouts left
  in the setup.

## 6. What I still wonder (the chapter should answer)

- What was the 49ers' actual run/pass balance in 1981–89, compared with the league? Was "couldn't
  run" true after 1983?
- Do today's tree teams show the fingerprint (more short early-down throws, more RB targets) more
  than non-tree teams? One small team-level chart, or a sentence from the existing TEAM25 data, would
  close the loop between the data and tree sections.
- On a modern EPA scorecard, was short-first *optimal*, or just optimal for a team with a weak line?
  (The deep-pass EPA bars beg the question.)
- What does a real Walsh call look like end to end? Decode "Change Left Slot – Sprint Right Option",
  or the drive diagram's call.
- How did the WCO's play-action work (Walsh's under-center play-action is the bridge to Shanahan,
  which the chapter only gestures at)?
- What did Walsh do differently with his assistants that grew the tree, beyond "coached the coaches"?
- Where can I actually watch the film-room games (NFL Films, NFL+, YouTube full-game uploads)?
  The boxes say "watch Craig's routes" without saying where.
