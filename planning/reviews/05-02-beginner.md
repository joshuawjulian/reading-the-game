# 05-02 Even Fronts — beginner-reader review

Reader persona: casual NFL watcher, never played. Has read Parts 1–4 and 05-01 in order. I read the
rendered PDF (pdfs/05-02-even-fronts.pdf, 22 pp., rasterized to _pdfbuild/05-02-even-fronts/) from start to
finish. I tried each drill before opening its answer.

Overall: a strong chapter. The Over/Under plates with a gap-owner row (Fig 2) and the "where each front is
soft" figure (Fig 3) are the best teaching in it. The 46 story is vivid and the bear-iso frame strip tells the
story in print. The real problems are a few **internal contradictions**, mostly about which linemen are
"covered". A careful reader will trip over them, because 03-01 drilled the covered/uncovered rule. There is
also **one drill answer that the chapter's own numbers contradict**.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why", and internal contradictions

**2a. "The center starts free" contradicts 03-01's covered rule and Fig 1 (most important).**
- "What makes a front even": "*In an even front the center starts free, and so does at least one guard.*" The Takeaways repeat it: "*That leaves the center and at least one guard uncovered.*"
- Fig 1's even front (5-3-3-5) has a 3-technique on **each** guard, so **zero** guards are free.
- The Over and the Under each have a 1-technique on a shoulder of the center. 03-01 says, in so many words, that "the 1 covers the center". So in the two fronts the chapter is built around, the center is **covered**. Fig 2 agrees: its green ring is on a guard, not on the center.
- So the headline definition ("nobody head-up on the center, so the center is free") is true only for the balanced front, which the chapter says is the passing-down version. I finished the section unsure whether a 1-technique covers the center or not.
- Fix: say "nobody *head-up* on the center, and *exactly one* interior lineman (the center or a guard) left uncovered". Or explicitly reconcile the even-front definition with 03-01's shoulder rule.

**2b. The Under's "clean Mike" reason contradicts the Under's weakness.**
- Reason 3: "*the nose and end on either side of the guard make it hard for him to get a clean path up to the Mike.*"
- Four lines later, under "what it gives up": "*Runs at that bubble … put the uncovered guard on a linebacker with a running start.*"
- Fig 3 then draws exactly that guard climbing cleanly to the Mike.
- So is the Mike protected or exposed? Also, the Over's Will sits in the same geometry (a 1 on one side, a 5 on the other), so this reason doesn't tell the two fronts apart. Either cut reason 3 or explain what actually makes the Under's Mike "cleaner" than the Over's Will.

**2c. The wide-9 reaches the quarterback "in fewer steps".**
- "*a fast end gets to the quarterback's depth in fewer steps than he would from a 5*". He starts **farther** away, so as an analytically minded reader I don't believe this yet.
- The missing "why": the 5 has to beat a blocker who is on him at the snap, while the wide-9 runs untouched until the tackle gets to him. Say that. Or say "faster" (in time), not "fewer steps".

**2d. Why does the weak end widen against 11 personnel?**
- Fig 9 caption: "*the weak end widens because there is no tight end on his side*".
- But the left panel (21 personnel) **also** has no tight end on that side, and there the end does not widen. So the stated reason is no reason. The real reasons are more likely the slot receiver, no fullback, and passing-down odds.
- The paragraph heading "**The ends get wider when the tight end leaves**" adds to the confusion, because in Fig 9 the tight end hasn't left.

**2e. "Turns the coverage behind him into a one-deep shell."**
- In the left panel of Fig 9 (21 personnel), the strong safety is **already** in the box ("8 in the box") with one safety deep. So the base defense was already one-deep. Nothing changes; the SS just moves from the weak side to the strong side.
- Either the 21 picture should show two deep safeties, or the text should say "keeps it one-deep".

**2f. Box arithmetic is missing a "why".**
- "The box goes from eight to seven." The reader wants to hear that this keeps the defense **one more than the blockers** (7 blockers vs 8 in 21 personnel, 6 vs 7 in 11). Otherwise "seven" sounds like a concession.

**2g. Will and strong safety on the "wrong" side.**
- 01-03 defined the **Will** as the weak-side linebacker and the **strong safety** as the safety on the tight end's side.
- The 46 plate puts the W on the tight end's inside shoulder and the SS over the weak tackle. Fig 9 (left) also has the SS on the weak side.
- Nobody tells me why the names stay attached to players who moved. A one-line note is needed, e.g. "the names are the players, not the spots: in the 46 the Will moves to the strong side".

**2h. The bear front is filed under "even" against the chapter's own advice.**
- The misconception box says "*When the counts argue, believe the alignment.*" Then the bear (a 0 head-up on the center) is kept in the even chapter "because it was built by a 4-3 team".
- The parenthetical admits this, but the reader is left thinking the rule only applies when convenient. Say plainly that the bear is an odd alignment played by 4-3 personnel, and that the chapter includes it for history, not taxonomy.

**2i. A "9" end in 05-01, a "5" end here.**
- 05-01's gap-accounting figure was captioned "*this is the Over front of Even Fronts*" with a **9** end and the Sam owning C.
- Here the Over's strong end is a **5** owning C, and the Sam owns D. The Go deeper box lists "a 5, a 6 or a 7" but not a 9.
- One clause would reconcile them ("05-01 drew it with a 9; same front, different end technique").

**2j. Wide-9 lane width doesn't match 05-01.**
- 05-01 Fig "wide-nine" put the tight-end-side lane at "almost five yards" (end to 3-tech).
- This chapter's Fig 5 labels the same lane **3.6 yd**, with the 1.1 yd body width subtracted. Same alignment, different number.
- Pick one convention, or say "measured between the players' shoulders".

**2k. The hook's first play starts from an Over that is never explained.**
- The tackle "*had been standing over the right guard*" before moving to the Under. Back to the huddle never says why he was there first (a default huddle alignment? a disguise?). It's minor, but the hook raises the question.

**2l. The wide-9 table carries more weight than it can.**
- "*the numbers tell the story better than the reputation*", from two seasons, ranked by **yards per game**. A per-game number depends on game script: teams that are ahead get run against more. On a per-carry basis the 2017 Eagles were 5th. The misconception box then calls them "*the best run defense in the league*".
- A data-minded reader in this course will object. Lead with yards per carry, or add a line about game script.
- Also, both rank columns are headed just "Rank". Label them "(per game)" and "(per carry)".

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **The wide-9 section re-teaches 05-01**: the definition, the sprinter's stance, the lane, "the Sam fills it", and Washburn again. Fig 5 is mostly 05-01's fig-wide-nine plus the weak side. Cut the re-definition to one line and spend the space on the *front* (both ends, and the weak-side lane, which is the new idea).
- **Doug Plank's number** is explained three times: in the glossary, in the text ("*the 46 defense is not a count of anything*") and in the misconception callout. Drop the callout or the sentence.
- **Over/Under** is stated in the body, the Why-it-exists box, Back to the huddle and the Takeaways. That's acceptable for the core idea, but the Why-it-exists box mostly re-lists the bullets just above it.
- **Go deeper "dialects"** is dense and partly opaque. "*others call it an Over from the linebackers' point of view*" means nothing to me. Either explain it or cut that clause.
- **Four film-room boxes.** The Lovie Smith box mostly repeats the "under tackle" idea from the Tampa/Sapp passage. Three boxes would be enough.
- **The Watch-for-it log switches reference points**: "at the bubble or away" in the bullets, then "at or away … relative to the 3-technique" in the log. Pick one.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- **What does this look like in 2026?** Every example is from 1985, 2004–12, 2013 or 2017. Which current NFL teams are Over teams and which are Under teams? How often do even fronts appear in 2025 at all, compared with odd/tite fronts? (The nickel section has 2025 personnel numbers but nothing on fronts.)
- **Where is the strength when the tight end is flexed out or detached?** Modern TEs often line up off the line. Does a detached TE still set the front? And with 12 personnel, two TEs on the same side?
- **What happens to the Under on a tight-end shift?** Fig 4 only shows the Over. The Under's Sam is *on the line*, so does he have to run across the formation?
- **How does the offense respond pre-snap?** If the center and guards "read the front", how do they call it? (A link to 04-02's Mike ID would answer this.)
- **Is the 1-technique one-gapping or two-gapping in these fronts?** 05-01 made a big deal of the choice, and this chapter says "one-gap football" for the Over but never says what the 1 does against a double team.
- **When do teams actually play the wide-9?** Every snap, passing downs only, or on early downs too? (05-01 hinted "no defense lined up that way on every snap".)
- **In the 46, who covers the tight end and the running back in man coverage** if the outside linebackers rush?
- **Why does the Under's weak end widen at all on early downs?** That trade (a wider C gap on the weak side) is never named.
