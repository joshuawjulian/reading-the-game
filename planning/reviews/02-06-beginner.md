# Beginner-reader review: 02-06 Motion and Shifts as Weapons

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher, never played, has read 01-01 to 02-05 only. I read the
chapter start to finish and looked at all 22 PDF pages (60 dpi throughout; figure pages 3, 5, 6,
8, 10, 18-20 again at 130 dpi). Line numbers refer to the `.qmd`.

**Overall:** strong and readable. The five-jobs frame, the running-start chart, the
numbers-don't-agree section and the checklist card are excellent. The biggest problem is
conceptual. The chapter's central tool, the man/zone indicator, depends on **bump = zone**, but
the reader was taught in 01-05 that bump is how *man* defenses avoid travelling. This chapter
then names the same man-coverage move three more ways (trade assignments, hand off, exchange),
never draws it, and gives no way to tell a man-bump from a zone-bump. Second, two frame strips
don't show what their panel titles claim.

## 1. Terms used before they are explained, or never explained

- **"bump", inconsistent with 01-05.** 01-05 (line ~520) says: "many man-coverage defenses now
  **bump** instead: the defenders slide one spot over and swap receivers." 02-06's glossary
  (line 18) and table (line 754) say bump = "**Zone coverage**", and the checklist card (line
  1375) says "do they bump (zone)". The same reader meets both. Choose one meaning across the
  two chapters. Either make "bump" the neutral word for sliding over (which can be man or zone),
  or give the man version its own term ("exchange"), and fix 01-05 to match.
- **One concept, four names:** "trade assignments" (l. 671), "hand him off" (l. 787),
  "exchange" (l. 791, 1441, 1585), "bump" (01-05). "Exchange" is never bolded or defined, yet the
  Watch-for-it box and Takeaways rely on it. Define it once in bold, with an alias list.
- **"Trade" is overloaded.** It is a glossary term here (TE trade). Line 671's "they trade
  assignments instead" uses the same word for something unrelated, two sections after the
  definition. Change line 671 to "exchange".
- **"2x1 formation"** (fig-numbers caption, l. 820). The "You'll need" box recaps only 2x2 and
  3x1. A 2x1 with an attached TE is not obviously "balanced", yet the panel is titled
  "Before: balanced". Explain in a few words why 2 receivers plus a TE counts as balanced.
- **"time constant of 0.55 s"** (fig-head-start caption): unexplained jargon. "reaches about
  two-thirds of top speed in about half a second" says the same thing.
- **"26 Zone"** in the Madden box (l. 585). Hole numbering is taught in 03-01. Add a 3-word gloss
  ("a run to the right") or drop it.
- **"shovel pass"** (l. 525), **"coverage check"** and "the linebacker who sets the front"
  (l. 1017-1018): used without a gloss. "Coverage check" appeared in 01-05 only in passing.
- **"robber"** appears first in Answer 1 (l. 1476). It is glossed adequately; just noting a new
  term inside an answer.
- **"waggle"** (l. 1123): fine as is.
- Minor: on fig-team-motion, the bold team abbreviations (MIA, SF, ATL, LA, KC, NYG) are never
  explained, and "LA" means the Rams, which a casual fan may not assume.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

- **How to tell a man-bump from a zone-bump.** This is the big one. "When the indicator lies"
  says man defenses often hand the motion off, and Watch-for-it says "travelers are rarer than you
  expect (many man defenses exchange instead)". So in real life the most common thing I'll see is
  a slide that could mean either coverage, and the chapter gives me nothing to separate them.
  Suggest 2-3 sentences of cues, kept at the 02-05 level. After the slide, is the defender now
  squared up close on a specific receiver (press, tight leverage), or sitting at area depth
  between receivers? Did the safeties also change? Better still, draw the exchange (see §3).
- **Jet strips: the defense moves left before Z is on the left.** In fig-jet-vs-zone, panel 3
  (snap), Z is still right of the center (w ≈ +2.6), yet the text says "After the motion there are
  more receivers on the left, so the defense moves more men to the left" (l. 732). As drawn, the
  defense is reacting to where Z is *going*. Say so ("they move to where the jet is heading, not
  where he is"). Otherwise the bump looks like guessing.
- **The two jet strips change two things at once.** The man version starts one-high (FS deep
  middle) and the zone version starts two-high. A beginner comparing them could learn
  "one-high = man" instead of "travel = man". Start both from the same shell, or add a sentence
  saying the shell difference isn't the lesson.
- **Does a sideways head start help an upfield route?** The running-start model has both
  receivers running in the same direction. Jet and orbit head start are lateral. The chapter
  applies the 3-yard figure to "a receiver who is covered and one who is open" (l. 928), but a
  receiver who turns upfield has to redirect. One sentence is needed on which plays get the full
  head start (sweeps, flat and wheel routes, the cheat-motion release) and which get less.
- **Cheat motion vs the 2024 language (l. 1072-1091).** The cheat motion is described as a tight
  receiver's quick *outward* motion. The 2024 language is about an on-the-line receiver moving
  *backward or changing stance*. The story never says the Dolphins' receivers changed stance or
  were on the line, so I can't link the rule to the trick. The section closes with "what the league
  closed off was stealing one with a last-instant change of stance", a behaviour never described
  earlier. Either describe the stance part of the trick (if sourced) or say plainly that the
  language didn't really touch it.
- **Job numbering contradicts itself.** The list numbers communication as job **5** (l. 353), and
  the text says "The fifth job" (l. 1016), but the heading is "**Job 4**: stressing the defense's
  communication". "Job 3" (l. 883) covers jobs 3 and 4, and inside it the running start (job 4)
  comes before leverage (job 3). Sections also run Job 1, then "When the indicator lies", then
  Job 2. Retitle as "Jobs 3 and 4" and "Job 5", or drop the numbers from the headings.
- **"the numbers advantage that The Pre-Snap Phase taught you to count"** (l. 864): box counting
  was taught in 02-05 (A First Look at Defense). 01-05 only counted defenders for a kill call. Fix
  the link.
- **Zoom in fig-numbers puts Z inside the box.** Z sets at w = -5.8, just outside the LT and
  inside the shaded box edge (box half-width 6). A reader who just learned box counting asks: is Z
  now a seventh box blocker? If so, the run arithmetic ("box drops to five against six") changes,
  and "left: 3 v 2" counts him as a perimeter receiver. Set Z wider (about the old H slot width)
  or address it.
- **Spin definition vs Drill 1.** Spin is defined (l. 726) as the motion-side safety coming down
  *toward the motion side*. In Drill 1 the motion-side safety comes down to the *middle* (a
  robber). It's fine, but the reader has to reconcile it. A word in the answer would help ("a spin
  doesn't always drop toward the motion; here it drops to the middle").
- **"Atlanta's 70.1% and 60.8% in 2025, quoted above"** (l. 1259-1260): only 60.8% was quoted in
  the text. 70.1% appears only in a footnote.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **fig-jet-vs-man (p. 5), panel 3 "Snap: he has crossed the formation".** He hasn't. At the snap
  the ringed corner is at about w = +3.5, still on the right side next to the SS. Z is also still
  right of center. The caption's "At the snap the corner is on the wrong side of the formation" is
  not what the picture shows.
- **fig-jet-vs-man, panel 4 "he is chasing from behind".** At t = 1.25 the corner is ~6 yards
  *downfield* of Z, between Z and the end zone. To a novice that looks like he's in position, not
  chasing from behind. The text built on it ("The price is in the fourth panel… inside and behind",
  l. 665) doesn't match. Use a later frame (about 2.0 s) where Z has turned the edge and the
  corner is visibly trailing, or say what to look at.
- **Strip panel 4s (p. 5, 6, 10):** defensive linemen's markers sit on top of the offensive
  linemen, and in the zone strip the W/M ghosts overlap the NB ("W[W]M[M]" cluster, p. 6 panels 3
  and 4). They're readable at 130 dpi but busy. Consider leaving the DL static in these strips,
  since the action is elsewhere.
- **fig-jet-vs-zone:** the caption says "Ringed players are the ones who moved", but they are
  already ringed in panel 1 before anyone moves. Write "ringed players are the ones to watch".
- **fig-motion-family (p. 3):** generally decodable and the brown snap dot works well. Problems:
  (a) the green ring is never explained in the caption (it marks the player who moves); (b)
  **Trade**: X's and Z's ghost and new markers almost coincide (0.8-yard depth change), so the
  only evidence of "X steps off, Z steps up" is the label; (c) **Zoom** draws Z at his *start*
  with an arrow, while Trade and Bunch draw the *end* position with a ghost at the start. The
  inconsistency makes me hunt for where Z ended up.
- **fig-return-motion (p. 10):** the panel 4 field is at the goal line, but nothing tells me the
  orange line is the goal line there or that the grey area is the end zone. The caption says "back
  out toward the pylon", but Z catches the ball around w = +12, far from the pylon. Six rushers
  (caption) can't be counted in the frames.
- **Drill 1 (p. 18):** the NB's dashed travel path runs right along the orange line-to-gain and is
  partly hidden by it, and both CBs sit on that line. Move the line (to_go) or the depth.
- **Drill 2 (p. 19):** clear, but see §5 on difficulty.
- **Layout:** p. 13 is about two-thirds blank because fig-team-motion was pushed to p. 14.

## 5. Can I do each "You'll be able to…" item?

1. **Name the motions on sight:** mostly yes. Jet/orbit/return/zoom/rocket are distinct. I
   couldn't reliably tell **glide** from **short** on TV (both come in a few yards; the difference
   is whether he sets), and **jet vs fly** comes down to gun vs under center, which is fine once
   stated. The plate covers this item well.
2. **Explain the five jobs:** yes, though the heading numbering (§2) made me doubt my count.
3. **Read travel / bump / spin, and know when the guess is wrong:** I can name the reactions,
   and I know the indicator can lie, but I can't tell which case I'm watching, because the
   exchange is never shown and no drill has an indicator that lies. **Add a drill where it lies**:
   for example, defenders slide over (looks like zone), and at the snap the slid defender plays
   tight man on the motion man; ask what gave it away. Or show a spin that backs out.
4. **NFL vs college vs CFL, and the 2024 change:** NFL/NCAA/CFL yes (the table is good). I could
   *not* explain what the cheat motion did that the 2024 language addressed (§2).
5. **The motion boom and why the series disagree:** yes. This section is the clearest in the
   chapter. The three-panel figure makes the rule ("never one axis") stick.
6. **Run the checklist:** yes. The card is clean and the "say it out loud" habit is good.

**Drills (attempted before opening the answers):**

- **Drill 1:** I said man (the NB travelled), one deep safety (FS rotated to the middle), SS at 8
  yards "some kind of extra underneath guy", and the offense likes H racing the NB to the flat with
  X one-on-one. That matched the answer except for the robber name and the "don't throw the cross
  into him" caution, which I learned from the answer. Good drill, right difficulty.
- **Drill 2:** I got it (jet sweep to Z) in about five seconds, because the title ("the jet they
  stopped respecting") and the caption ("linebackers don't move with him… end squeezes inside")
  hand over every cue. Retitle it neutrally and make the caption describe positions, not
  interpretations, so the reader has to notice that the edge is empty.
- **Drill 3:** I said man (a Mike has no reason to be out at the numbers) and a lighter middle, so
  a QB run or a throw to the back. Matched. Small error in the answer: "In zone, **the nickel** or
  the safety already standing on that side would simply have taken the back". The NB is on the
  *left* and the back motioned *right*, so only the SS is on that side.
- **Overall:** all three drills check whether the indicator tells the truth. None makes me deal
  with it lying, which is the chapter's most important caveat.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- **What does an exchange or hand-off look like?** I'd like one small static diagram next to the
  travel and bump strips: man coverage, jet motion, the right corner stays, and the left-side
  defender picks Z up as he arrives.
- **What can I actually see on the standard broadcast angle?** The far-side corner and the
  safeties are often off screen at the snap. Which part of the indicator is readable live, and
  which needs the replay or All-22? (01-06 set up broadcast limits; one sentence here would
  connect them.)
- **Does the defense's reaction differ for motion toward vs away from the strength**, or for a
  back vs a receiver? The RB version is covered nicely. What about a TE motioning?
- **How does the QB use the answer in two seconds?** "Kill call / check" is mentioned once. A
  one-line example ("vs man he throws the rub; vs zone he throws the hole between the
  linebackers") would make job 1 concrete. The "Why it exists" box comes close.
- **Shifts are in the title, but the chapter is ~90% motion.** Apart from the trade, the shift to
  bunch, and the Landry shift as history, I don't learn why an offense would shift (several men
  moving, then a full one-second stop) rather than motion. What does a shift buy that motion
  doesn't? (It moves several players, such as the TE and the back, and can create formations
  motion can't. But you lose the running start.)
- **Is the indicator still useful when the offense motions on 60%+ of plays?** If every team
  motions, defenses have had years to drill disguises. Is there any evidence of the indicator
  getting less reliable? It would be fine to defer this to 06-06 or 13-04 with an explicit
  pointer.
