# Beginner review: 12-03 Case Study: The Shanahan Tree

Reviewer persona: casual fan who has never played. I've read Parts 1–11 in order, plus 12-01 and 12-02,
but not Part 13. I read the qmd start to finish and the rendered PDF
(`pdfs/12-03-shanahan-wide-zone-tree.pdf`, 23 pp., built 07:10, after the qmd's 07:09 save). I looked
at pp. 6 and 8 at 130 dpi and at the chart and drill pages at 110 dpi.

Overall this chapter is very good. The hook pays off. "Four ingredients that are one idea" is the
clearest statement of a system anywhere in the book. The YACOE section (the worked example, then the
nflverse-vs-NGS contrast) is the best stats explainer since 01-02. The QB debate is honest. The big
problems are these:

- **The logic of the QB section contradicts itself.** The text says the 49ers were 1st on third-and-long, and it also says the system "can't supply the throw outside the design". The Goff takeaway draws the wrong lesson from the figure.
- **The frame strip doesn't show two things its caption claims.**
- **It re-teaches 08-02's crash/squeeze lesson** without saying 08-02 already taught it, so Drill 3 is a memory test rather than a new problem.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained (or never explained)

- **The aiming-point mark is a letter X**, in the fig-bear-wide-zone caption: "The back (green ring) aims at
  the Y's outside leg (X)." X is also the split end's label, and the X receiver is on the same
  picture. I first looked for the X *player*. Call it "the green ✕" or "the green cross", or say "aiming point
  (green ✕)".
- **"H"** in Drill 2 (fig-predict-2). The chapter's own legend ("How to read the diagrams") lists X, Z, Y, R
  and F but not H. 02-02 taught H as a move-player letter, but the legend should list it, or the caption
  should say "H, the third receiver".
- **Technique numbers 1 and 5** (Drill 2 caption, "a 3, a 1 and a 5") aren't in the legend, which
  lists only 0, 3 and 9. I know them from 03-01, but the legend sets them up as a complete key.
- **"E" vs "9"** on the bear figures. The legend says "E is an end", but the 9 on the other side is an end too.
  Why is only one of them numbered? A beginner reads E and 9 as different kinds of player. One clause would fix
  it: "the backside end is labelled E rather than by technique so you can track him."
- **"simulated, BDB-format: positions in Big Data Bowl coordinates generated by gridiron"** in the
  fig-boot-strip caption. I haven't reached 13-05, and I don't know what the Big Data Bowl or "gridiron" is. For
  this reader, "simulated (a computer-generated version of the play, in the same format as the NFL's real
  tracking data)" is enough.
- **"slice"** ("*slice* across the formation into the flat") is italicised as if it's a term, but it's never
  defined as a block versus a release. Later the chapter uses "slices across and *blocks* the end" (Answer 3), so
  slice means both a route and a block. Say which.
- **"split zone"** ("kick out an end on split zone"), and **counter, duo** ("gap runs (counter, duo)"): 03-02 and
  03-03 own these, but here they're unlinked and unglossed. I had to remember them from forty chapters ago.
  Add links.
- **"the alley"** ("the man who would otherwise meet the back in the alley"). 05-05 owns alley player; here it
  isn't linked.
- **"passer rating"** (Purdy "led the league in passer rating"). 13-02 owns it; give it a one-clause gloss or drop it
  (yards per attempt is enough).
- **FTN**, used six-plus times ("By FTN's charting"). I did meet it in 02-06, but 50 chapters ago. Add
  a one-line reminder at first use ("FTN, a charting service that tags every play by hand").
- **"Shanahan-Kubiak core"** (fig-fingerprint legend and caption) is a new label used before the tree
  section that explains it. Why is Kubiak's name in it? Why are the Packers and Dolphins "core" while the Vikings are
  "McVay's staffs"? The chart comes before the section that answers these questions.
- **Falcons and Texans as family members.** They're green dots in fig-fingerprint, but their play-callers (Zac Robinson,
  Nick Caley) appear only in a footnote. The text then says "Atlanta in 2025 was under center less than almost
  anyone" as if I knew why Atlanta counts. Give it one sentence in the text.

## 2. Leaps and missing "whys"

- **Third-and-long contradicts "the throw outside the design."** The section "The debate" ends: "What the
  system cannot fully supply is the throw outside the design: the third-and-11 where the coverage was
  right..." Two paragraphs later: "on third down with 7 or more yards to go ... the 49ers' dropbacks averaged
  +0.18 EPA, 1st of 32 teams ... it wasn't that the system has no answer on third-and-long." Which is it? As a
  reader I can't square these. Either (a) explain that the 49ers' third-and-long success is still *designed*
  (schemed throws, YAC) and the limit is about broken plays specifically, or (b) soften one of the two claims.
  As written, the data section refutes the "honest summary" it follows.
- **The Goff takeaway draws the wrong lesson.** Takeaway 4: "Goff's Detroit years and the 2021 Stafford
  trade show the limit: the system can't supply the throw outside the design." But fig-same-qb shows Goff
  *as good or better* in Detroit (+0.13 vs +0.12), and the caption explains why: Detroit's coordinator "borrowed
  heavily from the same ideas". That's evidence that Goff was fine in a cousin system. It says nothing about a
  "limit". The Stafford-trade argument is asserted with no number. If the point is "McVay thought Goff
  couldn't create off-script", show something (Stafford vs Goff on third-and-long, or on pressured
  dropbacks), or say plainly that it's the coaches' judgment.
- **Throwing on the move is "easier"?** "He also throws on the move, away from the rush, which is easier to
  protect." Everything I learned in 04-05/04-07 says throwing on the run is *harder* for accuracy. The chapter
  should name that trade: the read gets simpler, the throw gets harder, so the routes are short. Also,
  "easier to protect" is unexplained. Is it because rushers have farther to run?
- **The bear loads the box, but the answer in "How defenses answered" #2 is a light box.** The signature section
  says the bear's eight-man box takes away wide zone. Answer #2 says the most important defensive answer was
  two safeties deep with a *light* box. I wondered: which one actually works? Put them side by side in
  one sentence. The bear wins the run and gives up the shot. Two-high takes away play-action and the boot and
  gives up runs, so it's a bet that your front can hold.
- **How does the jet-motion man block the edge?** Drill 2's caption says the Z's motion "ends ... just behind
  the quarterback" at the snap. Answer 2 says running toward the motion "puts the Z's block ... on the play
  side". How does a player who is behind the QB at the snap block anybody on the edge? Say that he keeps running
  through the mesh and out to the edge.
- **"the boots and keepers that FTN counts with them"** (fingerprint, first surprise). I couldn't parse this.
  Does it mean FTN counts boots as play-action? If so, how does that explain a *low* play-action rate? This is
  the one sentence that's meant to resolve the chapter's biggest surprise (the 49ers rank 21st in play-action),
  and it doesn't.
- **"the figure is in the fingerprint section below"** (end of the Motion ingredient). There's no motion figure.
  The fingerprint section gives motion ranks only in prose. Either add motion to a chart or change "figure" to
  "numbers".
- **The jump from 1998 to 2017.** "Two problems, thirty years apart" goes from Elway straight to Kyle in 2017. The
  bridge (Kubiak's Houston, Washington 2010–13, the 2016 Falcons) only arrives in the tree section, 10 pages
  later. The first learning objective ("Trace ... from Gibbs ... to Kyle Shanahan's 49ers") makes me wait too long
  for it. One linking paragraph in section 1 would fix this.
- **Why a 21-personnel bootleg instead of 12?** The 04-05 boot used two tight ends. The chapter says
  the fullback can "block the backside end on a protected bootleg" but never says what the *fullback* adds
  to the boot that a second tight end doesn't (the slice from the backfield, his angle).
- **"one fewer defender against it"** (Answer 3). If the end "squeezed down the line slowly", he's still on
  the backside, where the cutback goes. Why is he out of the pursuit *altogether*? 03-02 taught me the
  backside end is the cutback defender. The answer needs to say what happens to the cutback when he squeezes.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **fig-bear-wide-zone (p. 6):**
  - The QB's path arrow points down and right and ends right at F. It reads as "the QB pitches to the fullback."
    The handoff to R isn't visible.
  - R's path (the "thick dark arrow" per the legend) runs from R through F's marker and roughly alongside F's
    own path toward the SS. At print size I can't tell which line is the ball carrier's and which is F's lead
    block. R's line doesn't look thicker.
  - The C's purple ring is almost hidden by the LG and RG markers. I found it only after the caption told me.
  - The caption doesn't mention the X (straight vertical arrow) or the Z (T-bar on the right CB), so I wondered
    whether they're part of the lesson. Half a sentence would do it: "X runs off his corner, Z blocks the other one."
- **fig-boot-strip (p. 8):**
  - **Frame 3 doesn't show what the caption claims.** "Three receivers at three depths on the boot side: the
    fullback in the flat, the Y crossing at about 7 yards, the Z on a deep over route." In frame 3 the Z is in the
    top-right corner, on the *opposite* side from the boot, and the X is out of frame. The flood I was told to see
    isn't there. Either widen the window, move the frame later, or change the note.
  - **Frames 3 and 4 seem to contradict the keeper claim.** The caption says "Nobody is in front of the
    quarterback, so the keeper ... is open too," but in frames 3 and 4 the ringed E is two or three yards from the Q.
    A beginner sees a defensive end about to sack the quarterback. The geometry is right (E is inside and
    behind him), but nothing says so. Add a frame note ("E behind the QB, can't catch him"), or draw the end's
    lost-contain angle.
  - Frame 2: the Y is already level with M on the *right* at +1.4 s. "Crossing behind the linebackers" isn't
    visible until frame 3.
  - R vanishes after frame 2 (in frame 3 he's under the 9, in frame 4 he's gone). Minor, but I wondered where
    the fake went.
  - "the Will trailing" (frame 4): W is about 2 yards from Y and level with him, so it reads as "the Will is
    covering him". Caption and picture disagree about how open the Y is.
- **fig-same-qb (p. 10):** Goff's blue label "+0.12 (2,374)" sits on the dashed league-average line. The league
  average is never given as a number, so I can't say whether +0.10 is good.
- **fig-fingerprint (p. 11):** the dashed median lines aren't labelled on the chart (only in the caption). The
  "Texans" label floats between a grey dot and a green dot. The caption's "2024–2025" contradicts the x-axis
  label "2025" until you work out that the arrow is the only 2024 element.
- **fig-staff-moves (p. 16):** it reads well. Klay and Klint Kubiak are a trap. Their rows are three apart, and both
  read "Gary Kubiak's son; 49ers staff". Make the difference explicit, e.g. bold the first names, or say "younger
  brother" / "older brother".
- **fig-predict-2:** the caption and drill say "receivers in tight splits", but H and X are about 10 yards from the
  ball. I don't know what "tight" means numerically, so the picture doesn't teach it.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Bear section bullets vs the Why it exists box:** the third bullet ("Make that choice expensive... left free
  on purpose... the bootleg is built to beat") is repeated almost word for word in the callout straight after it.
- **This chapter re-teaches 08-02 without saying so.** 08-02's constraint-tree figure and its crash-vs-squeeze
  figure already taught me that "the naked boot beats the crash and dies against the squeeze", with the same E,
  the same red ring and the same wording: "eyes on the quarterback". 12-03's "What if the end *doesn't* chase?"
  paragraph and Drill 3 repeat that lesson and never use 08-02's words *crash* and *squeeze*. Use them, link them
  ("08-02's squeeze"), and spend the saved space on what is new here: the bear front.
- **"Illusion of complexity"** is defined or restated five times: You'll need, the motion ingredient, "Why the four are one
  idea", What survives and Takeaways.
- **The return of under center** is told four times: You'll need, the fingerprint prose, What survives and
  Takeaways. The motion stats are given three times (FTN ranks, ESPN's 59%, PFF's 63.9%), each with a
  different-source caveat. That's correct, but by the third source I was tired.
- **Answer 3 restates the body paragraph** almost exactly, so the drill gives me nothing new to think about.

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? (drills attempted before opening answers)

- **Trace Gibbs to Kyle:** partly. I can name Gibbs, Mike Shanahan and Kubiak in Denver, then Kyle in 2017. I can
  rebuild the middle (Houston, Washington, Atlanta) only after the tree section, not from "Two problems".
- **The four ingredients as one idea:** yes, confidently. This is the chapter's best section.
- **Diagram wide zone vs bear, and call boot vs run from the end:** yes for the call. For the diagram, I could draw
  the front and the unblocked E, but I couldn't reproduce the RT/RG overtake from fig-bear-wide-zone (see §3).
- **The QB-efficiency debate:** I can state both sides, but the contradiction in §2 leaves me unsure what the
  chapter's verdict actually is.
- **Read the fingerprint:** yes for under center, play-action and YACOE. 21 personnel and motion have no chart, so I can
  only quote them.
- **Name 2025 vs 2026 play-callers:** mostly. 2026 has three unconfirmed play-callers (LV, ARI, LAC). In October
  2026, five weeks into the season, "the team had not confirmed who calls them" feels odd. A reader watching
  in October would expect to know. Also, Takeaway 6 lists 2025 play-calling as five teams, while the text says
  eight (it adds MIN, JAX and CIN).

**Drills, before opening the answers:**

- **Drill 1:** I answered "naked boot right, with the keeper, because the right-side end has been crashing."
  **Correct.** It was easy, almost too easy: the prompt says "crashed hard" and the whole chapter trains exactly
  this read. It works as a confidence builder.
- **Drill 2:** I answered "run, because of two-high and six in the box. Wide zone left toward the motion, and
  nobody following the motion means zone coverage." **Correct.** But the answer accepts "wide zone left (or the jet
  sweep itself)", and an either/or answer is hard to get wrong. I also guessed "jet sweep" first and wasn't sure
  whether that counted.
- **Drill 3:** I answered "run wide zone again, or a protected boot." **Correct, but only because the body paragraph
  "What if the end doesn't chase?" gives the answer word for word**, and 08-02 taught it too. This is recall,
  not transfer. A better Drill 3 would test something the chapter taught but didn't drill: for example, a two-high
  shell with a safety rotating down on motion (when is the play-action shot coming?), or a tite front (which run?).

## 6. What I still wonder (knowledge gaps this chapter should answer)

- **How does the QB decide between keeping it and throwing on the keeper?** "if nobody comes up to stop him, the
  quarterback runs" is all I get. Who is "nobody"? Which defender is the QB reading?
- **What does this offense do on obvious passing downs, in design terms?** The data says it's 1st on third-and-7+, but
  the chapter never shows a single Shanahan dropback concept. After four ingredients that are all about faking
  the run, I don't know what the offense looks like when it can't fake.
- **How often do the 49ers actually run wide zone, compared with other runs?** The chapter implies a lot, and the
  Defenses section mentions counter and duo from the same looks. One number or rough share would help.
- **Is the 2026 tree working?** The chapter is dated October 2026 but stops at 2025. One sentence saying "too early to
  judge; 2025 data only" would stop me hunting for it.
- **What counts as a good YACOE?** The team chart gives a range (−0.7 to +0.9), but a sentence on a single receiver's
  scale would help (Deebo's +5.2 dwarfs any team number; is that normal?).
- **Why does the fullback line up offset to the tight end's side, and what tips it off when he doesn't?** The
  Watch-for-it box asks me to record it but never tells me why it matters.
