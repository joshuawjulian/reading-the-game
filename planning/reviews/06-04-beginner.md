# 06-04 Cover 2 and Tampa 2: beginner reader review

Reviewer role: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read 01-01 to 06-03 in order.
I read the qmd start to finish and looked at all 21 PDF pages (60 dpi, plus 130-140 dpi crops of
Figures 5, 6 and 7). Overall: strong chapter. The opening question pays off, the hard-corner
section is excellent, and smash-vs-Cover-2 is the clearest "one defender, two jobs" demo so far.
The problems below are mostly arithmetic and wording slips a numerate reader trips on, two
frame-strip panels that don't show what the caption claims, and a Predict-the-play set that is
too easy. The chapter runs about 8,750 words of prose against a 4,500 target.

## Must fix (a careful reader will trip on these)

1. **Tampa 2 arithmetic doesn't add up.** Fig 6 caption: "Five defenders underneath, three deep
   after the first second: that is the Tampa 2 bargain." 5 + 3 = 8, but the chapter has drilled
   in "four rush, seven cover." The "Why it exists: Tampa 2" box makes it worse: "without giving
   up Cover 2's best feature, five defenders underneath". Once the Mike runs, there are four
   underneath. The body sentence "without giving up a fifth underneath defender until the Mike is
   already past the short routes" is the right idea, but I had to read it three times. Say it
   plainly: *five under at the snap, then four under and three deep once the Mike clears the short
   zones.*
2. **Fig 6 (Tampa 2 strip), last panel.**
   - The caption says "+3.0 s" but the panel is titled "+3.3 s".
   - The caption says the back is "in the left flat" and the corner is "waiting there" and "makes
     the tackle". In the panel, R is about 3 yards *behind* the line, inside the numbers, and the
     left CB is still about 10 yards away from him. No tackle is shown and he isn't in the flat.
   - Y is hidden under the Mike's highlight ring at +3.3, so I lost track of the receiver the whole
     strip is about. Only his trail is visible.
   - The strip never shows the cost the text names next ("the middle at 10 to 15 yards, in front
     of the running Mike"). H's curl sits at about 8 yards next to the Will, not in the vacated
     space. One panel or a marked green spot would make the trade visible.
   - The full-width field in a half-width panel makes every marker tiny in print.
3. **"Hole" means two opposite things.** In Fig 1, "hole" labels the Mike's *defended* zone (from
   06-01). In Fig 3 and the text, "hole shot", "sideline hole" and "the hole in Cover 2" are
   *undefended* spots. Then "hole ③, the deep middle" sits right behind the "hole" zone. One line
   would fix it: "the 'hole' zone is a defended area; 'a hole in the coverage' is an empty one."
4. **Smash depths disagree.** The text says the inside receiver "runs 10 or 12 yards up the field
   and breaks". The Fig 5 caption says "Y has broken toward the sideline at 8 or 9 yards". The code
   breaks at 10.
5. **"Squat" is used two ways.** The hard-corner section defines squat as step 3 (stop, plant and
   come forward on a flat threat). But the Fig 5 panel 1 title is "Snap · corner squats outside Z",
   the Fig 6 text has corners "squatting" before the snap, and "squat corner" names an alignment.
   Pick one meaning for the verb, or say that "squat" names both the alignment and the move.
6. **2-Man pre-snap: the figure, text and checklist give three different messages.** Fig 7 and
   Predict 3 teach a visible pre-snap difference: corners pressed at about 1 yard on the inside
   shoulder. The text says "Before the snap they can look identical, and good defenses make sure
   they do." Watch-for-it says "Corners on the receiver's inside shoulder ... Think 2-Man." Say
   that press with inside leverage is a *lean*, and that disguised 2-Man can come from the Cover 2
   squat look.

## (1) Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

- **fade**: used about six times ("go or fade route", "a fade is caught 20 or 22 yards downfield",
  the hole-shot glossary entry). It is never defined or linked; the index owner is 10-03, later in
  the book. Needs a gloss ("a go route thrown toward the sideline, away from the defender") or
  should be swapped for "go route" (04-01).
- **"Cover 5 ... (in the old numbering, five underneath players in man plus two deep)"**: which
  old numbering? 06-01 taught Cover 0-9 as roughly the number of deep defenders, so "5" looks
  random. One clear sentence on why it's 5 is needed.
- **"carries #3 vertical"** (What survived): neither "carry" nor "#3 vertical" is glossed here.
  02-04 taught #1/#2/#3 a long time ago, so a reminder would help.
- **"inside shade"** (Fig 7 pointer): "shade" was taught in 03-01 as a defensive-line alignment.
  Write "inside shoulder" to match the caption.
- **"point of emphasis"** ("made downfield contact past 5 yards a point of emphasis"): officiating
  jargon, not glossed.
- **passer rating** ("a 48.4 passer rating against it"): owned by 13-02 (later). It needs a few
  words, such as "an efficiency score where about 90 is league average".
- **"Steel Curtain"**, **"the chuck rule"**: the link points to 11-01, a later chapter, and the
  verb "chuck" is never defined. The Go-deeper box explains the rule, but not the word.
- **"curl-hook"** here vs **"hook-curl"** in 06-03 for the same area. 06-01 taught curl and hook
  as two zones; the merge is explained only in a bullet after Fig 1. Use one spelling across the
  two chapters.
- **flat depth**: "You'll need" says the flat is "0–6 yards deep", but Fig 1 drops the corners to
  8.5 yards and Fig 3 says "corners sit at 8 yards". Say that the Cover 2 corner sinks to the top
  of the flat.
- Minor: "pipe" is fine (glossed). The `trail technique` recap credits Man Coverage, but the index
  owner is 06-01. That doesn't matter to a reader.

## (2) Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

- **Hole-shot race ends in a tie.** "He needs close to 3 seconds; a well-timed fade is caught at
  about the same moment." So who wins by default? The next sentence says that anything that holds
  him a beat makes him lose. Give the throw's time (about 2.5 s) so the margin is explicit.
- **Why is 2-Man better than Cover 2 on 3rd-and-8?** The misconception box says Cover 2 corners are
  "the *most* aggressive in football against short throws". Then the 2-Man section says "zone
  coverage's habit of conceding the short completion and tackling isn't enough". Name the gap:
  receivers sitting at 8-9 yards in the seams between zone drops, right at the sticks.
- **Why did Bob Sanders fix the Colts' run defense?** "The injured strong safety Bob Sanders came
  back for the playoffs, and the same defense got stingy." The chapter has just called light boxes
  the cost of two-deep. Did Sanders play in the box, making it no longer Cover 2? One sentence on
  the mechanism is missing.
- **Smash: what is the nickel (curl-hook defender) doing?** In Fig 5, Y runs right past $ at 9-10
  yards and breaks behind him. Why can't $ carry him? The defense-fights-back list never mentions
  the curl-hook player.
- **Four thin spots, or three?** Fig 3 and the ② text say the smash corner is "the same strip of
  grass" as the hole shot, and the two ovals sit at almost the same depth and width, one on each
  side. It reads as one spot reached two ways. Either say "four ways to attack three places" or
  make the ovals visibly different (② deeper and further inside).
- **Cover 2 against trips is never taught,** but Predict 2 needs it ("the safety on the trips side
  has three receivers in his half"). Who takes #3 underneath? Does the shell shift? A short
  paragraph or a diagram is needed before the drill.
- **Telling Cover 2 from quarters before the snap.** Both are two-high. The text mentions only that
  quarters safeties are narrower, and Watch-for-it's "Corners 7 or 8 off ... Probably not Cover 2"
  leaves me with "so what is it?" A one-line forward pointer would close it.
- **Data claim strength.** "worth more on average than the same throws against Cover 1 or Cover 3"
  rests on +0.65 vs +0.61 (NGS) with no interval. The Cover 2-vs-Cover 3 gap is within noise for
  about 750 throws. Either show error bars or soften to "at least as valuable". The takeaway
  "Deep passes go to the middle against Cover 2 more often than against any other coverage" is
  based on only four coverages, and the FTN figures have Cover 2 and quarters tied (29% vs 28%).
- **Cover 2 share, one in seven or one in five?** The text says "roughly one dropback in seven"
  (NGS 13.9%), but the FTN line in Fig 8 sits at about 14-22%, and the reconciliation (23% in 2025)
  is only in a footnote. Put the two-sources caveat in the sentence itself.

**Predict the play: my answers before opening.**
- **P1:** "Cover 2. Call smash, or a go route with a seam inside." **Correct.** But the picture is
  almost identical to Fig 1's pre-snap and the caption points to the tells, so it tests memory,
  not reading. Try ambiguous corner depth (6-7 yards), or one corner squat and one off (the cloud
  look from 06-03), to make me discriminate.
- **P2:** "Plain Cover 2: nobody, Y splits the safeties. Tampa 2: the Mike runs with him; then hit
  the dig behind the Mike. The offense prefers plain Cover 2." **Correct.** The last sub-question
  is trivial. The interesting part, how Cover 2 handles trips, was never taught (see Leaps).
- **P3:** "2-Man; beat it with a QB scramble and with out-breaking or rub routes." **Correct.** But
  the diagram is Fig 7 again with the arrows removed, and the two answers are the "What 2-Man gives
  up" bullets one page earlier. This is recall. A harder version: a two-high look with corners at
  5 yards outside on 3rd-and-8, asking "Cover 2 or 2-Man, and what would you check after the snap?"
- None of the three tests Tampa 2 *recognition* ("what do you watch at one second?"), which is
  the chapter's headline skill.

## (6) What I still wonder (the chapter should answer)

1. How does Cover 2 adjust to trips or 3x1? Who has #3 if he runs a seam in *plain* Cover 2?
2. How do I tell Cover 2 from quarters before the snap? Just safety width? Corner depth?
3. In Tampa 2, how does the Mike know it's a pass (his key: guards? the QB's drop?) quickly enough
   not to get burned by a draw?
4. What does the curl-hook defender do against smash, and against a seam that splits the safeties
   in plain Cover 2?
5. Against a run, what does the "spare" safety do in Cover 2? Is the defense really one defender
   short in the box, and how short in numbers against 11 personnel?
6. Is Cover 2 played mostly from nickel today or from base? Who replaced the "Sam"?
7. Does 2-Man rush only four, or does it ever add a fifth (the Mike on the RB blitzing if the back
   blocks)?
