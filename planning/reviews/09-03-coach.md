# Coach / film review: 09-03 Contact Rules and Penalties as Strategy

Reviewed 2026-10-08 against the current `pdfs/09-03-contact-rules-and-penalties.pdf` (24 pages).
I looked at every figure at 110 dpi. The fact-check (`09-03-factcheck.md`) already verified the
rule text against the 2026 rulebook, so this review covers football accuracy, nuance a coach would
insist on, and the diagrams. Items marked *(verify)* are rule details I am confident of as a coach
but that the writer should confirm in the 2026 rulebook before printing.

Overall: strong. The price-list framing is exactly how staffs talk about penalties, the
enforcement section is accurate, and the data figures (fig-deep-dpi, fig-penalty-epa, tbl-bydown)
are excellent. The fixes below are one real overstatement in the rules text, one impossible
diagram, and a set of diagram-realism and legibility problems.

## A. Rules and explanations (priority order)

1. **"Pick plays are legal only inside the first yard" overstates the rule. This is the biggest
   technical fix.** OPI needs the offensive player to *initiate* the contact: push off, or block
   or obstruct a defender before the pass is touched. A receiver who just runs his route while a
   defender has to go around him, or runs into him, has not fouled at any depth. That is how
   mesh, drive and other crossers make "natural rubs" at 5–6 yards every week. The one-yard strip
   matters because inside it even a deliberate pick is legal; beyond it the officials ask who
   initiated the contact and whether the receiver was running a route or blocking. Fix it in four
   places:
   - "The one-yard strip" section: replace "That one-yard strip is why rub routes are legal at
     all" with something like "Inside that strip even a deliberate pick is legal. Beyond it, a rub
     is legal only if the receiver keeps running his route and the defender runs into *him*. If
     the receiver initiates the contact (stops, turns his shoulders, extends his arms), it's OPI."
     The paragraph after the figure, on the officials' giveaways, then supports this directly.
   - Takeaways bullet 2: "pick plays are legal only inside the first yard" becomes "deliberate
     picks are legal only inside the first yard; beyond it the receiver must not initiate the
     contact."
   - Predict 3 answer, the "Legal if…" paragraph: add one sentence on the natural rub.
   - Optional: one line linking to mesh in 04-04 as the everyday example of a legal rub at depth.

2. **Free plays: add the NFL "unabated / reaction" kill.** A defender in the neutral zone does
   not always give a free play. If an offensive player reacts (flinches), or the defender has an
   unabated path to the quarterback, the officials blow the play dead: neutral zone infraction,
   or encroachment if he touches someone. The free play only happens when the jumper draws no
   reaction and isn't unabated, typically a wide end who jumps and then gets blocked or retreats,
   or a 12th man. A coach would insist on this, and it is why quarterbacks snap it *fast* on a
   jump. Add two sentences to "Free plays" and the matching clause to the fig-free-play caption.
   Also note that the jumper in fig-free-play is the wide end and the LT blocks him, which is
   exactly the legal case, so the diagram becomes an example of the rule.

3. **Enforcement spot for offensive fouls on running plays.** The bullet says "spot of the foul …
   for fouls by the offense beyond the line on running plays", and the flowchart box 4 says
   "offense fouls downfield on runs". The NFL rule is: on a running play, an offensive foul
   *behind the basic spot* (the end of the run, or the line if the run ends behind it) is
   enforced from the spot of the foul. That includes holds *behind* the line. A hold 3 yards deep
   in the backfield on a run costs 13 yards, and that is how a hold in the end zone on a run
   becomes a safety. Suggested wording: "for offensive fouls on running plays that happen behind
   where the run ended (including behind the line), and on returns." Also soften "Holding costs
   ten yards, usually from the previous spot" to "from the previous spot on passes, and usually
   from the spot of the foul on runs." *(verify basic-spot wording in Rule 14)*

4. **Defensive holding on blockers.** The section is all about receivers. The defensive-holding
   flag a coach sees most from the box is a defensive lineman grabbing a blocker on a twist or
   stunt, to pick a guard so the looper can come free. Add one bullet or sentence. It ties to
   pass-protection games in 04-02.

5. **Ineligible downfield applies only to passes that cross the line.** Say so in "The lineman's
   one-yard limit, enforced". It is the reason screens let linemen release and lead downfield,
   and part of why RPO tags are built around screens and quick throws. Keep the fact-check's
   correction on OPI (8-5-1 "regardless of whether it crosses"). But confirm in 8-5-3 how
   receivers' blocks before a screen catch behind the line are treated, since fig-low-blocks
   panel 3 sits on a screen. *(verify)*

6. **Sliding QB: the committed-defender exception.** "Defenders must … pull up" needs the
   qualifier officials actually apply: a defender who was already committed to the tackle before
   the slide began is not flagged for unavoidable contact, unless it is forcible contact to the
   head or neck or is flagrant. This is the judgment behind most late-slide arguments on
   broadcasts. *(verify the note in 7-2-1 / 12-2-8)*

7. **Defensive foul on a touchdown.** "The offense declines and keeps the touchdown" is
   incomplete. The scoring team may instead have the yardage enforced on the try or on the
   kickoff, which coaches use to go for two from the 1 or to kick off from midfield. One sentence.
   *(verify current Rule 8/14 wording)*

8. **Block in the back: the close-line exception.** Above-the-waist blocks from behind are legal
   on linemen in the close-line play area (roughly tackle to tackle, near the line). That is why
   a guard who catches a slanting tackle from behind on a down block isn't flagged. One clause
   after the definition. *(verify Rule 12-1-3(b))*

9. **Hands to the face: add "forcible or sustained".** The NFL standard is forcible or prolonged
   contact to the head or neck. A hand that slides up and comes straight back down is not called.
   This is the line that decides whether an edge rusher's long-arm becomes a flag. *(verify Rule
   12-1-7 wording)*

10. **Low blocks in pass protection.** One line: running backs and linemen may legally cut a
    blitzer or a rusher inside the tight end box on a pass play, which is why you see backs "cut"
    a blitzing linebacker. Today the section reads as if cut blocks are only a zone-running tool.

11. **Face-guarding (optional, Common misconception).** A defender who waves his hands in the
    receiver's face without contact has not committed DPI in the NFL. Fans ask about this
    constantly, and it reinforces the point that PI requires contact.

12. **Hail Mary answer, small nuance.** Add that an offensive foul in the scrum (a push-off) ends
    the game, because a half *can* end on an offensive foul. So receivers are coached to box out,
    not shove.

Everything else checks out from a coaching standpoint: the 5-yard chuck rule and the pocket
exception, illegal contact vs holding vs DPI timing, the spot foul and the college/HS cap, the
frame and the "material restriction" test, the see-the-numbers coaching point, the chop and
peel-back history, the roughing categories and enforcement, the defenseless list, the helmet rule,
the horse-collar and hip-drop rules, the three-strike policy (with the AP/PFT discrepancy handled
honestly), accept/decline logic, half the distance, and the "what down" rules. Both film rooms are
well chosen and accurately characterized. The 2003 AFC Championship → 2004 emphasis story is the
right example of "the rule didn't change, its price did."

## B. Diagrams

**fig-contact-zone (p.3).** The left panel is good. In the right panel, the green label "both
playing the ball: contact is legal" floats in empty field at 7 yards and is attached to nothing,
so it reads as if contact at 7 yards is legal. Either drop it (the caption already says it) or
attach it with a leader to the catch point as a second, green "if C were turned for the ball"
note.

**fig-pick-yard (p.5).**
- H's "quick out" is drawn as a 1.6-yard diagonal stub that ends under the outside corner. Draw a
  real flat/arrow route: release at 1–2 yards depth to about 6–7 yards outside (w ≈ 16), and
  widen `LAT` to about (2, 19.5) so it fits with the label 1.5+ yd inside the edge.
- Z's slant, `path((1.0,-3.0),(4.5,-9.5))`, actually crosses the nickel's spot at about 1.2 yards
  depth, just beyond the strip it is meant to illustrate. Flatten the first step, e.g.
  `(0.6, -3.6)`, so the crossing is clearly inside 1 yard and matches the star.
- Show why H is open: draw the corner (C) following Z inside (a short defensive trail), so the flat
  is visibly vacated.
- The nickel at d = 1.0 on an H who is 1.5 yards off the ball has a 2.5-yard cushion, which is not
  "pressing". Either say "aligned tight on H" in the caption, or move H to d = -1.0 and the nickel
  to d = 0.8.

**fig-dpi-enforcement (p.7).** Correct and clear. No change.

**fig-dpi-movie strip (p.9).**
- The story doesn't read: in "+1.4 s · past him", C is level with or slightly *ahead of* X, and at
  the throw they are still level. X should have a visible step (1–1.5 yd) from +1.4 s on, since
  the caption says he "gets a step".
- The timing is slow: 37 yards in 5.6 s is about 6.6 yd/s on average. NFL receivers cover 40 yards
  in about 4.5 s. Suggest `pass_to("X", t≈2.5)` with the catch point around 4.4–4.7 s, and match
  the corner's `speed`, so the last panel becomes "+4.6 s · the grab".
- The right edge clips the DT "T" squares in all four panels. Widen `lateral` to about (-21, 4).

**fig-frame (p.10) and fig-passer (p.13).** Good. No change.

**fig-low-blocks (p.11).** The weakest figure. Three fixes per panel:
- *Panel 1 (legal cut):* the green star sits right on top of the highlighted "T" label, so the
  label is unreadable. Offset the star to the defender's knee side, (0.4, -2.0). The RB marker is
  clipped by the bottom edge: change `WIN` to about (-7.5, 8). The caption could add "the backside
  end is left unblocked", which is true to outside zone and explains the empty left side.
- *Panel 2 (chop):* illegible. Three rings, a star and two tiny block stubs overlap on one
  defender, and nothing shows the RT going *low*. Zoom to `lateral=(-3, 8)`. Draw RG's block as a
  short solid arrow into T1's chest labelled "high", and RT's as a dashed arrow aimed at T1's
  outside knee labelled "low". Put the labels on leaders above the line, not under the OL. Ring
  only T1.
- *Panel 3 (block in the back):* H starts *on* the line (d = 0) and there is no ball or QB, so
  "screen catch" is just a label. Start H about 1 yard behind the LOS and add a short ball arrow
  from a QB stub (or a ball marker at the catch point). Remove the stray orange tick at the start
  of the corner's trail (top right, near Z). The geometry itself (Z downfield of the corner, the
  corner facing the ball) is right.

**fig-free-play strip (p.16).**
- The offside can't be seen: in "Set" (-0.45 s) the end is already on the line. Start the first
  panel around -1.2 s with the E in his stance at d = 1.0, and add an arrow/note "jumps into the
  neutral zone" on panel 2.
- The panel-4 note "catch it, or decline and take 5 yards" is backwards: the offense declines
  *when it catches it*. Use "caught: decline the flag / incomplete or picked: take 5 yards".
- The right edge clips the DT squares and the QB in panel 4. Widen `lateral` to about (-20, 5).

**fig-half-distance (p.17), panel 3: impossible as drawn.** The line of scrimmage and the ball
are inside the end zone at -3. A snap can never be in the end zone. Redraw: ball and LOS at the 2,
the QB dropped into the end zone, with the flag/star at about 4 yards deep in the end zone and a
"hold in the end zone → safety" label. The caption is fine once the drawing matches.

**fig-flag-flow (p.18).** Update box 4 per A3 ("offense fouls behind the end of a run").

**fig-predict-hold (p.21).** The rusher's path goes from his wide alignment *inside* the LT and
ends on top of the running back. That is an inside move, not "beaten around the edge", and it
ends at the wrong player. Redraw the WDE as an edge arc: upfield to about 2 yards past the LT's
outside shoulder, then flattening to the QB at about QB depth (-5 to -6). Draw the LT kick-sliding
back and out, with the star/grab behind the rusher's near shoulder at about -4.5 depth. The rest
of the plate is realistic: two-high nickel on 3rd-and-8, Z's out at 9 past the sticks.

**fig-predict-hail (p.22).** The deep defenders are too shallow for a Hail Mary from the 42. FS
and SS at 33 are 9 yards short of the goal line, and the deepest DB is 4 short. Real Hail Mary
defense keeps the deep group at the goal line to a few yards deep in the end zone (or bails there
at the snap) so nobody gets behind it. Move FS/SS to about 40–41 and D to about 44 (in the end
zone), and keep labels at least 1.5 yd from the top edge (the window may need to go to 49). The
"goal line, 42 yards away" label sits 1.6 yd short of the line with no pointer; put it on the line
or add a tick.

**fig-predict-bunch (p.23).** Legal formation, good point-man bunch. Two nits. SS (w = 6.8) and
SDE (w = 4.3) nearly touch: move SDE to 4.0 and SS to 7.1. And with Y/Z 1.8 yards off the ball,
"pressed" defenders at 1 yard are about 2.8 yards away from them; set Y/Z at d = -1.2 so it reads
as press. The answer text is right apart from the A1 nuance.

**Charts (fig-deep-dpi, fig-penalty-rates, fig-penalty-epa, tbl-bydown).** Clean and correct.
Holding at 2.51 in 2024 matches the text. Illegal contact jumps to 0.35 a game in 2025. If there
was a 2025 point of emphasis, one clause in the caption would explain it; if there wasn't, the
"jumps around" wording is fine.

Two animations, both shown as frame strips in print, well under the limit of 4. Gridiron is used
sensibly. The chapter-local helpers (`snapshot`/`strip`, `no_numbers`, `say`, `flag`, `star`) are
