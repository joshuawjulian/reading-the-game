# 03-05 Perimeter Runs and Misdirection: beginner-reader review

Reader role-played: watches the NFL casually, never played, has read 01-01 through 03-04 in order (so
knows personnel, formations incl. Wing-T and condensed splits, the crack-toss animation from 02-04, jet/fly/orbit
motion and travel/bump/spin from 02-06, gaps/techniques/blocks from 03-01, zone and the backside end/bootleg
from 03-02, pullers/kick-out/log/squeeze/toss/trap/counter from 03-03, read keys and option from 03-04, EPA and
success rate from 01-02). Read the qmd start to finish and every page of `pdfs/03-05-perimeter-and-misdirection.pdf`
(20 pages, rasterized at 60 and 110 dpi).

Overall: strong chapter. The edge-as-a-race framing, the series diagram, the eye-candy "half a second is three
yards" arithmetic and the WR-carry chart are the best parts. The problems are a handful of genuine internal
contradictions (squeeze, how linebackers read, the seal), several diagrams that don't visibly show the thing the
caption tells you to notice, and heavy overlap with 02-04 and 02-06 that makes two of the three drills near-free.

## 2. Leaps (missing or assumed "why")

1. **How do linebackers read, the backfield or the linemen?** l.505: "Linebackers are taught to **read the
   backfield**, and a receiver sprinting across ... is the loudest thing in the backfield." l.984: "Linebackers
   are taught to **read the linemen** in front of them (their keys) ... not the receiver in motion." As written
   these contradict. Reconcile: e.g. "many linebackers key a lineman or the running back, but nobody can ignore a
   man at full speed ...".
2. **The seal (l.462-463):** "the tight end **can't reach around him**; instead Y gets his body on the end's
   **outside shoulder**". If he can't reach around him, how does he get to his outside shoulder? And the seal
   definition (l.415-416) says the TE gets "between that end and the sideline", i.e. outside him. I finished the
   paragraph not knowing which shoulder Y is on or why a 9-technique can't be reached.
3. **Why the tackle pulls here when the guard pulled in 02-04.** 02-04's crack-toss animation had the *guard*
   replace on the corner; here it's the right tackle "who has the shortest path". A reader who remembers 02-04
   will wonder if one of them is wrong. One sentence: "either can; teams that pull the tackle ...".
4. **Buck trap "indistinguishable for the first second" (l.801-802).** On the sweep *both* guards pull wide; on
   the trap *one* guard pulls a short distance inside. A linebacker reading the guards (l.746 says some do) would
   see a different picture. Why are the linebackers still fooled? Answer 2 asserts they "are about to vacate by
   chasing the guards" without explaining what the guards show them on the trap.
5. **Fig 1, right panel ("Defense flies to the ball"):** the caption says the defense wins the race, but the
   back's thick arrow still runs untouched to the second level, exactly as in the left panel. I couldn't see the
   race being lost. Shorten the back's path or stop it at the force defender.
6. **Rarity vs. the Rams (l.1080-1093 vs l.1133-1136).** The constraint section says "the yards come *from* the
   rarity" and "run it twenty times ... it would turn into a two-yard loss". The Film room then says the team that
   ran it *most* (2018 Rams, twice anybody else) got about +0.44 EPA per WR carry, among the league's best. That
   looks like evidence *against* the rarity claim, and the chapter never addresses it. Either show the
   team-volume vs. efficiency relationship (you have the data loaded) or explain why the Rams were the exception
   (they ran the fake far more than the real thing, as the Woods paragraph hints).
7. **"Eye candy is only as good as the last time the ball actually went there" (l.952)** then Woods "carried the
   ball only 19 times". So how often is enough? The chapter raises the dosage question twice and never gives even
   a rough answer (e.g. Rams ~2 real jets a game vs how many fakes; PFF or FTN motion data could give a ratio).
8. **The quarterback as lead blocker (l.669, Fig 4):** "the quarterback, who has nothing else to do, leads around
   the edge." A beginner's reaction is "quarterbacks block?". One clause: is it common, does he actually hit
   anyone, is it a risk teams accept?
9. **Answer 1 introduces a new concept:** "Many offenses let the quarterback choose between the jet and the
   inside run at the snap from exactly this read." A jet-or-run *read* by the QB is never taught in the body. If
   it's true and common, it belongs in the jet sweep section (it links to 03-04's option logic nicely);
   otherwise the drill question "Should the quarterback give Z the jet sweep?" assumes a decision the reader
   didn't know the QB makes.
10. **Answer 3 introduces "fake the crack and throw over him"** with no prior mention. Fine as a bonus, but say
    it's a pass concept covered later (04-x).
11. **Fly sweep vs. Wing-T origin.** The You'll-need box (l.308) says fly motion "is the Wing-T's name for the
    same thing" (as 02-06 taught); l.856-863 says the fly sweep "grew up beside" the Wing-T with separate
    origins (Beck, Speckman) and Wing-T coaches later adopted it. Not wrong, but the reader is left unsure
    whether jet/fly is a Wing-T child or a cousin. Say "adopted child" explicitly.
12. **The Madden callout (l.473-487)** frames the rule as "in a video game a receiver's block is a formality".
    That isn't a Madden misconception that changes how you watch, and the callout then delivers a dense
    rule-text paraphrase with three footnotes. The useful takeaway (legal crack = hands, chest-high, from the
    side; illegal = low or to the head; 15 yards) is buried at the end. Lead with it.

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

1. Who is the force player in common NFL looks (corner in Cover 2? safety in Cover 3?), and how can I tell from
   the broadcast angle before the snap?
2. Force vs. contain vs. "backside end": three names for edge jobs; how do they relate?
3. How often does a team need to actually give the jet for the fake to keep working? Any data on real-vs-fake
   jet ratios (e.g. FTN motion + carrier)?
4. Why did the Rams' high volume not kill the jet sweep in 2018, and did it decline afterwards (PFF's 2017-18
   numbers stop there)? Is there a league-wide WR-carry decline after defenses adjusted? Fig 9 shows WR EPA
   dipping in 2024; why?
5. Is the buck sweep run in the NFL today, by whom, and what does it look like from the sideline camera?
6. How common are reverses and double reverses in the modern NFL, and how do they fare (the chart lumps them
   with jets)?
7. How dangerous is the jet exchange really: is there fumble data for jet handoffs vs normal handoffs?
8. When a receiver cracks, does the corner always "crack-replace", or is that a specific coverage rule? What
   happens in man coverage when the corner is following Z?
9. Where do the pop/shovel jet passes fit: are they the same play to the defense, and should I count them when I
   log jets on Sunday?
