# 11-04 Dynasties and Counterpunches: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read only chapters 01-01 through 11-03 in
curriculum order, including 02-01, 03-04, 05-03, 06-03 and 06-04. Read start to finish in the PDF
(`pdfs/11-04-dynasties-and-counterpunches.pdf`, 18 pp., built after the latest .qmd edit) and in the source.
Line numbers refer to the .qmd file.

**Overall:** a strong chapter. The Manning-vs-Mike seam strip, the base/nickel table and the "find the player
with two jobs" principle are the high points, and the voice matches the pilots. Most problems are below the
level of the main argument: a hook that is never paid off, three logical contradictions a careful reader will
catch, two places where it repeats prerequisite chapters with *different numbers*, and some small diagram
legibility issues.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **The hook is never paid off.** The chapter opens with Super Bowl XLVIII and asks "what Seattle did to Manning" (L167-177), then promises the answer is the whole era. But the Seattle section (L793-811) is general. It never comes back to that game and never says *how* Cover 3 with press corners beat the 606-point offense: jamming within 5 yards to wreck Manning's timing, four-man pressure, tackling the short throws. I finished the chapter still not knowing what happened on February 2, 2014. Add a paragraph or a short film-room box that closes the loop.
2. **The chapter contradicts itself about who showed off the new passing game first.** L534-535 says "The first team to show the league what the new passing game could do… was not the Colts. It was the team that had beaten them." But L412-414 has just said Manning's 2004 season (49 TDs, 121.1 rating) was a record year in the first season of the emphasis. So the Colts showed it first. Did the Patriots wait until 2007? Why three years? Reword: "the first team to *rebuild itself* around it."
3. **The rule supposedly hurt Tampa 2, yet Tampa 2 won Super Bowl XLI.** L531-534 says the 2004 change "hurt his scheme, and the league's Tampa 2 copies started fading." But the timeline (Fig 1, 2006 row) shows "Dungy's Colts and Smith's Bears: a Tampa 2 Super Bowl (XLI)", two seasons *after* the rule. The text never squares the two, and a careful reader will notice.
4. **Why did Dungy leave Tampa, and who won Super Bowl XXXVII?** L301-314 credits Tampa 2 with the 2002 title and then says "Dungy moved to Indianapolis in 2002." Nothing says he was fired or that Jon Gruden coached the 2002 Bucs (06-04 says so, but a reader could easily assume Dungy won it). The film room's "the defense he had built" only hints at it.
5. **Did the Colts' offense practise against its own defense?** L314-316: "Manning practised against Dungy's Tampa 2 every day, and the Colts' offense… was built to beat exactly that kind of defense." It isn't obvious why a team would build its offense to beat its own defense. The real reason is that Tampa 2 copies were all over the league. That needs saying, and it should say that Dungy kept Tampa 2 *as the Colts' defense*.
6. **Why is every run worth negative EPA?** Fig 3 shows designed runs below zero in every season from 1999 to 2016. Dropbacks were also negative before 2004. A beginner's natural question is "then why does anyone run?" The chapter doesn't address it. One sentence would do (average EPA, situational value, keeping the defense honest), with a pointer to 13-02.
7. **The 2004 "jump" is partly a bounce back from a low 2003.** The right panel of Fig 3 shows 2002 at about 80.5, 2003 at 78.3 and 2004 at 82.8. The text (L508) says "rose from about 78 to about 83 in a single season" and never mentions that 2003 was itself a dip. The left panel has the same pattern. A reader looking at the chart sees this and starts to doubt the claim. Acknowledge it.
8. **How can the hybrid edge be both too light and big enough?** L759-762 says he is too light to hold up as a 4-3 end against the run on every down. L721 says he is big enough to set the edge against a 310-pound tackle. Why is the same job survivable in a 3-4? "Let the big three-man line do the heavy work" gestures at it but doesn't explain what changes about his run assignment.
9. **"Ten times a game" doesn't match the chart.** L908 ("exposes him to linebackers ten times a game") conflicts with Fig 6, where even Washington ran about 6 designed QB runs per 100 plays, roughly 4 a game. Either the figure includes read fakes and hits, in which case say so, or soften the number.
10. **"Peaked in 2013" doesn't match the chart.** L896-897 says the league rate peaked in 2013 at about 1 per 100 plays, but Fig 6 shows the league line at about 1.4 in 2020 and 2022. The text means a *local* peak, so say "peaked for that era."
11. **Why did tempo hurt Kelly's own defense?** L945-946 says the bill "landed on his own defense, which faced more plays." The mechanism (quick drives, less rest, more possessions for the opponent) is one clause and is missing.
12. **The 4th-and-2 numbers can't be checked.** L982-985 gives 79% vs 70% but not the conversion rate (about 60%) or the win chances in each branch. A beginner can't follow how the 79 is built, which undercuts the "shape of the argument" lesson. Show the three-line expected-value sum.
13. **Romer gets no concrete example.** L957-964 says teams "kicked far too often" but never gives a single situation (for example "4th-and-1 at midfield: go"). One example would make it concrete.
14. **The pattern table makes claims the body never argued.** In the row "The 3-4's hybrid edges → Quick passing, then the read option, which turns an edge rusher's aggression against him" (L1006), the body never presents the read option as an answer to hybrid edges. In the row "12 personnel with a move TE → … → Offenses built on tempo and college ideas" (L1005), the causal link is not explained anywhere.
15. **The Erhardt-Perkins link is thin.** L545-550: "That is what made the next two shapes possible." The reader has to take this on faith. Name one EP concept (for example "Ghost" or "Tare") and show that the same word works from 12 personnel and from empty.

## 3. Diagrams and captions

- **Fig 1 (timeline):** the chapter covers 2000-2016, but the timeline runs 2002-2015. The Patriots' 2001/2003/2004 Super Bowls, which are the "dynasty" in the title, are missing, the 2014 row is empty, and there is no 2016 row. "Read across a row to see a punch and the counterpunch" is only sometimes true, because counterpunches often land in a later season (2012 read-option, 2013 rule).
- **Fig 2 (seam strip):** this is the best figure in the chapter, and the story reads clearly in print. Two problems. (a) H also runs a vertical to about 22 yards, but the caption credits only "the outside go routes" with pulling the safeties, so I couldn't tell who was holding the FS. (b) In panel 4 the ball's dotted path sits on top of the Mike's dashed trail, which makes "Mike trails" hard to see. Offset one of them.
- **Fig 4 (12-personnel dilemma):** in the left panel, F's lead path and R's run path are both solid blue and cross near the right tackle and Y, so I couldn't tell which line belongs to whom without the pointer. Y's block on the end ("Gronkowski drives the end") is a tiny tee that is hard to see. In the right panel, F's route arrowhead ends at the W square, so at a glance it looks like the *Will* is moving.
- **Fig 5 (hybrid edge):** in the 3-4 panel both outside linebackers are labelled E, so the line reads E E N E E. That cuts against the caption's own point that the 3-4 calls him an outside linebacker. Label the ringed player "OLB" or "J"/"Jack", or at least leave the second OLB labelled differently. The "standing" label touches the dashed drop arrow.
- **Fig 6 (QB runs):** the text names Vick's 2006 Falcons (L893), but that dot (about 6.1, 2006) is not labelled. "Ravens (Jackson)" floats without a leader line. The caption says "dots above 8"; that is fine, but put a leader on the label.
- **Fig 8 (Drill 2):** the caption says "one safety deep in the middle," but two safeties are drawn. The SS at 9.5 yards and the Mike are unassigned and unexplained (a robber? a spy?). "Inside shoulder" leverage, which the answer depends on, isn't visible at this scale, because the corners look head-up.
- **Fig 9 (Drill 3):** the QB's keep path is drawn running *through* the ringed read defender, so it looks as if the QB runs into him. Route the keep outside him. H is in the picture with no job. The answer mentions "a lead blocker," but there isn't one in the picture.
- **Fig 7 (Drill 1)** is almost identical to the right panel of Fig 4 (same personnel, the same empty-ish spread, F in the left slot against the Will). It tests recall, not transfer. See section 5.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **The illegal-contact story is told twice, with different numbers.** 06-04 L1122-1130 already tells the Ty Law / point-of-emphasis / "irony: Dungy was the Colts' head coach" story, with foul counts **79 → 191**. This chapter says **76 → 190** (L429-430) and tells the irony again (L531-534). A reader of both chapters sees two sets of numbers. Pick one count, or say in the text that the sources differ. Cut the repeated irony, or point back to it ("as 06-04 noted…").
- **The designed-QB-run chart is a near-duplicate.** 03-04's `fig-qb-run-data` already charts designed QB runs from 2006 to 2025 with EPA. This one uses a different unit (per 100 plays rather than per team per game) and a different QB definition (50+ attempts rather than 10+), so the numbers I remember from 03-04 don't match. The team-season dots *do* add something (the "four offenses, not the league" point). Keep the chart, but say "03-04 showed the league trend; here are the teams," and reconcile the definitions or flag the difference.
- **Super Bowl 50 / Broncos hybrid edges** come up three times (Fig 1; L777-779; L922-924), and 05-03 already has it as a film room. Once here is enough.
- **"Punch and counterpunch"** appears in the subtitle, the intro, the Fig 1 caption, the pattern section and the table header. It feels thin by the fourth use.
- **Footnotes** ([^tylaw], [^34count], [^vrabel]) argue about sources at length. In print they take up half of pages 5 and 10. They are fine as sourcing, but they read as drag.

This **partly matched.** I got the 5-yard logic and the Will-on-TE matchup. I did not get "out-breaking routes against inside leverage" or crossers, because the chapter body never discusses press-man leverage, and the figure doesn't show the inside shoulder. The answer also says the defense moved to more zone and pressure, "part of why the zone-blitzing 3-4 spread," which is a good link, but it is new material in an answer box. One more wrinkle: the drill says this coverage "worked beautifully against the Colts in the 2003 playoffs," but the body describes New England's DBs bumping and holding, not "press man across the board." Is that the same thing? Make the body match.

**Drill 3 (unblocked edge, 2013 DC).** My prediction: scrape exchange (the edge crashes, a linebacker loops outside), slow-play, and "the QB can be hit like a runner." This **matched fully**, because all three are in the body (L910-920). The figure problem (the keep path running through the read defender) made me look twice. The answer's closing "hybrid edge… take on a lead blocker" refers to a blocker who isn't in the picture.

## 6. What I still wonder after reading

1. What exactly did Seattle do to Manning in Super Bowl XLVIII? (the hook)
2. Who coached the 2002 Bucs, and why did Dungy leave? How did a Tampa 2 Super Bowl (XLI) happen after the rule supposedly hurt Tampa 2?
3. Who were the Tampa 2 Mikes (for example Brian Urlacher)? The chapter says the Mikes were rare and the job was the whole scheme, but never names one.
4. What were the 2001-2004 Patriots, the first dynasty, actually built on? The chapter title promises dynasties, but the Patriots' three early titles get one sentence (L539-541).
5. Why do runs show negative EPA every year, and is running "bad"?
6. What is passer rating, and what is a good number?
7. How did the Brees/Payton Saints actually attack defenses? There is one paragraph, and it is mostly about Graham's contract. The spec lists them as an era example, but there is no concept.
8. Is the 12-personnel dilemma still alive today? The Watch-for-it box assumes it is. A modern example (Kittle in San Francisco, LaPorta in Detroit, or whoever fits FACTS-current) would bridge 2012 to Sunday.
9. The 2015 Broncos defense gets one sentence. What made it the best of the era beyond "Miller and Ware"?
10. Did anyone *outside* New England act on Romer or Burke before the late 2010s? The chapter says "a decade later," and I'd like one sentence on how the gap closed.
