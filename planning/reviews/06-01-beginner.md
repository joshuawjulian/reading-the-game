# 06-01 Coverage Fundamentals — beginner reader review

Reviewer persona: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read Parts 1–5 in order (incl. 02-05 A First Look at Defense, 04-01/04-03/04-04). Read start to finish in the PDF (`pdfs/06-01-coverage-fundamentals.pdf`, 18 pp, rasterized at 60/110/200 dpi) and the source.

**Overall:** strong, readable, and the "how many deep?" spine holds the chapter together. The zone map, the press/off/bail triptych and the dialect strip are clear. The real problems are (a) Fig 4, the spot-drop/match/man strip, which leaves out the moment the text tells me to watch, (b) a hash-width rule of thumb that the chapter's own diagrams contradict, (c) a deep-split figure that puts quarters safeties at 15 yards right after the text says 8–11, and (d) Predict-the-play drills that copy Fig 5's panels, so they test recognition, not reasoning.

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

1. **How does a spot-drop defender choose between his two areas?** The zone-map section says there are 7 underneath areas and 4 defenders, so each covers "two areas each, as 'curl-flat'… starting in one and sliding to the other." It never says *what triggers the slide*: the QB's eyes? #2 going to the flat? This is the mechanism that makes flood and curl-flat work, which 04-04 already taught from the offense's side. Fig 4 makes it worse: in the spot-drop row H is completely alone in the flat at +1.2 and +2.4 s, and the caption only talks about the dig. My reaction was "H is wide open, why isn't *that* the lesson?" One sentence would answer it ("the $ sinks under the curl first and breaks to the flat when the QB looks there; here the QB threw the dig").
2. **Fig 2 vs the text on quarters safety depth.** Fig 2 draws the quarters FS/SS at about 15.5 yd "at their deep landmarks". The next sections and Drill 2 then teach that quarters safeties line up at 8–11 yd, and that this depth is the tell. The caption does say "landmarks" (after the snap), but a beginner compares the pictures and gets confused. Either say "after the snap; before it they often line up at 8–11, see below" in the Fig 2 caption, or draw both.
3. **The "outside the hash means halves" rule contradicts the chapter's own diagrams.** "Watch for it" says: "how wide (outside the hash means halves)?" The tells list says the Cover 2 safety is "outside the hash" and the quarters safety is "narrower, over the slot or even over the tackle". But in Fig 5's quarters panel and Drill 2, the quarters safeties stand at 6.5 yd from the middle, more than 3 yd *outside* the hash (hash = 3.1 yd from the middle; the slots are at 9). Fig 5's own Cover 1/Cover 3 caption puts quarters safeties "a few yards inside the slot", which is still outside the hash. Applying the Sunday checklist to Drill 2, I'd call it halves. Fix the rule of thumb ("near the numbers = halves; over the slot = quarters") or move the drawn safeties.
4. **The shell is "usually honest", but the Eagles film room shows a shell that lies.** The "Why it exists: reading the shell" callout says the shell "is usually honest". The Fangio film room then says the Eagles showed MOFO "every time" and rotated underneath it. Neither gives a number. How often does a pre-snap two-high shell stay two-high? (See §6.) The 32.9% → 42.0% split-safety stat also doesn't say whether it's measured before or after the snap, which is exactly the distinction the chapter has just taught.
5. **"Eight players who can reach the run" (Answer 2)** has no count shown. 02-05 taught that two-high means a *lighter* box (usually six), and this answer says quarters gives eight. Spell it out: 4 DL + 2 LB + 2 safeties reading run (+ the $?).
6. **Option-route paragraph (Reading the shell):** "Middle open usually means a zone with five or three underneath". 2-Man was just listed as MOFO, so the reader asks: what does the receiver do against 2-Man? Add one clause.
7. **Bail figure vs the press definition:** press is "within about a yard", but the bail ghost is "a press-like alignment two yards off". Is 2 yards press or not? Say "a yard or two" consistently, or explain that bail corners usually show a slightly softer press.
8. **Trail technique** has no picture. The only visual is the Fig 4 Man +2.4 s frame, "the CB trailing on X's hip", where the CB is about a yard behind and outside X. That doesn't look "underneath" (inside/in front of the throw). A small inset of trail vs on-top would make "underneath" concrete.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, and captions that don't say what to notice

- **Fig 4 (spot-drop / pattern-match / man strip) is the biggest problem.** The text says: "Watch one defender in each row, the nickel ($), for the first second … did he start with him or pick him up after a beat?" The strip's frames are snap, +1.2 s and +2.4 s. At +1.2 s the $ is already on H in *both* the match row and the man row, so the one distinguishing moment (≈0.3–0.7 s: two drop steps vs going with H at once) is missing from print. The prose also describes things the strip can't show ("without a glance at him", "takes two drop steps"). **Add a +0.5 s column** (or replace +2.4 with +0.5 and move the end-state tags to the +1.2 frames). This is the chapter's "You'll be able to" skill #3, and in print it can't be learned.
  - Spot-drop prose: "he runs away from H to his curl landmark". In the frames the $ and H both move toward the sideline, the $ just goes deeper. "Away" reads wrong. Say "he sinks to his curl landmark, leaving H to run underneath him".
  - Spot-drop +2.4 s: "the dig sits between $ and W". The X marker is about 2 yards from the $ at the catch, so it looks *covered*. Widen the gap, or draw the window, so "open" is visible.
  - The caption names the W as the key player in the match row, but only CB and $ are ringed. Ring the W, or say "watch the W" in the match tag.
  - Pattern-match +2.4 s: the W's dashed trail is a scribble (up-left, then back, then up) that's hard to parse.
- **Fig 2 (deep splits):** the four faint squares at the line are the rushers, but the caption doesn't say so. I first read them as underneath defenders. The quarters safety depth issue is in §2.2.
- **Fig 5 (four shells):** the caption is eight lines long and repeats the bullet list right below it. Shorten it to "what to notice": *compare safety depth and width across the bottom row; compare corner depth across the top row*. The "N out" labels are in §1.
- **Fig 6 (dialect strip):** Cover 6 vs Cover 8 are unreadable as mirrors without a strength marker (§1). Cover 9 is drawn as thirds + "match under", but the text gives four meanings, including Madden's flipped Cover 6. Fine as "one common meaning", but name which one in the panel note ("drawn: Cover 3 match").
- **Fig 3 (press/off/bail):** clear. The only nit is the orange double meaning (§1).
- **Fig 1 (zone map):** clear. But the ball is in the middle, and on a broadcast it's usually on a hash; the text describes the hash case and no picture shows it. A small inset of the zones with the ball on the left hash would help skill #1.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **The shell section re-teaches 02-05.** 02-05 already named MOFC/MOFO, gave the one-high/two-high trade, and gave the corner tell ("one-high with the corners pressed is the classic man picture; … 7 or 8 yards off and turned to the quarterback usually means Cover 3"). 06-01 introduces MOFC/MOFO as if new ("Coaches and quarterbacks usually say the same thing in different words, and those words are worth owning"). Acknowledge 02-05 and go straight to what's new (depth/width).
- The **option-route explanation** is a third pass on something 04-01 and 04-04 both covered (04-04 has a figure for it). Cut it to a sentence and a link.
- **Corner tells appear five times:** Fig 5 caption, the tells list, Watch for it, Answer 1, Answer 3.
- **Cover 9** gets the glossary, a Fig 6 panel, a bullet with three meanings plus Madden, a chart bar, and a chart bullet, for a call the chapter itself calls "niche … 1.5 to 2.5 percent". The spec asks for it, but the data bullet could lose a sentence.
- **"The two charters disagree" bullet:** five numbers in two sentences (17.2, 13.9, 9.2, >20, ~10), with a parenthetical pointing back to a footnote source. For this reader the lesson is "labels are estimates". Keep one contrast (NGS 2025 Cover 2 13.9% vs FTN >20%) and move the rest to the footnote.
- **The Cody Alexander Cover 8 sentence** stacks a split-field definition, a name-drop, a direction and a parenthetical mirror. Split it.
- **Print layout:** p.11 is half empty because the chart floats to p.12, and each Predict drill sits on its own half-empty page (pp.15–17). Not a reader-comprehension issue, but the chapter feels long.

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? (drills attempted before reading answers)

**Drill 1 (off corners, one safety).** My answer: MOFC → Cover 1 or 3; corners off with outside leverage → Cover 3. Against four verticals, three deep vs four routes, so the seams are the target: the slot receivers (H, Y). **Correct.** But the picture is almost exactly Fig 5's "MOFC · off, eyes in (Cover 3 look)" panel, and the caption *tells* me the decisive cue ("shoulders turned in toward the quarterback", which the diagram can't show). I matched a picture; I didn't read one. I didn't think to add "the hitch in front of the corner is open" (the answer adds it), which is fair.

**Drill 2 (two safeties, close to the box).** My answer: MOFO; shallow (10 yd) → quarters, *but* I hesitated, because the Sunday checklist says "outside the hash means halves" and these safeties are visibly outside the hash marks (§2.3). I settled on quarters because the drill title says "close to the box" and the picture matches Fig 5's bottom-right panel. Thinnest: underneath outside (only three under), which I remembered from the deep-quarter bullet. **Correct, but partly for the wrong reason.** I also asked "who has Y? nobody is near him". The answer covers it (the safety reads #2), but only implicitly.

**Drill 3 (third-and-6, everybody tight).** My answer: Cover 1. Like routes that win at the break, crossers/rubs, back-shoulder vs trail. 5-yard rule: one legal jam, then he's chasing. **Mostly correct.** I didn't say "don't sit down in a soft spot", although the shell section's option-route paragraph had taught it. Again, the picture copies Fig 5's top-left panel.

**Recommendation:** at least one drill should be a picture that is *not* in Fig 5. Options: a two-high shell with Cover 2-deep safeties but off corners, so the cues conflict and the answer weighs them; ball on a hash; or a post-snap drill ("here is +0.8 s: spot-drop, match or man?"). That would test skill #3, which no drill tests now.

Per-objective verdict:

On a broadcast (sideline angle, ball on a hash, deep players off screen) the chapter gives no help beyond "use the high replay angle". There's also no post-snap cue for thirds vs quarters (what the corners do at the snap).
2. Press vs off, spot a bail, what each gives up: **yes.**
3. Spot-drop vs match vs man by watching one defender for one second: **not from the print edition** (Fig 4 has no frame inside that first second). From the prose alone, roughly.
4. Call MOFC/MOFO and use depth/width: **yes for depth; width is confused** by the hash rule (§2.3).
5. Decode Cover 0–9 and the dialects: **0–6 yes.** 7–9 I can repeat but not apply: "mirror" without a stated strength, Saban 6 = others' 9 left implicit.
6. 5-yard rule and corner play: **yes.** This section is very good.

## 6. Knowledge gaps (what I still wonder)

1. **How often does the pre-snap shell match the post-snap coverage?** The chapter's thesis is "hypothesis, then evidence", and its Watch-for-it drill asks me to check it, but it never gives a base rate. Is the split-safety 42% pre-snap or post-snap? If NGS publishes both, say so.
2. **What does each coverage look like from the TV angle?** A one-paragraph "on the broadcast camera" translation: the corners at the top/bottom of the screen, safeties often out of frame, what a bail looks like from the sideline view.
3. **What does a curl-flat defender actually read?** (§2.1)
4. **How do I tell Cover 3 from quarters *after* the snap,** when both may show off corners? (Cover 3 corners turn and bail to a third; quarters corners stay on #1 and read him.) One bullet in "Watch for it".
5. **Who calls the coverage, and can the defense change it at the line?** The Madden callout hints at calls and tags. The reader will wonder whether the defense checks coverage after seeing the formation (a one-line forward link is enough).
6. **Where does Cover 0 sit in the MOFC/MOFO scheme?** It's listed under MOFO "with no safeties deep at all", which is technically right but counter-intuitive (nobody deep anywhere). One sentence on why it still counts as "open" for the QB's read.
7. **Why one-high still leads (Cover 1 + Cover 3 > 50%) when the chapter's history (Fangio, split-safety growth) all points to two-high.** Two lines on why most defenses still default to one-high (run defense, simplicity) would resolve it. 02-05 gives the run-box reason, so a link would do.
