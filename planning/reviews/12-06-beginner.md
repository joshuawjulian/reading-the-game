# 12-06 beginner-reader review

Reader persona: watches the NFL casually and has never played. Has read the chapters before this one in curriculum order, so already knows EPA (01-02), personnel and box counts (02-01, 02-05), shotgun and pistol (02-02), duo and outside zone (03-02, 03-03), RPOs (04-06), quarters (06-05), pressure looks (07-03), the tush push (10-03), The Modern Era (11-05) and the earlier case studies. I read the rendered PDF (17 pages) from start to finish.

**Overall:** this is a strong case study with a clear thesis ("the lines are the part that lasts"). The tush-push section is the best thing in the chapter: it argues honestly against its own hype. Three things cost the most understanding:

- **Figure 1** (the Philly Special) and the outside-zone panel of **Figure 2** can't be decoded without guessing.
- **Answer 1** has an arithmetic contradiction.
- The **"volume is the edge"** argument is never put into numbers, and it sits awkwardly next to the orange dots in Figure 4.

## (1) Terms used before they are explained, or never explained

- **"FTN" / "FTN's charting"** (p.5 "In 2022, FTN's charting has Philadelphia using RPOs…", and about 10 times after). The chapter never says what FTN is: a company whose staff watch film and tag every play. It also never explains what "charting" means. "PFF" (p.2) and "NFL Next Gen Stats" (p.10) have the same problem. One sentence the first time each appears would fix this.
- **Code flag names in the prose:** "`is_qb_sneak`" (p.8 body text, and the Figure 4 caption). A non-coder stops reading at these. Keep them in the footnotes.
- **"press the doubles"** (Figure 2 caption: "Barkley … presses the doubles and reads the Mike"). "Press" is used as a verb with no gloss.
- **"the 'extra hat' of Option Football"** (p.5). "Hat" as slang for a blocker is used without a gloss. "Option Football" reads as a phrase here, not as a chapter title.
- **Chapter titles used as nouns in print:** "RPOs tells the mechanics and that season's story in full" (p.2). Also "Duo (from Gap Schemes)" and "from Quarters and Match". In the PDF there is no link, italics or "chapter" marker. At first I read "RPOs tells" as a typo.
- **"Cover 2, 2-man, Cover 6"** (footnote 23). These appear only in a footnote, but the body uses "two-high coverage of some kind on 30". I can't tell which coverages count as "of some kind".
- **"90% intervals"** (Figure 4 caption). I can see what the bars do, but nothing says what "90%" means: "if the Eagles were truly league-average, a gap this big would happen by chance less than 1 time in 10." The analytics chapters that would teach this come after this one.
- **"first-team All-Pro"**, **"ACL and MCL"**, **"salary cap" / "Over the Cap"**: these are probably fine for a fan, but "Over the Cap" appears in the body (p.12) with no hint that it is a contracts website.
- **"rookie-contract window"** (p.12) is defined inline, which is good. Its owner chapter is 14-01, so it should link there.

## (2) Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **Philly Special eligibility** (p.4, step 1): "a quarterback in the shotgun is just a back … When Foles walked forward and stood behind his right tackle … he was a back like any other." The Figure 1 caption says Foles *started* in the shotgun, so he was already eligible. Why does the walk matter for eligibility? As written, I come away thinking the walk is what *made* him eligible. The text needs to say plainly that the walk was for deception, not for eligibility. It should also say why he stayed eligible: he wasn't under center, and the ball went to Clement.
2. **Jeffery and the illegal-formation aside** (p.4): "only six players on it and made it an illegal formation". The seven-on-the-line rule is assumed. I had to infer "you need seven on the line". Remind me of it in half a sentence.
3. **"Some of it is not"** (p.7, coordinator churn). The chapter never says which departures were *not* the price of success: Johnson after the 2023 collapse, and Patullo. I had to work it out from the table.
4. **Figure 3's conclusion** (p.8): "which is what you would expect if the line, more than the coordinator, was the source of it." Why the line, and not Hurts or Barkley? Both were also constant across those seasons. The chart also shows dropbacks above average in all five years, and *above* runs in 2023–2025. The text never says what the orange line means for the thesis.
5. **Selection** (p.9): "on full yards as well as inches, against defenses that had a week to plan…" and "Matching the league's rate while taking the harder snaps is itself a sign of an edge." No evidence is shown that the Eagles' snaps were harder. Footnote 18 even admits the data can't tell inches from a yard. Say that in the body ("the data can't show this; it's an inference from how often they called it").
6. **Volume** (p.9): "sneaks on 1 yard to go converted 81%; other runs converted 70% and passes 58%." Two paragraphs earlier I was taught that other teams' sneak rates are inflated by selection. So why is 81% vs 70% a fair comparison? A demanding reader will catch this. The chapter needs to answer it, or soften the claim.
7. **The volume edge is never put into numbers.** The chapter's central analytic claim is "the edge was volume". So how many extra first downs a season did it buy? For example: (Eagles sneak share − league share) × snaps × (sneak rate − alternative rate). Without that number, "the talent edge … was being able to call the play every time" is an assertion.
8. **Figure 4 vs the takeaways.** The orange dots show 2024 at +1 and 2025 at +1: on every 1-yard snap, the Eagles still matched or beat the league. But the takeaways say "the edge was volume without a drop in success, and by 2025 even that was gone." The prose (p.10) relies on the blue −14 and on volume falling to 26. Say explicitly which measure "gone" refers to, and admit that the orange dots don't show a collapse.
9. **The roster section works against itself** (p.12): "a sneak that converts every time the offense needs a yard makes fourth-and-1 a play to run". Three pages earlier the chapter showed it converts about 82% of the time and fell to 65%. "Every time" undoes the honesty of the tush-push section.
10. **Answer 3's mechanism** (p.17): "makes the offense set its protection for six, so the center and guards are busy with linebackers who aren't coming, while the four real rushers may get a one-on-one". If the linebackers drop at the snap, why are the center and guards still "busy"? Wouldn't they then be free to double a rusher? The missing step is that the protection is assigned *before* the snap, so the blockers aimed at the linebackers are out of position or late to help. Without it, the logic reads backwards.
11. **"A single snap could hand off, keep or throw"** (p.5). How does one quarterback read two defenders on one snap (the zone-read defender and the RPO defender)? This is stated, not shown.
12. **"Kelce … small for a center"** (p.5). Why is being small usually a problem for a center, and why did it not matter (or even help) for Kelce? This is a natural "why" left unanswered.

  - I can't tell which line is Barkley's cut-up option and which is a block.

## (4) Drag and repetition

- **The Philly Special is described four times.** It appears in the opening, in the defining paragraph (p.3), in the Figure 1 caption (a four-step walk-through), and in the numbered four steps (p.4). The caption and the steps are close to word for word. Shorten the caption to the alignment and let the text carry the steps.
- **The fourth-and-1 to Ertz is told twice.** It appears on p.2 ("fourth-and-1 at their own 45 with 5:39 left, trailing New England 33–32 …") and again in the 2017 Film room box with the same details. Keep one.
- **"Two-high concedes a light box" is explained four times:** in the Figure 2 caption, the paragraph after it, the Barkley Film room, and Answer 2.
- **The Super Bowl LIX counts paragraph** (p.10) gives NGS and FTN numbers that disagree: 42 vs 45 dropbacks, 0 vs 1 blitz, 16 vs 14 pressures. For a beginner this is a pile of near-identical numbers. Lead with one source in the body and move the reconciliation to a footnote. The title "four rushers, no blitzes" and the takeaways' "no blitzes" also sit uneasily next to FTN's one blitz.
- **Structure:** the 2022 team, one of the three "Super Bowl teams" the thesis promises to test, gets only a Film room box, and it sits *after* the 2024 section. Chronologically it belongs between the 2021 run-game section and the tush push, which began in 2022.
