# 05-05 Run Fits and Box Math: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: a casual NFL watcher who has never played, has read 01-01 through 05-04 in order and
nothing after. Read start to finish in the PDF (`pdfs/05-05-run-fits-and-box-math.pdf`, 22 pages, rendered
2026-10-06 18:09, after the last .qmd edit). I looked at every page at 130 dpi. Line numbers refer to the .qmd.

**Overall verdict.** It is strong. The opening hook is excellent, "Why choose one or the other?" is the best
section, and the box-count chart and its three cautions are clear and honest. I could do most of the
"You'll be able to" list. The problems are concentrated: (a) the fit chart breaks the chapter's own "exactly one
owner" rule, (b) the plus-one count in the fit chart doesn't match the picture, (c) the "Watch for it" rule for
finding the force gives the wrong answer on Drill 1, (d) the five lettered "S" and the number "5" look alike in
every front diagram, and (e) the argument for the light box has a logic gap a data-minded reader will notice.

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

1. **The fit chart puts two defenders in the backside A gap (L724 and L729).** Row 2, the nose, says "Hold the
   backside A gap"; row 7, the Will, says "own the backside A gap and the cutback". The chapter has just told me
   (L402-404) that "a gap with *two* defenders in it is the same problem in disguise". As a beginner I concluded
   either the chart is wrong or I misunderstood gap integrity. From the 4-3 Over in 05-02 I believe the Will
   should own the backside **B** gap. The table also never names owners for most gaps: who has the
   play-side A gap after the Mike leaves, and who has the backside C? Objective 1 ("account for every gap... name
   the defender who owns each one") needs a gap → owner column, or gap letters drawn on fig-fit-chart.
2. **"Before the snap, the Mike was over the right A gap. After the snap, the right A gap has a nose, a tackle and
   two linemen in it" (L750-751).** The table and figure put the nose (1-technique) on the *left*. How did he get
   into the right A gap? This needs fixing or explaining.
3. **The plus-one count doesn't match the picture (L744-748, L829-830).** The text says seven blockers against
   eight defenders, so the strong safety is the plus-one. But in the table the *backside end is also unblocked*
   (row 1, "Power leaves him free on purpose"), and the left tackle "hinges" and blocks nobody. Counting free
   defenders in fig-fit-chart, I get two (the backside 5 and the SS), plus the FS. So why is the SS "the"
   plus-one? The missing sentence: *the offense chooses which defender to leave unblocked; power leaves the one
   farthest from the hole, so the plus-one that matters is the free man at the point of attack.* Without it,
   "count blockers vs defenders" and "who is unblocked" give different answers.
4. **What does the alley safety read, and how does he know it's a run in time?** The force, the spill player and
   the linebackers all get a read. The safeties only get one in the quarters aside ("reads the tight end",
   L647). A safety coming down 12 yards on a play-action fake is the cost the chapter names, so how he decides
   is the obvious next question.
5. **Why "four or five yards"?** (L1002, L1010, L1300, L1334.) This is stated four times and never derived.
   The reader is analytical. A two-line time-and-distance sum (safety reads for ~0.5 s, covers ~8 yards at ~7
   yd/s; the back reaches the line in ~1 s and is at 4-5 yards by ~1.8 s) would make the whole light-box section
   land. Fig-light-box also contradicts it: the safety arrows stop about 7 yards deep, not at the 4-5-yard spot.
6. **"Who makes the call?"** Over and over the chapter says force, spill or box "is decided by the call" (L452,
   L511, L619-624). It never says who calls it, when (sideline, or the Mike before the snap?), or how all
   eleven hear it. That is a Madden-bridge moment begging to be used.
7. **"Spill inside, force outside... the strong safety boxes" (L619-622).** Box was defined as a technique for
   meeting a kick-out blocker. The strong safety isn't meeting any blocker, so here "boxes" means something
   looser ("keeps the ball inside him"). Say so, or use "the force keeps it inside".
8. **The offense's answer to spill is missing.** 03-03 taught me that when the end wrong-arms, the kick-out man
   *logs* him and the back bounces outside on purpose ("kick or log"). This chapter never closes that loop. Is
   the spill call daring the offense into the log? How does the force beat a logged bounce?
9. **The cost of the scrape exchange (L954-957) is muddled.** "If the back had kept the ball (if the end had
   stayed home after all, or the quarterback had handed off), that gap would have been the hole." A running
   back doesn't "keep", and if the end crashes on a handoff, the end is the one taking the back. So I couldn't
   reconstruct the cost. 03-04 put it more clearly: "the scraping linebacker is no longer filling his gap
   inside, so the give runs at one fewer defender, and the back can cut back into the space the linebacker
   left." Use that, and mention 03-04's counters (bash, arc) in one line.
10. **The light-box argument has a logic gap (L1134-1138, Takeaways L1400-1402).** The chart says dropbacks beat
    runs at *every* box count. So why do offenses run at all? And "steering the offense toward its less
    efficient option" assumes offenses run *more* against light boxes. The chapter never shows that, and a
    rational offense would keep throwing at +0.06 rather than run at −0.05. The takeaway "each extra box
    defender cost runs about as much as it gave passes" reads like a wash, which makes the "reasonable bet"
    sound unproven. Two fixes: show the run rate by box count (it is the same data), and add one sentence on why
    teams run anyway (sacks and interceptions, clock, play-action needing runs, third-and-short).
11. **Drill 2's nickel back.** In fig-drill-2 (and fig-plus-one and fig-light-box), the $ stands 4.5 yards deep,
    about 6.5 yards from the ball, unblocked. 02-05 told me he is "just outside the box". On a zone read away
    from him he is right next to the read end. Why isn't he the defense's free man on the keep? The answer only
    names the safeties. One sentence would help ("he is the overhang, outside the box; the H receiver occupies
    him" or "he can scrape to the QB too").
12. **Fig-plus-one: is the rotated safety in the box?** The ringed SS lands right on the edge of the shaded box
    (w = 7.0 against a shade ending at 6.9), so to my eye he looks *outside* it. Move him half a yard in or widen
    the shade.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **"5" and "S" look almost identical** at diagram size in figs 1, 2, 3, the strip and Drill 3. The play-side
  5-technique stands right next to the S (Sam). In fig-spill-box frames 1, 4 and 5 I had to count from the
  center to tell them apart. This is the most damaging visual issue, because the whole spill/box idea is about
  the S. Label the Sam "SAM" or "Sm", or ring him in *every* frame (he is ringed in the strip, which helps).
- **Ring colours change meaning from figure to figure.** Green is the alley player in figs 1 and 3, the running
  back in fig 2, the cutback Will in fig 4, and the quarterback in fig 6. Orange is the spill Sam in figs 2 and 3
  and the Will in fig 6. Only purple (force/plus-one) is consistent. Keep green = alley/free fitter throughout,
  and use a neutral ring for "the ball carrier".
- **fig-spill-box (p.5).** (a) The text says "the pulling guard turns up into a lane already full of bodies" and
  "the Mike... meets the pulling guard". The linemen are unlettered, so I could not find the puller in any
  frame. Ring him or letter him "G". (b) "A yard wide instead of three" isn't visible; nothing marks the hole.
  (c) Frame 6 is a pile of overlapping markers (5, R ring, M, S, F), and I couldn't see the Mike "filling".
  (d) The box row starts at +0.6 s, not at the snap, so the rows aren't parallel. Start both at the snap, or say why.
- **fig-fit-chart (p.7).** The puller's path is hard to follow through the line. The backside 5's orange arrow is
  a tiny stub under the guard. Badge 4 sits on the tight end's block line. There are no gap letters, which is
  exactly what objective 1 needs.
- **fig-zone-fit (p.9).** The offensive line's blocks are tiny 1.6-yard stubs, so the caption's main point ("the
  line steps right... the gaps slide with the blockers") is not visible. The "end: squeeze, chase, boot" leader
  ends on an *offensive* lineman, not the backside 5. The SS has an arrow but no job in the caption or bullets.
  "FS: cutback alley" uses *alley* for a backside lane, which isn't the alley as defined two sections earlier.
- **fig-exchange (p.12).** The ownership tags float far from their players ("E: the QB" sits in the offensive
  backfield; "E: the back" sits under the quarterback, not near the end). Frame 4 puts the X spot on top of the W
  and hides the Q. The frame note "the LG climbed to nobody" names a lineman the diagram doesn't letter.
- **fig-light-box (p.13).** The "6 v 6" label sits beside the $, who is *outside* the box, so I counted him in
  and got 7. Nobody visibly blocks the $. The safety arrows end around 7 yards, not where the caption says they
  meet the run.
- **fig-drill-1 caption** mentions "outside the hashes", but the diagram has no hash marks (the helper strips
  them).
- **Go deeper (p.16), print.** The paragraph ends "(this cell is not run in the build):" and nothing follows in
  the PDF, because the code is hidden. Reword it for print, e.g. "the website shows the code".
- What worked: fig-force-alley is the clearest picture in the chapter. Side by side with labels, I could do the
  force/alley reading straight away. The data chart is clean, and its caption states the finding.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **The scrape exchange is drawn for the third time** (03-04 fig at L607, 05-04 fig at L964, here). The new
  part, the ownership tags, takes one frame. Either keep the strip and say up front "you have seen this from the
  offense (03-04) and the Will (05-04); here are the *ownership* changes", or spend the strip on something new
  (a slant + linebacker exchange against inside zone, or the dive/QB/pitch assignment chart from L967-970).
- **Spill and box are re-explained from scratch** (L524-536). 03-03 (L689-695) and 05-01 (L1013-1018) already
  defined both, including wrong-arming. As the owner, this chapter should define them, but open with "you met
  these in 03-03 and 05-01; here is the full version" and skip re-teaching the basic body positions.
- **The 2024 Eagles light-box stat appears twice, almost word for word**: the Common misconception at L1059-1061
  and the film room at L1218-1220 ("about two thirds... third-highest... fewest EPA per designed run"). The
  Inquirer's 73.5% (snaps, first two weeks) next to "two thirds" (designed runs, full season) also left me
  wondering which number is right. Keep it in one place.
- **"Light box is a bet" is argued five times**: the section intro, Why a defense would do this, the
  misconception, the data section and the Takeaways. The data section is the only one that adds anything after
  the first.
- **Internal file paths in reader-facing footnotes.** "(planning/FACTS-current.md §10)" and similar appear in
  footnotes 2, 4, 7, 9, 14 and 15. A reader shouldn't see the book's planning files.
- **Print layout.** Pages 4, 8, 11, 18 and 19 are half or more blank because figures were pushed to the next
  page. Pages 4 and 11 hurt most, since they break the reading flow just before the key figures. Smaller figure
  heights or moving the lead-in paragraph would help.
- The Fangio sentence at L1030-1032 is a 60-word run-on.

- **Drill 3. My answer:** a tight split Z means a crack block on the strong safety (the force), so the right
  corner has to crack-replace. **Correct**, thanks to fit chart row 10 and 03-05. A good drill: it makes the

  reads as a post-snap reaction. I assume it means "when they see a crack split pre-snap"; say that.

## 6. What I still wonder (the chapter should answer these)

1. Who calls spill or box and the force assignment, and when? Does the Mike signal it pre-snap? Can it change
   by formation at the line?
2. What does the alley safety read to tell run from play-action, and how late can he decide?
3. How does the offense beat spill (the log block from 03-03), and how does the force handle a designed bounce?
4. In a 6-man light box, who is the force on each side? The fig-light-box caption never says, and it's the
   most common modern picture according to the chapter's own numbers (55-58% of designed runs).
5. Do offenses actually run more often against light boxes? The "steering" argument depends on it, and the
   same data could show it.
6. If even the light-box run is negative EPA, why does anyone run? One sentence would stop the reader
   concluding "never run".
7. Where does the 4-5 yard "price" come from? Time and distance would convince me.
8. With an unbalanced or two-tight-end formation, how does the force rule change? Each side now has a
   different end man, and there is no tight end on the side of the $.
