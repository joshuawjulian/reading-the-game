# 12-04 Beginner reader review: Case Study: Andy Reid and the Kansas City Chiefs

Reviewer persona: a casual NFL watcher who never played and has read Parts 1–11 in order, plus
the chapters the "You'll need" box points to. Read start to finish in the rendered PDF
(`pdfs/12-04-reid-chiefs.pdf`, 24 pages; figure pages re-rasterised at 110 dpi to read the
labels). Line numbers below refer to `chapters/12-case-studies/12-04-reid-chiefs.qmd`.

**Overall:** this is a strong chapter. The three-sources framing, the late-throw scatter, the
"every defensive answer costs" list and the four-part "how opponents responded" section are
excellent and taught me things I didn't know. Most of my problems are **internal
contradictions** (numbers or pictures that disagree with the sentences next to them), and a
handful of promised skills the chapter doesn't fully deliver. Those are what an analytically
minded reader trips on, so they're listed first.

---

## 0. Must-fix: places where the chapter contradicts itself or an earlier chapter

1. **The success-rate rule in "Watch for it" isn't the book's rule.** L1256–1258: "success (half
   the yards needed on first down, 70% on second, all of it on third or fourth: the success rate
   rule from Downs, Distance, and Field Position)". 01-02 defines success rate as *the share of
   plays with positive EPA* (its glossary entry, L21 of 01-02), and 13-02 (L709) gives the
   yardage rule as **40% / 60% / 100%**. So I get a third rule here, credited to a chapter that
   doesn't contain it. Use 40/60/100, credit 13-02, and say it's a stand-in for "positive EPA".
2. **The RPO figure's caption doesn't match the picture (Fig. 2, p. 5).** The caption (L366) says
   the nickel is "aligned in the apex between the box and the slot, with the tight end (87)
   running a 5-yard hitch **outside** him". In the drawing 87 is at about 8.5 yards right of
   center and the $ at about 11.5, so 87 is *inside* the nickel and the $ stands between 87 and
   Z. That isn't the apex as 02-05 taught it (between the end man on the line and the slot).
   Also, the caption says "five defenders in the box (E, T, T, E, M)", but **W** sits at
   linebacker depth about 6 yards left of center, and I counted him as a sixth. Nothing says why
   he doesn't count or who blocks him. (He's the backside defender inside zone leaves alone, but
   I only know that if I go back to 03-02.) Fix the alignment, or the caption, and give W one
   clause.
3. **"87 is alone" while a safety stands next to him (Fig. 4, p. 8, panel "+5.45 s · the
   catch").** The SS is about two yards from 87 at the catch. The caption and the panel note both
   say "87 is alone". Either move the SS so he visibly trails by several yards, or change the
   words to "a step late" or "open".
4. **Wasp isn't a "late" throw by the chapter's own definition.** The whole Mahomes section draws
   the line at **four seconds** (L474–519, Fig. 3). Then L730–732 says Wasp is "the defensive
   arithmetic of the previous section in one snap", but the caption says the ball was out at
   **3.8 s**. The prose also mixes a ~3-second coverage budget (L467–471) with a 4-second
   statistic. One sentence would fix it: "coverages are built for ~3 s, and we use 4 s as the
   cutoff so we only count throws that are clearly past it; Wasp, at 3.8 s, is already past the
   budget."
5. **The ceiling paragraph contradicts the chart.** L252–254: "In those years [the first five KC
   seasons, through the January 2018 Tennessee loss] the Chiefs' offense ranked between 9th and
   15th". Fig. 10 and L406 both say the 2017 offense was **3rd**. Say "2013–2016" (the Fig. 10
   caption already does).
6. **Spagnuolo's blitz rate: "ordinary" vs. rank 5.** The section says the famous thing is "not
   how *often* he blitzes" (L926). The Fig. 8 caption says "ordinary in most seasons", the
   takeaway says "only modestly above average", and "What survives" says "a blitz rate only
   modestly above average". But Table 1 says **5th of 32** pooled (31.0% vs 27.0%), and Fig. 8
   shows 4th, 6th and 3rd in 2020, 2024 and 2025. To me, 5th of 32 isn't ordinary. Reconcile:
   "above average, but nowhere near his Cover 0 rank, which is 2nd and twice the median".
7. **Drill 2's answer contradicts itself.** L1359–1360 says the hot throw to Y or R is "a quick
   slant or a quick out". L1367–1369 says "a slant is risky and an out-breaking route has room".
   The leverage clue cited there ("pressed corners with inside leverage") is about X, H and Z,
   but the question is about Y and R, who are covered by safeties 11 yards off. Choose one
   answer and tie the leverage clue to the right receivers.
8. **Drill 3's blocker count includes the ball carrier.** L1408–1409: "Only six defenders are in
   the box against five linemen and a running back, so an inside run has a blocker for nearly
   every defender." On a handoff the back carries the ball and doesn't block. 02-05 taught me to
   count box defenders against blockers, which here is 6 against 5, so the defense is +1. Either
   explain how the offense "blocks" the sixth man (read him with the QB, an RPO, as in Fig. 2), or
   compare against the 7- or 8-man box a one-high defense would show. As written, it undermines
   the box-count lesson.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

- **"Stinger"**: the title of the fourth Wasp panel ("+7.0 s · the stinger", p. 10). It's never
  explained in this chapter. Reid's quote ("Literally put the stinger on 'em") is in 04-08; quote
  it here. It's the best line about the play.
- **"2-3 Jet"**: L711 promises "Take the call one word at a time", then explains only "Chip",
  "Wasp" and the drift. "2-3 Jet" is skipped. I know jet *motion* from 02-06 and saw no jet
  motion in the figure, so I was confused. Add the 04-08 caveat: probably the protection, a
  different "jet" from jet motion. L330 ("protection, a word for the back, a route name") also
  doesn't say which word is which.
- **"Corn Dog" / "return-motion play"** (L441, ledger row LVII "two fourth-quarter 'Corn Dog'
  touchdowns against man coverage"): it decides one of the five Super Bowls, and the chapter
  gives it zero description. One sentence (receiver motions in, turns back out, and the man
  defender who chased him is now trailing) would do it. Link "return motion" to its glossary
  entry (owned by 02-06).
- **"mesh"** (L359): it's glossed inline ("the instant of the possible handoff"), which helps, but
  the glossary's "mesh" (04-04) is the crossing-route *concept*. Say "the mesh point" or note the
  two meanings.
- **"2-Man"** (Drill 1 answer, L1311): not linked. Owned by 06-04.
- **Quarters / Cover 6 / Cover 2** (opponents section L1180, footnote [^sblv]): "quarters coverage
  on 27 of them" isn't linked (06-05).
- **"throws hot"** (L1186), **"Set the protection to the mug"** (L1369): jargon from 04-02 (hot
  route, slide protection) with no link. "Set the protection" in particular means nothing to me
  without one.
- **"bracketed Kelce and Hill"** (L1157): bracket is 06-02; link it.
- **"deep crosser" / "stem"** (Wasp section, L718; panel title "the crosser stem"): stem is 04-03,
  but "crosser" isn't in the term index. 04-08 called it "a deep dig that turns into a corner";
  reuse that image.
- **"pressured on a Super Bowl-record 29 of his 56 dropbacks"** (L1177): what counts as a
  "pressure" in charting? Link to 07-01 or define it in a clause.
- **Diagram labels "S" and "D"** (Fig. 9, p. 15): the reading key (L225–231) lists FS/SS, CB, $,
  M, W, E, T. It doesn't include a bare "S" (there are two of them) or "D". I couldn't tell
  which S is blitzing and which is covering Y.
- **"the 2022 overtime rule"** (ledger LVIII row): referenced but not stated. One clause: "both
  teams now get a possession in the playoffs". Link 10-04, which owns it.
- **EPA per play / per attempt / per dropback**: three denominators. Fine for me, but one
  sentence noting that they differ (and that sacks are in "dropback" and not in "attempt")
  would help when comparing the +0.30 (attempts) with the +0.31 (out-of-pocket dropbacks).

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

- **"It didn't cost him" (L516) ignores sacks.** The chapter says, correctly, that the league's
  late throws are bad "before you even count the sacks the long-held ball produces". Then
  Mahomes's late throws are measured *without* his sacks, and the conclusion is that holding the
  ball didn't cost him. For an analytically minded reader that's the obvious hole. Give
  Mahomes's EPA on all dropbacks of 4+ s, sacks included, against the league's (the data is
  already loaded), or at least his sack rate on long-held dropbacks.
- **Cover 0 rate high, blitz rate "ordinary": how?** Cover 0 needs six or more rushers, so every
  zero is a blitz. The reader needs the arithmetic: zero is ~8% of snaps, blitzes ~30%, so about
  a quarter of Spags's blitzes are zero against roughly one in seven league-wide. That's what
  "what he blitzes *with*" means. As written, L926 is a riddle.
- **What does a coordinator change if Reid calls the plays?** One learning objective is to name
  the OCs by season, and the chapter does. But the L308–317 paragraph says Reid calls everything,
  so I finished not knowing why Bieniemy-vs-Nagy matters, or what to expect from Bieniemy's
  return. Even "the coordinator shapes the weekly plan and the QB room; nobody outside the
  building can isolate his effect" would close the loop.
- **Close-game regression set up and never paid off.** L909–913 explains that 11–0 in one-score
  games won't repeat. Then 2025 happens (6–11) and the chapter blames "Time" without giving the
  2025 one-score record. That's the payoff the reader is waiting for. If the 2025 Chiefs lost
  most of their close games, say so. It's the clearest lesson in the chapter.
- **"Two-high coverage is a deal"** (L798) is good. But "Mahomes ... learning to take that deal"
  doesn't say *how* he got the deep ball back beyond listing "play-action, motion or a play like
  Wasp". Meanwhile Table 1 says the Chiefs ran **less** play-action than most teams (27th). So
  which was it? One sentence reconciling these would help.
- **Option route "can't be called wrong"** (L890). Then what beats it? The chapter mentions
  QB–receiver disagreement but not the defense's counters: a robber or bracket on Kelce,
  disguising man as zone (06-06). That's a natural "how opponents responded" item.
- **Wasp: "two linebackers and a nickel inside the 49ers' five defensive backs"** (L630–631).
  This is muddled: the nickel *is* one of the five DBs. Something like "four linemen, two
  linebackers and five defensive backs (a nickel package)" is clearer.
- **"Is there time for Wasp?"** (L737): "time" means game clock here, but the chapter has spent
  two sections on the *coverage clock*. Say which clock.

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items? (drills attempted before opening answers)

- **Three sources, spot each on a broadcast**: yes for explaining. For *spotting*, the chapter
  gives me screens, RPOs and shotgun, but no on-screen tell for "a play built for one opponent".
  Partly.
- **Late-throw arithmetic with numbers**: yes. I can recite the numbers and the four costly
  answers. The sack hole (§2) would let a sceptic knock me over.
- **Hill era vs Kelce era; read the option route vs man and zone**: yes. Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 do
  this well.
- **Recognise Spagnuolo's Cover 0 before the snap**: **no, not really.** The only pre-snap Cover 0
  picture is the drill, and its answer opens with "You can't know". The goal-line figure is a
  case where nobody is deep anyway. I'd want a short pre-snap checklist (no player in the deep
  middle; safeties at 8–11 yards, sometimes creeping in; press on the outside; mugged A-gap
  linebackers; more potential rushers than blockers) and the honest caveat about simulated
  pressure. Either add it or soften the objective to "recognise when zero is possible".
- **OCs and Super Bowls with what decided each**: yes (ledger table), except that LVII's "Corn
  Dog" is a name to me, not a mechanism.
- **How opponents answered, and what survives**: yes. This is the best section.

**Drill 1 (Kelce's choice).** My answer before opening: the W travelling all the way across with
Kelce means man; two safeties staying deep means two-deep man underneath (I remembered "2-Man"
from 06-04 only vaguely); W is inside, so Kelce breaks out. **Correct.** Fig. 7 and the 02-06 man
indicator were enough. Good drill.

**Drill 2 (Spags on third down).** Before opening: six potential rushers (4 DL + 2 mugged LBs)
against five blockers, empty backfield, and nobody visibly on Y and R except the safeties, so if
it's zero the ball goes to Y or R right now. I also suspected it might be a sim pressure, from
07-03. **Mostly right**, but the answer then confused me (§0.7: slant or out?), and I couldn't
have produced "about 10% of third downs" or "set the protection to the mug" (I don't know what
that instruction means mechanically).

**Drill 3 (the shell).** Before opening: two-high, so don't throw deep. Run, or throw underneath
to Kelce. But my box count was **6 defenders vs 5 blockers**, so I wasn't sure the run was
"right": the defense still has an extra man. The answer resolved that by counting the running
back as a blocker (§0.8), which I think is wrong. **Right conclusion, but the drill's key
arithmetic doesn't hold up.**

## 6. What I still wonder (and this chapter should answer)

1. **The "13 seconds" game** (Bills–Chiefs, January 2022 divisional round). It's the most famous
   example of Mahomes beating the clock, and the reason the playoff overtime rule changed (the
   rule LVIII was played under). It's missing from a Chiefs case study. It also explains why the
   2018 AFC title game ("Mahomes never touched the ball in overtime") mattered.
2. **What was 2025, really?** Offense 12th, defense 12th: that's an average team, so why 6–11?
   One-score record, turnovers, injuries before the ACL? "Time" is a shrug.
3. **What's the 2026 status, today (October 2026)?** The chapter says "2026 questions this book
   can't answer". But the season is five weeks old. Is Mahomes playing? Is Kelce still on the
   roster? If the book can't follow the season, say "as of the start of the 2026 season".
4. **What beats the option route?** And did defenses do it in 2023, the drop year?
5. **Does Mahomes's late-throw edge survive including sacks?**
6. **Why did Reid run so *little* play-action** (27th) when his West Coast cousins (Shanahan) live
   on it? The chapter states the fact but gives no reason. Is it shotgun, Mahomes's style, or
   because the RPO does play-action's job?
7. **What does Corn Dog look like?** One sentence and ideally a small static diagram, since it
   decided LVII and LVIII.
8. **The Chiefs' run game.** Apart from the RPO figure and Kareem Hunt in 2017, there's almost
   nothing on how (or whether) Kansas City ran the ball, which leaves "a light box against the
   run" (two-high's price) feeling theoretical.
