# 03-03 Gap Schemes: beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: casual NFL viewer, never played, has read 01-01 to 03-02 in order. Read the qmd start to finish
and looked at every page of `pdfs/03-03-gap-and-power-runs.pdf` (24 pages, at 60 and 120 dpi). Line numbers refer to
`chapters/03-the-run-game/03-03-gap-and-power-runs.qmd`.

Overall: strong chapter. The voice matches the pilots, power is taught well, and the "where do the extra blockers come
from, and who is left free?" frame (l.386) carries the whole chapter. The draw strip and the duo figure are the
clearest diagrams. The serious problems are a few **internal contradictions** a careful reader will catch (squeeze and
spill, which way the helmet sends the back, which guard is the backside guard, "every lineman climbs" on wham, "every gap
run has three parts"), and several **simulated-panel diagrams where the key event can't be seen at print size**.

## 2. Leaps: a missing or assumed "why"

1. **"Every gap run … is assembled from three parts" (l.361) is broken by half the chapter.** Duo (no puller, no
   kick-out), iso (no puller, no kick-out, no down blocks), wham (no puller) and the draw (none of the three) don't fit
   it, and the takeaway repeats it (l.1323: "Every gap run is down blocks, a kick-out and a puller"). The real unifying
   idea is the one in the glossary definition, "blockers assigned to men/gaps before the snap, a hole chosen in
   advance". Lead with that. Then present the three parts as power's anatomy, and say plainly that duo, iso and the
   draw are gap plays because blockers are assigned to men, not because they pull.
2. **Power's "five on four" (fig-power-count, l.468, l.484-487) doesn't add up for me.** The five play-side blockers
   include the RG, who (per fig-power) leaves the 3 to climb to the **Will**, who is *not* one of the four play-side
   defenders. So where is the spare blocker? The answer is presumably that the spare becomes the double team on the 3
   (two on one), which then releases a man to the backside linebacker. That is the most important arithmetic in the
   chapter, so say it: "the 'extra' man shows up as the RT+RG double team, and it's the double that frees the RG to
   take the Will." The later backside sentence (l.489-491, "the center, the hinging left tackle and nobody else
   against the 1, the Will and the backside end. The right guard's climb takes care of the Will") makes it worse,
   because the RG is now counted on both sides.
3. **"The play side is identical" to power (l.520).** It isn't, as drawn. In fig-power the Y blocks down on the 5 and
   the FB kicks out the Sam. In fig-counter there is no play-side Y or Sam, and the guard kicks out the 5 himself.
   Comparing the two figures side by side, I spent a while hunting for the "identical" play side. Say "the same
   *rules* on the play side" and note that the end man is the 5 here.
4. **Wham: "*every* lineman climbs to the second level" (l.812) contradicts the figure.** In fig-wham the C blocks the
   1, the RT blocks the 5 and the LT blocks the backside end; only the guards climb. The fig-wham caption also says
   "three linemen are on linebackers", but it's two guards and the Y (not a lineman). The arithmetic paragraph
   (l.822-825) compares trap and wham against **different fronts** (trap has no Sam, wham has a Sam and an extra H), so
   I can't see the gain the text claims.
5. **The helmet rule contradicts the log block.** l.658-659: "the back's eyes are on the puller's helmet. Where the
   helmet goes (inside or outside the defender), the back goes the other way." But the log is defined as the puller
   getting "his head and body outside him … and the ball carrier bounces **outside**" (l.614-615), and Answer 3 says
   the back, "reading the guard's helmet, takes the ball outside the log" (l.1311). On the kick-out the puller hits
   from the inside and the ball goes inside. Both cases say *same* side, not "the other way". I genuinely don't know
   which rule to use on Sunday.
6. **Which guard is "the backside guard" on Sunday?** l.1182-1183: "watch the *backside* guard (the one away from
   where the back lines up …)". But l.1160-1161 says a shotgun back offset left usually runs **right**, so the
   backside guard is the one on the **same** side as the back, and fig-counter shows exactly that (back offset left,
   LG pulls). Under center in the I, the back is centred, so "away from the back" means nothing. "If you cannot tell,
   the left guard": why left? Unexplained. This is the chapter's headline habit ("Follow the guard"), so it has to be
   right.
7. **Pin-and-pull's two-option rule vs the Y.** The rule (l.949-950) is "defender in your backside gap? Pin. Empty?
   Pull." In fig-pin-and-pull the Y has an empty backside gap but neither pins nor pulls: he "goes inside to the Mike".
   A third option appears without explanation. The rule is also stated for "each play-side blocker", yet the
   backside LG pulls under it. Does the rule cover the whole line?
8. **Duo's "extra blockers" answer (l.700) dodges the chapter's thesis.** "From the defense's own choice" means duo
   doesn't manufacture anyone, which l.714 then says outright. Fine, but say early that duo is the gap family's
   no-manufacture member, and why that is still a gap play (see item 1).
9. **The draw on 3rd-and-11 (l.1053-1056).** "May not get a first down, but it can steal five or six yards." 01-02
   taught me success rate and EPA, and by those a 6-yard gain on 3rd-and-11 is a failed play. Why is it still
   rational? Is it a punt-setup play, a pass-rush constraint, or a bad call that coaches make anyway? One sentence tied
   to 01-02 would land with this reader.
10. **"The defense cannot match that move, because its players are reading the snap, not leading it" (l.487).** I
    couldn't parse "leading it". "Reacting to the snap, not knowing in advance" is clearer.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, or captions that don't say what to notice

- **fig-power (p.3).** The puller's path, the FB's path and the RB's thick arrow merge near the Y. I couldn't find the
  FB's kick-out bar, and the "F kicks out the S" leader points at a junction of three lines. The label "unblocked: the
  hinge walls him off" reads as a contradiction (is he blocked or not?). Try "unblocked: the LT's hinge only gets in
  his way". "RT + RG double the 3" floats above the linebackers, far from the 3.
- **fig-counter strip (p.5), the chapter's showpiece animation.** At print size:
  - Frame 1: the purple rings cover the LT and LG labels.
  - Frames 3-5: the RT is hidden behind the 3, the LG sits on top of the 5, and the LT overlaps the 3 and the ball.
    The 5 never visibly moves outward, so "LG kicks out the end" (frame 4) isn't visible.
  - Frame 2: the body text says "the Mike's first two steps go toward the back's jab (frame 2)", but in frame 2 the M
    has barely moved. The false step, the whole point of counter, can't be seen.
  - Wider spacing or a closer zoom on the play side for frames 4-6 would help.
  - Also, "Look at what changed from power in @fig-counter" renders as "from power in Figure 3", which reads as if
    Figure 3 were power. Say "compare @fig-counter with @fig-power".
- **fig-kick-or-log (p.7).** Defenders are drawn at their pre-snap spots. In the right panel the puller's block bar
  lands in about the same place relative to the 5's square as in the left panel, so "outside shoulder" isn't visible.
  The geometry that separates kick from log is the lesson, and I had to take it on faith from the caption. Show the
  end at his post-squeeze spot, with the bar clearly on his outside.
- **fig-trap and fig-wham right panels (p.9, p.10).** The R, the ball, the green ring, the trapper, the 3 and the RT
  form one overlapping clump. The 3 doesn't look "kicked out toward the sideline"; he is roughly where he started. In
  the left trap panel the LG's short pull is mostly hidden under the RB's path. The trap panel title says 1.4 s and its
  caption says "about 1.5 seconds".
- **fig-packers-sweep (p.12).** Six crossing paths make it spaghetti. I could not trace which line is the RG versus
  the halfback. The "RG (first puller) takes the first defender outside" label sits bottom-right with no leader line,
  far from his path. The Z's block on the SS isn't in the caption. "H" is the halfback (see §1).
- **fig-pin-and-pull (p.14).**
  - The "first puller kicks the end man; second leads up inside" label sits beside the Z/CB block, so it reads as a
    note about the corner.
  - The "pin" and "pull" tags float 2.5-4.5 yards behind the line among crossing paths, and the RT's "pin" sits on
    the toss line.
  - The W's arrow appears to point at the M.
  - The Y doesn't follow the stated rule (see §2.7).
- **fig-drill-3 (p.22).** "Freezes the play as the pulling left guard … arrives", but the end is drawn at his pre-snap
  spot with an arrow. That's acceptable, but it is the same picture as fig-kick-or-log's right panel (see §4).
- Good: fig-power-count (shaded play side plus the two labels make the arithmetic visible), fig-duo, fig-iso, the
  fig-draw strip (each frame's caption tells the story, and the linebackers' rings make the "moving the wrong way"
  point obvious).
- **Print layout.** Pages 7, 13 and 16 are about half blank (figures float to the next page), and Answer 3 sits alone
  on p.24. Not a content problem, but it makes the PDF feel padded.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Power is explained four times.** There is the fig-power caption (about 110 words), the four walk-through
  paragraphs, the fig-power-count caption, and the paragraph after it. The captions duplicate the body almost line for
  line. Several captions run 90-110 words (fig-power, fig-duo, fig-counter, fig-packers-sweep). Trim the captions to
  "what to notice" and let the body walk through.
- **03-01 already taught this power picture.** Its Drill 1 shows the same I-formation power with the LG pulling and
  names it power. This chapter's **Drill 1 is that drill mirrored**, and its caption narrates every action ("the U
  tight end and the left tackle have stepped inside … the right guard is pulling left, the fullback is heading for the
  left edge"). It's a gimme twice over. A drill showing **counter or trap at the snap**, where I have to tell them
  apart, would test far more.
- **Drill 3 repeats fig-kick-or-log's right panel** with an SS added. Pick a case the chapter didn't draw, such as the
  end staying wide on counter, or trap vs a disciplined tackle who squeezes.
- **Film-room trivia.** The Ravens box's "Roman left Baltimore after the 2022 season and John Harbaugh was fired after
  2025 …" and the Eagles box's Moore/Stoutland parenthetical are about staff turnover, not about what to watch. For this
  reader they are drag. Move them to footnotes.
- Five film rooms is one or two more than needed. The Titans and Eagles boxes overlap ("watch the back press the
  doubles / follow the puller patiently").
- The "Power O" name paragraph (l.505-508) and the two competing draw-origin stories (l.1063-1067) are fine but long
  relative to what they teach.

## 5. Can I do each "You'll be able to…"? Drills attempted before opening the answers

**Drills (my answers before opening):**

- **Drill 1.** *My answer:* power left. The U and LT block down, the FB kicks out the S, the RG pulls and leads on the
  Mike, the backside 5 (right) is unblocked, and the hole is outside the U. **Correct.** But it was trivial: it's
  fig-power mirrored, and the caption narrates each block.
- **Drill 2.** *My answer:* draw, because third-and-long, four wide rushers, one linebacker who will drop, and two deep.
  It's ruined by a linebacker blitzing or sitting in the middle. **Correct.** But the chapter also told me duo is "a
  two-high play" against light boxes, and this box is five vs five with two high safeties. **Why not duo?** The answer
  never says. One sentence (duo needs the linebacker to fill so the back can read him, and a dropping linebacker on
  3rd-and-11 is what the draw exploits) would make the drill teach the contrast.
- **Drill 3.** *My answer:* log, bounce outside, and the SS makes the tackle. **Correct**, because fig-kick-or-log
  already showed this exact picture.

**The objectives:**

1. **Explain the three parts and why each exists.** Yes, for power and counter. But the chapter's own duo, iso, wham
   and draw sections taught me the three-part claim isn't true (§2.1), so I'd explain it wrong for those.
2. **Recognize all ten and draw each against a front you are shown.**
   - *Recognize:* partly. Power, counter (two pullers) and the draw, yes. Trap vs wham, yes, if I can see who arrives
     at the tackle's hip. Duo vs inside zone at TV speed, not confidently; "linemen step forward vs sideways" is subtle
     from the sky-cam.
   - *Draw against a front I'm shown:* **no.** Every play is shown against one front only. The gap-down-backer rule is
     stated (l.406-408) but never applied block by block. Counter, trap, wham and iso are given no rules at all, only
     one picture each, so I couldn't redraw counter against an odd front or trap against a 1-technique. A short "same
     play, different front" panel for power (the 3-technique on the backside: who does the center block now?) would
     deliver this objective.
3. **Count blockers and defenders at the point of attack and show the gain and its price.** For power, mostly, but
   the RG double-count (§2.2) left me unsure I was counting right. For counter I'd have to count it myself; the chapter
   doesn't.
4. **When a coach calls gap vs zone.** Yes. The five factors plus the table (tbl-gap-zone) are clear, and this is the
   best-delivered objective.
5. **Pre-snap and first-step tells, and calling direction.** First-step tells, yes ("backward and sideways" vs "sideways
   and forward" vs "straight back"). Pre-snap tells are mostly hedged ("the best pullers hide it"). The one concrete
   instruction (which guard to watch) contradicts the shotgun offset rule (§2.6).

## 6. Knowledge gaps: what I still wonder

- **What does gap football look like from the shotgun, which is what I actually see on Sunday?** The chapter says
  about 1 snap in 10 has two backs (l.880). Yet power, iso and the sweep are all drawn from the I. There is one
  sentence on shotgun power with an H-back (l.507). I want a shotgun or pistol power picture with the H-back as
  kick-out man, the version I'll see most.
- **Which gap does each play hit?** A one-line table (power: C/D off-tackle; counter: same; trap: B; iso: the bubble;
  duo: A/B by read; draw: A) would tie everything back to 03-01's gap letters.
- **How often do NFL teams run gap vs zone, and does either work better?** I'm analytically minded, and 03-02 gave me an
  EPA chart. Here I get no number at all on gap vs zone frequency or efficiency, beyond the Shanahan anecdote. Even one
  sourced sentence (or a pointer to where the BDB `pff_runConceptPrimary` split is computed) would help. The Go Deeper
  cell prints the counts but the text never tells me roughly what they are.
- **What do the QB and the receivers do on power?** Is the QB just a handoff man, and do receivers block the corners?
- **How does a defense actually stop power?** It's forward-linked to 05-05, which is fine, but one sentence on the most
  common answer (e.g. the backside end chasing and the linebacker "spilling" the puller) would close the loop opened by
  the kick-or-log section.
- **Why is it "counter trey"**, and what does the "O" in Power O mean? The chapter admits the second is unclear, which
  is fine. The first is never addressed.
- **How do I tell duo from inside zone from the broadcast angle?** Both start with double teams and no pullers.
- **Toss vs handoff sweep.** Why pitch at all if a handoff works? The chapter answers "time and width" (l.891-892),
  which is good, but there's no picture of a plain toss or a handed sweep, only the Packers sweep and the pin-and-pull
  toss.
