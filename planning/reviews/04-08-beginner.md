# 04-08 beginner-reader review

Reviewer persona: a casual NFL watcher who has never played, is analytically minded, and has read
01-01 through 04-07 in curriculum order. From those chapters I already know: the parts of a call
("Gun Trips Right Zip 60 Stick X Dig"), verbiage, installs, the wristband and picture boards, the
15-second radio cutoff and the substitution rule (01-04). 01-04 also gave me a **preview of the
three families**: West Coast (with the Shanahan "Y Short to Strong Right…" call), Erhardt-Perkins
(with "Ghost"), Air Coryell ("896"). I know #1/#2/#3 numbering (02-04), jet/zip/orbit motion
(02-06), $ = nickel, one-high/two-high, the zone read and the mesh *point* (03-04), the route tree
and option/choice routes (04-01), **chip** as a back's block-then-release (04-02), the 14 dropback
concepts, including flood/**sail** with a ~13-yard sail route, mesh as "the purest man-beater",
the Air Raid origin story and tags ("Mesh, Z-wheel") (04-04), boots and play-action (04-05), RPOs
(04-06) and QB progressions (04-07).

Sources: `chapters/04-the-passing-game/04-08-offensive-systems-and-languages.qmd` (lines `L###`)
and `pdfs/04-08-offensive-systems-and-languages.pdf` (19 pages, rasterized at 60/90 dpi with
200-dpi crops of figs 2 and 3; pages `p##`).

**Overall.** This is an enjoyable, well-argued chapter. The "what does a word stand for" spine
(Fig 1, p3) is excellent. It is the clearest single idea in Part 4, and the families-vs-axes table
(p10) is a good summary. The install map and the checklist card both work. The main problems:
(a) **one Predict answer contradicts its own diagram** (the direction of the jet motion);
(b) **two claims contradict the fingerprint chart** printed beside them; (c) the chapter **re-teaches
01-04 and 04-04 without saying so**; (d) the first "You'll be able to…" item **can't be done as
written** for 3 of the 7 families; (e) length: about **8,400 words of prose against a 4,500 target**.
The overlap and the four overlapping "tells" lists cause most of that overrun.

## 1. Terms used before they're explained, or never explained

1. **"Chip"** in the Chiefs call, greyed as "meaning not public" (Fig 3, L494, p6). 04-02 *owns*
   "chip" and taught me it means a back or tight end hits a rusher and then releases. So my first
   thought is "the back chips, then goes out". Is that right? The chart tells me nobody knows. Either
   say "probably the back's chip (04-02), not confirmed", or explain why the course won't guess. As it
   stands it reads like the chapter forgot 04-02.
2. **"mesh point"** in the spread-option tells (L1053, p14): "a mesh point with the back on most
   runs". Two bullets earlier it says "mesh and Y-cross on third down" (L1052), meaning the pass
   concept. 03-04 warned me these are different, but this chapter puts them back to back with no
   reminder. Write "the give/keep mesh with the back" or add "(the handoff mesh from 03-04, not the
   pass concept)".
3. **"S"** means two different things in one chapter's diagrams. It is the **Sam linebacker** in
   Predict 1 (L1169, p16), next to "SS". It is a **slot receiver** in the run-and-shoot figure and
   in Predict 2 (L621, L1207, p8, p17). In Predict 2 the offensive "S" sits right under the defense's
   "$". I had to check the colours to know which side it was on.
4. **USFL** (L602, p7): "offensive coordinator of the USFL's Houston Gamblers". This is never
   explained. One gloss would do: "a spring pro league, 1983–85".
5. **"2-3 Jet"** (L581, p7): the Film room spells out the call but leaves "2-3" hanging. "Jet" is
   also the name of a motion I learned in 02-06, and Brown's WCO call at L365 has "Fire 2 **Jet**",
   apparently a protection. I now have three possible meanings for "Jet" and no guidance.
6. **"Routes are taught as rules that adjust to the coverage, and the long call lets a coach specify
   which version of a rule applies"** (L370–371, p4). This is abstract and has no example, so I
   can't picture what it means.
7. **"the 8-yard out" in Ghost vs flood's sail** (L233–238, p2). The chapter says this is "the
   family Dropback Concepts calls flood". But 04-04 drew flood with a **sail** that settles at about
   13 yards, and it lists "sail" as an alias. Is Ghost's 8-yard out the same route as a sail, or is
   Ghost a shorter version of flood? One clause would settle it.
8. **"H Out" called "a tag"** in the Coryell row of Fig 1 (L276, p3). Later "tag" means "a word that
   changes one thing in a base play" (L872). Naming a non-digit receiver's route is not that. These
   are two meanings of one owned term, 30 lines apart in the source.
9. The **"protection or run piece"** in the 49ers' call order (L542, p6) is never pointed out in the
   actual call. Fig 3 colours "with Roll Right" as "QB launch point / protection", so I assume "Ace" or
   "H2" is the protection, but the text doesn't say. The order is described with a slot I can't find
   in the example.

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

1. **Why is the Coryell digit order X, Y, Z, and why only three digits?** (L321–324, p4) A modern
   reader's question is "what about the slot?" The chapter says slots and backs "get their routes by
   name". It never says *why*: the system was built for 1970s two-back, three-receiver formations.
   That one sentence would also answer the next leap.
2. **Why are pure Coryell calls "rare in the NFL" today?** (L347) This is stated with no reason. Is it
   because three-wide sets broke the three-digit grammar? Because of the reads problem? Because the
   coaches retired?
3. **"under center more than you'd guess"** (L1023, p13). Why would I guess anything about the
   Patriots' under-center rate? The text assumes I link Erhardt-Perkins with the shotgun or
   no-huddle, but it never taught that link. (Also, 44% is 8th in the league, so this is a real fact
   that deserves a real why: McDaniels' run game? Maye?)
4. **"why NFL teams that sign many veterans like them"** (L784, p10). Why would veterans favour
   concept languages? Because concept words carry over between teams? Because there's less to
   re-learn? Spell it out.
5. **The language belongs to the head coach** (L804–812). What if the head coach isn't an offensive
   coach (e.g. the 2025 Patriots' Vrabel)? Whose language does the team speak then, and what happens
   when that coordinator leaves? The chapter's own churn examples raise this and don't answer it.
6. **Air Raid signals can be seen by everyone** (L699–711). If the call is two fingers from the
   sideline, why can't the defense read it? Dummy signallers? Signals changed weekly? A beginner asks
   this immediately, and the run-and-shoot / Air Raid "buys" never mention the cost.
7. **The run-and-shoot figure says "Both panels show the same call"** (L617). *Which* call? Go,
   Choice, Streak, Dog? Naming it would tie the figure to the text's list. It also says nothing about
   how the QB knows which branch the receivers took. Does he read the same safeties?
8. **The tempo column of the table** (L775–780, p10): Air Coryell "quick", Erhardt-Perkins "fast",
   Air Raid "fastest". The text argues only the WCO-slow and Air Raid-fast cells. Why is a digit call
   "quick" but not "fast"?
9. **Predict 1 answer, last sentence** (L1196): "The point of the long call in the huddle is that
   you, and the defense, can't tell which of the two it is". The *formation and the line's first
   steps* hide the play. The call doesn't. As written it sounds as if the call's length is the
   disguise.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, and captions that don't say what to notice

1. **Predict 1 answer vs. its own diagram (the biggest problem).** The diagram (p16) shows Z starting
   on the **right** and moving **leftward**, across the formation. The fullback is offset to the
   **left**. The answer (L1190) says "The jet motion stretches the defense **toward the right**… and
   outside zone attacks the edge in that direction with the fullback leading". I could not reconcile
   this. If the run goes toward the motion, it goes left, where the fullback is. Fix the sentence (or
   the drawing) so the direction of the motion, the fullback's offset and the run's direction agree.
2. **Fig 3 (call-length chips, p6):** the "Y Bingo" and "X Comeback" chips touch or overlap. The
   border cuts the "X" of "X Comeback" (seen at 200 dpi). Add spacing.
3. **Fig 2, Bunch panel (p5):** at print size the three dots (Y, H, Z) and their labels nearly touch.
   Z's go route is drawn straight up through H's dot, and #3's flat arrow appears to start from Z. The
   caption promises "a tight bunch, where the routes cross". I can't see who crosses whom. Widen the
   panel's spacing, or offset Z, or add a one-line note ("Y, the innermost, runs under H's out").
4. **Fig 4, run-and-shoot (p8), right panel:** the grey "branch not taken" hooks are short stubs right
   beside the FS and SS boxes. They read as stray arrows, not as "the curl he would have run". The left
   panel's grey branches are clear. In the right panel they need more length or a label.
5. **Fig 5, install map (p11):** each Base box has an arrow to the Tags box *in the same row*
   (Formations→Route tags, Protections→Formation tags, Runs→Motion tags…). That suggests a pairing
   that doesn't exist. Use one bracket arrow from the whole Base column to the whole Tags column.
6. **Fig 6, Boot Ghost (p12):** the caption says "the defenders near the line take a step toward the
   fake". No defenders are drawn, so the picture can't show the thing the caption tells me to notice.
   Add the two or three underneath defenders with a step-left arrow, or cut the clause.
7. **Fig 7, fingerprint chart (p13)** is good and readable. But see §4.1–4.2 for two prose claims it
   contradicts. Also, **"condensed" isn't a column**, though the Takeaways (L1291), checklist line 9
   and the Watch-for-it tally all make condensed splits a key fingerprint. If FTN doesn't chart it,
   say so in the caption.
8. **Fig 1** is the best figure in the chapter. One nit: the caption is long, and its *what to notice*
   ("nothing about the play changes from row to row") is in the prose after it, not in the caption.

## 4. Contradictions with the chapter's own data, and inconsistencies with earlier chapters

1. **"the 49ers and Rams skipped the huddle on under 6% of neutral-situation plays in 2025"** (L566).
   The chart (p13) prints the Rams at **6%, ranked 10th**, *above* the median team's **5%**. So "in the
   huddle" is a 49ers fingerprint. The Rams are slightly *more* no-huddle than average. The bullet
   "The Shanahan tree (Rams, 49ers): under center, in motion, in the huddle, no RPOs" (L1016) repeats
   the claim. Either reword ("near the league norm") or explain McVay's quick-huddle/no-huddle mix
   (which L570–575 actually describes!).
2. **Predict 2 answer recommends mesh against two deep safeties** (L1227–1230). 04-04's concept
   table (which I just read) lists mesh as "Man-beater… Built to beat: Man (Cover 1, Cover 0)". So I
   ruled mesh *out* when I saw two-high. The answer needs one sentence reconciling the two: mesh's
   crossers "sit in the zone holes" (04-04 L709). Otherwise it reads as a contradiction.
3. **Washington film room** says they were "more than ten times the rate of the median NFL team"
   (L727): 63% vs a 5% median. That checks out (12.6×). Fine.

## 5. Drag and repetition

The chapter runs ~8,400 words of prose against a 4,500 target. Biggest cuts available:

1. **01-04 already did a preview of the three families** (01-04 L563–583): West Coast with this same
   Shanahan call, Erhardt-Perkins with this same "Ghost" definition, Coryell with this same "896", the
   hybrid "Stick… X Dig" observation, and "time against precision". 04-08 re-introduces all of it as
   new. Only "The hybrid in every playbook" (L460–468) admits the link, and that section repeats 01-04
   almost word for word. Open with "You met three families in 01-04; here's the full comparison". Then
   cut the hybrid section to two sentences.
2. **The Air Raid origin repeats 04-04** (04-04 L1160–1175 and its Texas Tech film room). Both
   chapters tell Mumme, Leach, Iowa Wesleyan 1989, Valdosta State, Kentucky 1997, "subtraction" and
   "rep it ten thousand times". 04-08 L691–707 never says "you met them in Dropback Concepts". Cut to
   the *language* point plus the Leach quote.
3. **Tags are re-defined** (L872–875) after 04-04's "Tags and route conversions" section, also with no
   back-reference.
4. **Family tells appear four times:** "Read the chart" bullets (L1016–1029), "Further tells by
   family" (L1041–1054), checklist line 9 (L1078), and the Watch-for-it bullets (L1146–1153). Merge the
   first two. The other two can reference the merged list.
5. **Buys/costs three times:** each family's "What it buys / What it costs", then the table, then the
   Installation/Tempo/In-game paragraphs restating them. The three closing paragraphs could shrink to
   the *new* points only (concept languages and college roster turnover; who adjusts and when).
6. Small: "Mouse Davis, a **famously** small coach who made it **famous**" (L600–601). The
   Mariota/injury aside in the Washington film room (L732–734) is detail I didn't need.

   and L779), so hearing "Trips Right Mesh" I can't tell them apart. The **run-and-shoot** call is a
   single word like "Go", which I can't tell from either. The **spread option** gets *no example call at
   all* (L742–765). Every other family gets one. And "Shanahan/McVay sentence" vs "West Coast" is the
   same family by the chapter's own table. Either narrow the objective or give each family a sample
   call plus a "how you'd tell" line.
2. **Explain buys/costs for installation, tempo, adjustment:** yes. The table makes this easy.
3. **Describe install layers:** yes. Fig 5 plus the text.
4. **Name fingerprints and guess a family:** yes for Shanahan tree, Air Raid lineage and spread option.
   **No** for Erhardt-Perkins, Reid and Coryell. The chapter itself says the E-P fingerprint "these
   averages can't show", and the Watch-for-it lumps E-P and Reid together as "a bit of everything".
   There is no Coryell example team for 2025.
5. **Run the checklist after Part 4:** yes. The card is clear.
