# 06-03 Cover 3: Sky, Buzz, Cloud, and Seattle's Match Version — beginner reader review

Reviewer persona: a casual NFL watcher who has never played and has read everything before this chapter in curriculum order (Parts 1–5, 06-01 Coverage Fundamentals, 06-02 Man Coverage). I read it start to finish in the PDF (`pdfs/06-03-cover-3-family.pdf`, 22 pp, rasterized at 60 and 110 dpi) with the source open next to it. I attempted the three Predict-the-play drills before opening the answers.

**Overall:** this is the best "one coverage, taken apart" chapter so far. Fig 1, the curl-flat vs seam-flat idea, the two four-verticals strips and the Bradley chart all work, and the "sky = safety, cloud = corner" memory aid sticks. The real problems are: (a) **the "eighth man" story clashes with the chapter's own chart**, which shows 6.3 box defenders for Cover 3; (b) **the "count three deep" rule fails on the chapter's own Cover 3 match strip**, where five defenders are deep at +2.1 s; (c) **Seattle's corners are "press-bail" here but "press-and-carry, not a bail" in 06-01**; (d) Predict 2's answer contradicts its own figure about the hash; and (e) the Seattle material is the third telling of the same 2013 story after 06-01's film room.

## 2. Leaps (a missing or assumed "why")

1. **Where's the eighth man in the data?** The section is titled "the eighth man" and Fig 3 counts 8. Then Fig 4 shows Cover 3's average box at **6.3**, the same as Cover 1 (the text says "almost as many as Cover 1", but the bars are equal). A beginner concludes the defense never has eight in the box. The chart is *dropbacks* (passing downs, mostly against spread sets) and box counts are measured differently, but the text doesn't say so. Add one sentence: "On dropbacks against three-receiver sets the box is lighter. The eighth man shows up against run formations, and what matters is the *extra* defender compared with two-high."
2. **Hole (2) sits inside the SS's own zone.** In Fig 1 the right curl-flat box covers 2 to 13.5 yards deep, out to the sideline. In Fig 7 the "(2) hitch under the corner" hole is drawn inside that box, about 3 yards from where the SS stands. The text says the curl-flat defender is "still several yards inside", but the picture shows him close. Explain why he can't get there: he's sinking toward the curl at 10–12 yards and facing the QB, so a hitch thrown on time at 6 yards is caught before he can turn and drive. Or draw the hole after a 3-step drop, when he's still moving.
3. **Which holes still apply to match?** The chapter says "Most NFL Cover 3 today is closer to the seam-flat version". The holes section then analyses spot-drop holes only. For each hole, I want to know whether it survives against match. The callout's "What it gives up" covers a few, but the holes list should say so (for example "against match, the seam closes, the flat under the carry opens").
4. **The recognition rule vs the match strip.** "One second after the snap … count. Three … means a Cover 3 family call." In Fig 9's +2.1 s panel, five defenders are 10+ yards deep (2 CB, $, SS, FS), because the seam-flat players carried. A beginner watching match Cover 3 against four verticals counts five and says "quarters?". Say explicitly that the count must be taken at about +1 s, before carries, and that carried seam-flat defenders look like extra deep players later in the play.
5. **Who are the four under in a base 4-3?** The chapter draws nickel personnel everywhere except Fig 3 and Predict 1, which use a base 4-3 (Sam, Mike, Will, two safeties). In Predict 1, if the SS skies to the right curl-flat, what do the Sam, Mike and Will do, and who has the left curl-flat? Neither the figure nor Answer 1 says. One line in Answer 1 would do it ("Sam to the right hook-curl, Mike left hook, Will left curl-flat").
6. **Buzz "everyone slides one zone":** the slide is hard to see, because the buzz figure starts from a *different alignment* than the sky figure (the $ moves from the left slot to the right slot, and the Will is now apexed over the left slot). Without a sky-vs-buzz comparison from the same pre-snap picture, "slides one zone" doesn't land. Either keep the alignment from Fig 1 or add one line explaining why the $ changed sides.
7. **Cloud "to the boundary … where the deep third is narrow enough":** every diagram in the chapter has the ball in the middle of the field. How do the thirds change when the ball is on a hash? The chapter assumes this and never shows it. A sentence would do (NFL hashes are only 3 yards off centre, so the shift is small but real).
8. **Trips check:** "the backside corner plays the lone receiver … man to man, and the rest of the defense plays three-deep match toward the trips." If the backside corner is in man, *who are the three deep?* (The FS, the trips-side corner, and …?) Without a diagram or one more sentence I can't picture it. The "In spot-drop it's the free safety, which leaves the trips side's seam to the corner and the curl-flat defender" step also skips the "because" (the FS can't be over #2's seam and on #3 at once).
9. **Data paragraph:** "and turn to man coverage when the field is short" is asserted, but the numbers given only show Cover 3 *falling*. Either add the Cover 1/Cover 0 share for those situations or soften the claim.
10. **Fig 4, the completion rate:** why does Cover 1 (man) allow *fewer* completions (60%) but *more* 20+ yard plays than Cover 3? A beginner notices this and gets no explanation. Half a sentence would do ("man contests every catch but has no one behind a beaten defender").
11. **Bradley chart, Colts panel:** the predecessor's 38% vs Bradley's 41% barely moves. The text quietly skips Indianapolis. A demanding reader asks why "the coach is the scheme" didn't show up there. Acknowledge it (roster? era? the league shift?) or say the Colts were already a Cover 3 team.

## 3. Diagrams I couldn't decode, and captions that don't say what to notice

- **Fig 2, left panel (curl-flat, spot-drop):** the nickel is drawn at his **start** (5 yd) with an arrow to his landmark, while the CB in the same panel is drawn at his **end** with a ghost at the start. That reverses the convention I learned in Fig 1 one page earlier. The thin "breaks to the flat on the throw" arrow starts near the landmark, crosses X's curl stem, and ends about 4 yards deep outside X, but no receiver runs to the flat and no throw is drawn, so there is nothing to break *on*. X's curl is a tiny hook at 12 yards that I only found on the second look. Draw the $ at the landmark with a ghost, and add a faint flat route.
- **Fig 2, right panel:** three H branches and three defender arrows all start inside the ringed $ marker. The "#2 in" branch (toward W) is almost hidden under the ring. Separate the start points slightly or shorten the ring.
- **Fig 5 (buzz):** the ringed player, the SS, barely moves (from ~11 yd to 9.5 yd, a few yards across). The long, eye-catching arrows belong to the W, the $ and the M. The "buzz" label sits under his path inside the hook-curl box. My first read was that the Will rotated. The caption should say what to notice: "the SS moves only a few yards. Buzz is visible in *who else* moves: the $ widens to the flat, the W runs to the far curl-flat, the M slides over."
- **Fig 7 (holes):** no receivers or routes are drawn. "go takes the CB deep" points at a CB with no go route near him. The (2) and (4) markers on the right overlap the same area, with "(4) flood" floating between them. Drawing the flood routes faintly on the right (as Predict 3 later does) would make (4) readable.
- **Fig 8 (four verts vs spot-drop):** (a) the frames have **no depth ruler**, so "Caught 25 yd deep" can't be checked. (b) At the catch, the FS is only about 4–5 yards from H and closing, so the seam looks contested rather than "open". (c) The FS's "drift right with the QB's eyes" in panel 3 is about 3 yards, which is hard to see, and nothing shows where the QB is looking. The strip's story ("the eyes moved the safety, the ball went the other way") needs a bigger drift or an arrow for the QB's eyes.
- **Fig 9 (four verts vs match), panel 4:** the caption says the check-down goes to "the back (R) **in the flat**". R is caught about 3 yards past the line, **over the right guard/tackle**, which isn't the flat by the chapter's own zone map. Either route R wider or say "underneath".
- **Fig 3:** the "deep ⅓" labels float beside CBs drawn at 7.5 yards, under the orange line. At a glance, the deep third seems to start at 10 yards. Add a small arrow ("drops to") or move the labels up.
- **Fig 4:** the dashed divider between the one-high and two-high bars is only explained at the end of the caption. Put "one-high / two-high" labels on the y-axis.

## 4. Drag and repetition

- **Seattle is told three times across two chapters.** 06-01's film room already covers Thomas, Sherman, Browner, Maxwell, Chancellor, step-kick, NFL.com's "press man, Cover Three", "led the league in points, yards and takeaways" and 43–8 over Denver. This chapter repeats all of it in the "Seattle's version" section and then again in the "Film room: Seattle 2013 — Super Bowl XLVIII" callout. Keep what's new here (the five pieces as *scheme* reasons, the match-on-verticals point) and cut the duplicate film room, or make it about a single play.
- **The tree** is a run of coaching CV dates (Bradley ×5 stops, Quinn ×3, Saleh ×4) that a beginner skims. The Bradley chart makes the point better. Trim the prose to the chart's three teams plus a one-line "and Quinn, and Saleh".
- **"Recognising Cover 3" and "Watch for it on Sunday"** say almost the same thing twice, back to back. Keep the checklist and turn the prose section into the *why* behind each check, or merge them.
- **The seam weakness** is explained in the misconception callout, the holes list (1), and the four-verticals intro, each with the "about 9 yards either side of the middle" line. Cut one of the three.
- **"Why it exists: three rotations"** restates the three "Why sky/buzz/cloud" paragraphs just above it almost word for word. Make the callout the comparison table the paragraphs lack (rotating player, lands where, best against, tell, beater).
- **Print layout:** pages 10 and 12 are about 60% blank, because figures float past their section heads. Page 22 holds six lines. The four-verticals section heading is stranded at the bottom of p. 10, with its figure on p. 11.

## 5. Can I do the "You'll be able to…" items?

- **Draw Cover 3 from memory:** yes. Fig 1 plus "The deep three / The underneath four" is excellent.
- **Tell sky, buzz and cloud apart one second after the snap, and say why:** sky and cloud, yes. Buzz, only from the text: the figure hides it (see §3).
- **Explain the bargain:** yes for the holes. The "eighth man" half is undercut by Fig 4's 6.3 (see §2.1).
- **Explain how match handles four verticals, and what offenses attack instead:** the first half, yes (Figs 8 and 9 together are the chapter's best moment). "What offenses attack instead" lives in one callout paragraph (post-wheel, switch, motion) with no picture, so I can name them but can't explain them.
- **Recognise before and after the snap:** mostly. But the count-to-three rule trips on match Cover 3 (see §2.4).

**Predict the play (attempted before opening answers):**

1. *First-and-10, 12 personnel.* My answer: one high safety plus corners off with outside leverage means Cover 3 (or Cover 1); the SS outside the TE will sky down to the curl-flat and be the force player; throw play-action to a seam, or hitches. **Matched the answer.** I wasn't sure what the Sam does (see §2.5), and I first confused S with SS.
2. *Two-high, mismatched corners.* My answer: cloud to the right (squat corner with a safety over him, other corner off), or maybe Cover 6; watch whether the FS runs to the middle. **Matched.** But the answer's supporting tell is wrong against its own figure. It says the FS is "only about 5 yards from the middle … narrower than a Cover 2 safety (who would be outside the hash)", yet the chapter's code puts the hash at 3.1 yards, so an FS 5 yards off centre is *already outside the hash*. (06-01's beginner review flagged the same hash rule of thumb.) Rephrase as "between the hash and the numbers, narrower than a Cover 2 safety near the numbers", or move him inside the hash. Also, Cover 6's *pre-snap look* was never taught (06-05 owns it), so "whose left side would also have an off corner" is something I take on faith.
3. *Flood, 2.2 s after the snap.* My answer: the read is the SS; he's sunk under Y's sail, so throw to R in the flat. **Matched.** This is the best drill: the frame really does make you reason. The match addendum in the answer is useful.

## 6. Knowledge gaps (what I still wonder)

- **Cover 9 / weak rotation.** 06-01 defined Cover 9 as "a Cover 3 in which the safety away from the strength comes down" on some staffs, or Cover 3 match on others. This chapter, which owns Cover 3 match and the rotations, never mentions weak rotation or connects to Cover 9 and the BDB "Cover-3 Seam" label. One paragraph would tie the dialect lesson back.
- **How does the defense pick the rotation side** against a balanced 2x2 with no tight end? Field, boundary, the back's side, the call? The Madden callout lists rules ("rotate to the strength", "cloud the boundary") but doesn't say what "strength" means in a balanced set.
- **What do the hook defenders do against the run** in each rotation? We learn the safety's run job (force/alley) but not how the Mike and Will fit behind it, even briefly (with a pointer to 05-05).
- **Cover 3 vs a running QB / scrambles:** "every underneath defender faces the QB" is sold as a strength. Does that make Cover 3 better against scrambles than man? One line would do.
- **How long can a corner stay "on top"?** The holes section says the corner concedes the hitch. How deep must a receiver get before the corner stops bailing and drives on a hitch, and how does the corner know it's a hitch and not a hitch-and-go (the double move from 06-02)?
- **How often is the pre-snap Cover 3 look honest?** Since the chapter ends on "now it's usually disguised", I'd like one number: how often a one-high shell ends up as a three-deep coverage after the snap (if the data supports it), or a pointer to 06-06.
- **Why did Cover 3 fall from 38% on first down (2018–22) to 27.7% overall in 2025?** These are different measures under different charters. The chapter puts them near each other and invites the comparison. Say explicitly that they can't be compared.
